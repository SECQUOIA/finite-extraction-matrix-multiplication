\section{Tensor maps for splits and triplings}
\label{sec:maps}

This section constructs the tensor maps behind the profile inequalities
\((h+1)P(r,b)\ge\sum_{j=0}^hP(r,b+h-2j)\), a consequence of the concavity
axiom~\eqref{eq:concavity} (Remark~\ref{rem:multiway-scalar}), and
\(P(r,4r-1)\ge3P(r,r)\), the tripling axiom~\eqref{eq:tripling} at \(b=r\). A \emph{split} of width \(h\) converts the
convolution tensor \(C(r,b)\), together with a dot product of dimension
\(h+1\), into the convolution tensors \(C(r,b+h-2j)\), \(0\le j\le h\). A
\emph{tripling} converts \(C(r,4r-1)\) into three copies of \(C(r,r)\), one
of them with its second and third legs exchanged.
Corollary~\ref{cor:oriented-relations} states both as relations among the
symmetrized convolution tensors \(S^\sigma_{r,b}\) that also produce an
auxiliary matrix multiplication tensor; Section~\ref{sec:extraction}
applies it to every relation of the construction.

Each relation is built in three steps. First, a restriction of a tensor
power (Sections~\ref{subsec:splits} and~\ref{subsec:tripling}) produces a
sum \(T=\sum_{g=0}^{M-1}T_g\) of \(M\) \emph{sectors sharing the first
leg}: \(T_g\in X\otimes Y_g\otimes Z_g\) with \(Y=\bigoplus_gY_g\) and
\(Z=\bigoplus_gZ_g\). This is not a direct sum, because all sectors use the
same first-leg variables, so a coordinate change that depends on the sector
can be applied on the second and third legs but not yet on the first.
Second, the separator (Section~\ref{subsec:separator}) gives each sector
its own first leg. Third, the relation is applied in three orientations
(Section~\ref{subsec:orientations}). The split, the tripling and the
separator each adapt a construction of \citet{OAI26}; we state the
correspondence after each result.

\subsection{Splits from the determinant filtration}
\label{subsec:splits}

We obtain a split from a filtration, preserved by multiplication, of the
input and output spaces of polynomial multiplication. Its successive
quotients carry the tensors \(C(r,b+h-2j)\), so in adapted bases
\(C(r,b)B_X(h+1)\) is block triangular with these blocks on the
diagonal. For \(r\ge2\) the
off-diagonal blocks are not zero (Example~\ref{ex:determinant-basis}), but
in a tensor power they disappear when we keep only the coordinates with a
prescribed number of factors from each quotient (Lemma~\ref{lem:multiway}).

\paragraph{Binary forms.}
For \(n\ge1\) let \(V_n=\Q[u,v]_{n-1}\) be the space of binary forms with
\(n\) coefficients (indexed by dimension, not degree), with monomial basis
\(u^{n-1-i}v^i\), \(0\le i<n\). The convolution
tensor~\eqref{eq:convolution} is then the tensor of multiplication
\(V_r\times V_b\to V_{r+b-1}\): the monomials with indices \(\alpha\) and
\(\beta\) multiply to the monomial with index \(\alpha+\beta\), and
\(z_\gamma\) reads the coefficient of the \(\gamma\)-th monomial of
\(V_{r+b-1}\). Let \(V'_n=\Q[u',v']_{n-1}\) be a copy of \(V_n\)
in new variables. Multiplication by \(f\in V_r\) on the first factor maps
\(V_b\otimes V'_{h+1}\) to \(V_{r+b-1}\otimes V'_{h+1}\), and in monomial
bases its tensor is \(C(r,b)B_X(h+1)\): the factor \(V'_{h+1}\) passes
through unchanged, which is the dot product \(B_X(h+1)\) with its
one-dimensional first leg.

\paragraph{The filtration.}
Put \(\Delta=uv'-vu'\). For integers \(h\ge0\) and \(c\ge h+1\), filter
\(V_c\otimes V'_{h+1}\) by
\[
 \mathcal F^j(V_c\otimes V'_{h+1})=\Delta^j\,(V_{c-j}\otimes V'_{h+1-j})
 \quad(0\le j\le h),\qquad
 \mathcal F^{h+1}(V_c\otimes V'_{h+1})=0,
\]
where \(\Delta^j\) multiplies in \(\Q[u,v,u',v']\) and \(V'_1=\Q\). The
letter \(c\) stands for a number of coefficients; we use \(c=b\) for the
second leg and \(c=r+b-1\) for the output.
Lemma~\ref{lem:determinant-basis} below shows that dividing by
\(\Delta^j\) and substituting \((u',v')=(u,v)\) gives an isomorphism
\begin{equation}\label{eq:filtration-quotient}
 \mathcal F^j/\mathcal F^{j+1}\cong V_{c+h-2j}.
\end{equation}
This is the universal Clebsch--Gordan filtration of
\citet[Lemma~2.5, Theorem~2.8, Corollary~2.9 and Definition~2.10]{Maakestad23}
for the symmetric powers of degrees \(h\) and \(c-1\) of a rank-two
module; we construct the integer bases we need directly.

For \(0\le i<c+h\), the \emph{lift} of the \(i\)-th monomial of
\(V_{c+h}\) is
\[
 L_{c,h}(i)=
 \begin{cases}
 u^{c-1-i}v^iu'^h,&0\le i\le c-1,\\
 v^{c-1}u'^{c+h-1-i}v'^{i-c+1},&c-1<i<c+h.
 \end{cases}
\]
It lies in \(V_c\otimes V'_{h+1}\), its total exponent of \(v\) and \(v'\)
is \(i\), and the substitution \((u',v')=(u,v)\) maps it to
\(u^{c+h-1-i}v^i\). The \emph{determinant basis} of
\(V_c\otimes V'_{h+1}\) consists of the vectors
\begin{equation}\label{eq:determinant-basis}
 \mathbf b_{j,i}=\Delta^jL_{c-j,h-j}(i),\qquad
 0\le j\le h,\quad 0\le i<c+h-2j.
\end{equation}
We call \(j\) the \emph{filtration index} of \(\mathbf b_{j,i}\), and we
write \(\mathbf b^{(c)}_{j,i}\) when the space must be indicated.

\begin{lemma}[Determinant basis]\label{lem:determinant-basis}
Let \(h\ge0\) and \(c\ge h+1\).
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] The vectors \eqref{eq:determinant-basis} form a basis of
\(V_c\otimes V'_{h+1}\) in which every monomial has integer coordinates.
Those with filtration index at least \(j\) form a basis of
\(\mathcal F^j(V_c\otimes V'_{h+1})\).
\item[(b)] Dividing by \(\Delta^j\) and substituting \((u',v')=(u,v)\)
gives the isomorphism \eqref{eq:filtration-quotient}, which maps the
class of \(\mathbf b_{j,i}\) to the \(i\)-th monomial
\(u^{c+h-2j-1-i}v^i\) of \(V_{c+h-2j}\).
\item[(c)] For \(r\ge1\) and \(f\in V_r\), multiplication by \(f\) maps
\(\mathcal F^j(V_c\otimes V'_{h+1})\) into
\(\mathcal F^j(V_{r+c-1}\otimes V'_{h+1})\). On the \(j\)-th quotients
it induces multiplication by \(f\) from \(V_{c+h-2j}\) to
\(V_{r+c+h-2j-1}\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1: part (a).} We rewrite monomials with the relation
\(uv'=vu'+\Delta\) until they become lifts. Let \(\psi\) be a monomial in
\(V_c\otimes V'_{h+1}\) with total exponent \(i\) of \(v\) and \(v'\). If
\(uv'\) divides \(\psi\), write \(\psi=\psi_1uv'=\psi_1vu'+\Delta\psi_1\)
with \(\psi_1\in V_{c-1}\otimes V'_h\); the monomial \(\psi_1vu'\) has the
same total exponent \(i\) and smaller exponents of \(u\) and \(v'\).
Repeating this, we reach a monomial not divisible by \(uv'\), namely
\(u^{c-1-i}v^iu'^h\) or \(v^{c-1}u'^{c+h-1-i}v'^{i-c+1}\), which is the
lift \(L_{c,h}(i)\). Hence \(\psi=L_{c,h}(i)+\Delta\rho\), where \(\rho\) is
an integer combination of monomials of \(V_{c-1}\otimes V'_h\); when
\(h=0\), every monomial is already a lift and \(\rho=0\). Since
\(c-1\ge h\), the monomials of \(\rho\) can be treated in the same way, and
after at most \(h\) rounds every monomial of \(V_c\otimes V'_{h+1}\) is an
integer combination of the vectors \(\mathbf b_{j,i}\). Their number is
\(\sum_{j=0}^h(c+h-2j)=c(h+1)=\dim(V_c\otimes V'_{h+1})\), so they form a
basis in which every monomial has integer coordinates. Applying this to
\(V_{c-j}\otimes V'_{h+1-j}\) and multiplying by \(\Delta^j\), which is
injective, shows that the \(\mathbf b_{j',i}\) with \(j'\ge j\) form a
basis of \(\mathcal F^j\).

\emph{Step 2: part (b).} The map \(\mathcal F^j\to V_{c+h-2j}\) that sends
\(\Delta^j\rho\) to \(\rho(u,v,u,v)\) is well defined, because
\(\Delta^j\rho\) determines \(\rho\). It sends \(\mathbf b_{j,i}\) to
the \(i\)-th monomial, and \(\mathbf b_{j',i}\) with \(j'>j\) to zero,
because \(\Delta\) vanishes when \((u',v')=(u,v)\). By~(a), it is
surjective with kernel \(\mathcal F^{j+1}\).

\emph{Step 3: part (c).} For \(\rho\in V_{c-j}\otimes V'_{h+1-j}\) we have
\(f\Delta^j\rho=\Delta^j(f\rho)\) with
\(f\rho\in V_{r+c-j-1}\otimes V'_{h+1-j}\), so multiplication by \(f\)
preserves the filtration. Since
\((f\rho)(u,v,u,v)=f\cdot\rho(u,v,u,v)\), the induced map on quotients is
multiplication by \(f\).
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The tensor in the determinant bases.}
Let \(\Pi_c\) be the matrix whose columns are the vectors
\(\mathbf b^{(c)}_{j,i}\) in monomial coordinates; by
Lemma~\ref{lem:determinant-basis}(a), \(\Pi_c\) and \(\Pi_c^{-1}\) have
integer entries. We keep the monomial basis \(u^{r-1-\alpha}v^\alpha\) of
the first leg \(V_r\), with variables \(x_\alpha\), and change the bases of
the other two legs. On the second leg, the input space
\(V_b\otimes V'_{h+1}\), using the basis \(\mathbf b^{(b)}_{j,i}\) means
substituting \(y=\Pi_by'\) for the vector \(y\) of second-leg variables.
The third leg is the dual of the output space \(V_{r+b-1}\otimes V'_{h+1}\),
since its variables read off the coordinates of an output, so changing the
output basis to \(\mathbf b^{(r+b-1)}_{j,i}\) changes the third-leg
variables contragrediently, by \(z=\Pi_{r+b-1}^{-\top}z'\). After these
substitutions, the coefficient of \(x_\alpha y'_{(j,i)}z'_{(j',i')}\) is
the coordinate along \(\mathbf b^{(r+b-1)}_{j',i'}\) of the product
\(u^{r-1-\alpha}v^\alpha\,\mathbf b^{(b)}_{j,i}\). By
Lemma~\ref{lem:determinant-basis}, this coefficient is zero when
\(j'<j\), and for \(j'=j\) it is one if \(i'=i+\alpha\) and zero otherwise.
Thus multiplication never decreases the filtration index, and the
diagonal blocks of the tensor are exactly \(C(r,b+h)\), \(C(r,b+h-2)\),
\ldots, \(C(r,b-h)\).

\begin{example}\label{ex:determinant-basis}
Take \(r=2\), \(b=3\) and \(h=1\), so that \(C(2,3)B_X(2)\) is
multiplication by \(f\in V_2\) from \(V_3\otimes V'_2\) to
\(V_4\otimes V'_2\). The determinant bases of these two spaces are as
follows, with \(i=0,1,2,\ldots\) from left to right in each row:
\[
\begin{array}{c|l|l}
 &\mathbf b^{(3)}_{j,i}\in V_3\otimes V'_2
 &\mathbf b^{(4)}_{j,i}\in V_4\otimes V'_2\\ \hline
 j=0&u^2u',\ uvu',\ v^2u',\ v^2v'
    &u^3u',\ u^2vu',\ uv^2u',\ v^3u',\ v^3v'\\
 j=1&u\Delta,\ v\Delta&u^2\Delta,\ uv\Delta,\ v^2\Delta
\end{array}.
\]
The diagonal blocks are \(C(2,4)\) for filtration index \(0\) and
\(C(2,2)\) for index \(1\). Most products of a basis vector with \(u\)
or \(v\) are again basis vectors; for instance
\(v\,\mathbf b^{(3)}_{0,3}=v^3v'=\mathbf b^{(4)}_{0,4}\) and
\(u\,\mathbf b^{(3)}_{1,0}=u^2\Delta=\mathbf b^{(4)}_{1,0}\). The only
exception is
\(u\cdot v^2v'=v^3u'+v^2\Delta
=\mathbf b^{(4)}_{0,3}+\mathbf b^{(4)}_{1,2}\).
The first term is the diagonal entry \(x_0y'_{(0,3)}z'_{(0,3)}\), since
\(u\) is the monomial with \(\alpha=0\) and \(i'=i+\alpha=3\). The second
gives the single off-diagonal coefficient, at
\(x_0y'_{(0,3)}z'_{(1,2)}\). In a tensor power, a term that uses this
coefficient in some position has a larger total filtration index on the
third leg than on the second, so the type restriction of
Lemma~\ref{lem:multiway} removes it.
\end{example}

\paragraph{Words, types and sectors.}
In the \(n\)-th tensor power we use on the second leg the product basis
vectors \(\mathbf b_{j_1,i_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbf b_{j_n,i_n}\), and
on the third leg the coordinates dual to the corresponding product basis
of the output. The \emph{word} of such a basis vector, and of its
coordinate, is the sequence of filtration indices
\(\mathbf j=(j_1,\ldots,j_n)\in\{0,\ldots,h\}^n\). The \emph{type} of a
word is its count vector \((n_0,\ldots,n_h)\), where \(n_j\) is the number
of positions \(\theta\) with \(j_\theta=j\). A type restriction keeps, on the
second and third legs, only the coordinates whose words have a prescribed
type. If every surviving term has the same word on both legs, the
surviving terms with word \(\mathbf j\) form the \emph{sector} of
\(\mathbf j\), a tensor
\(T_{\mathbf j}\in X\otimes Y_{\mathbf j}\otimes Z_{\mathbf j}\), where
\(Y_{\mathbf j}\) and \(Z_{\mathbf j}\) are spanned by the coordinates
with word \(\mathbf j\) and \(X\) is the whole first leg; we use the word,
its coordinates and the tensor \(T_{\mathbf j}\) interchangeably. The
sectors thus share the first leg.

\begin{lemma}[Type restriction for a split]\label{lem:multiway}\label{lem:determinant}
Let \(r\ge1\), \(h\ge1\) and \(b\ge h+1\), and let \(n_0,\ldots,n_h\) be
nonnegative integers with positive sum \(n\). In the determinant bases on
the second and third legs, keeping only the coordinates whose words have
type \((n_0,\ldots,n_h)\) is a restriction of
\((C(r,b)B_X(h+1))^{\otimes n}\) to a tensor with
\(M=n!/\prod_{j=0}^hn_j!\) sectors sharing the first leg
\(X=V_r^{\otimes n}\), one for each word of this type. After its tensor
positions are reordered according to its word, each sector is
\(\prod_{j=0}^hC(r,b+h-2j)^{\otimes n_j}\). On the shared first leg, this
word-dependent reordering is made only after separation.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Apply the coordinate change above in every tensor position. In
product bases the coefficient of a term is the product of the
coefficients in the \(n\) positions, so a term with second-leg word
\(\mathbf j\) and third-leg word \(\mathbf j'\) can be nonzero only if
\(j'_\theta\ge j_\theta\) for every position \(\theta\). Setting to zero every
second- and third-leg coordinate whose word does not have type
\((n_0,\ldots,n_h)\) is a restriction. For a surviving term both words have
this type, so
\(\sum_\theta j'_\theta=\sum_jjn_j=\sum_\theta j_\theta\), and with the
positionwise inequalities this forces \(j'_\theta=j_\theta\) for every
\(\theta\). Hence the restricted tensor is the sum of the sectors
\(T_{\mathbf j}\) over the \(M\) words of the given type. In
\(T_{\mathbf j}\) position \(\theta\) uses the diagonal block of index
\(j_\theta\), so
\(T_{\mathbf j}=\bigotimes_{\theta=1}^nC(r,b+h-2j_\theta)\), with the factors
in the order of the positions. Reordering the positions so that their
indices \(j_\theta\) increase gives the stated product.
\end{proof}

The restriction in Lemma~\ref{lem:multiway} involves no degeneration
parameter, so it adds nothing to the interpolation degree. For \(h=1\),
the same filtration and the same two diagonal blocks \(C(r,b+1)\) and
\(C(r,b-1)\) appear in the proof of \citet[Lemma~4.1]{OAI26}, which
removes the off-diagonal block by a degeneration that scales coordinates
by powers of \(t\); here the type restriction removes it.

\begin{remark}[Wider splits]\label{rem:multiway-scalar}
A split of width \(h\) uses an auxiliary dot product of dimension
\(h+1\), whereas producing the same lengths \(b+h-2j\) by \(h\) successive
splits of width one would use dimension \(2^h\). Wider splits therefore
lower the finite cost; the auxiliary dimension \(d_{r,x}=h_{r,x}+1\) enters
through the occupation bounds of Section~\ref{sec:flows}. They give no new
scalar inequality: since \(b+h-2j\) and \(b-h+2j\) lie symmetrically about
\(b\), the concavity inequality~\eqref{eq:concavity} along the row gives
\(P(r,b+h-2j)+P(r,b-h+2j)\le2P(r,b)\) for every profile \(P\), and summing
over \(0\le j\le h\) gives \((h+1)P(r,b)\ge\sum_{j=0}^hP(r,b+h-2j)\).
\end{remark}

\subsection{Tripling with only the middle count fixed}
\label{subsec:tripling}

The coordinates of \(C(r,4r-1)\) fall into three blocks, two copies of
\(C(r,r)\) and one of \(C(r,r)^{(23)}\). As in Section~\ref{subsec:splits},
a restriction of a tensor power removes the terms that join the middle
block to an outer one; it suffices to fix the number of middle labels in a
word, which leaves at least \(3^n/(n+1)\) sectors in the \(n\)-th tensor
power.

Write the terms of \(C(r,4r-1)\) as \(x_\alpha y_\beta z_\gamma\) with
\(0\le\alpha<r\), \(0\le\beta<4r-1\) and \(\gamma=\alpha+\beta\). Label
each second-leg index \(\beta\) and each third-leg index \(\gamma\)
\emph{left}, \emph{middle} or \emph{right} according to the following
intervals, with endpoints included:
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
 &\text{left}&\text{middle}&\text{right}\\ \hline
 \beta &[0,r-1]&[r,3r-2]&[3r-1,4r-2]\\
 \gamma &[0,2r-2]&[2r-1,3r-2]&[3r-1,5r-3]
\end{array}.
\]
Figure~\ref{fig:tripling} shows the case \(r=2\). A left label on the
second leg forces a left label on the third, since \(\gamma\le2r-2\) when
\(\beta\le r-1\). A right label on the second leg forces a right label on
the third, since \(\gamma\ge\beta\). A middle label on the third leg
forces a middle label on the second, since \(2r-1\le\gamma\le3r-2\) and
\(0\le\alpha\le r-1\) give \(r\le\beta=\gamma-\alpha\le3r-2\). Hence
every term whose two labels differ has a middle label on the second leg
and an outer label on the third. In the \(n\)-th tensor power, the word of
a second- or third-leg coordinate is its sequence of \(n\) labels.

\begin{lemma}[Type restriction for a tripling]\label{lem:tripling}
Let \(r\ge2\), \(n\ge1\) and \(0\le m\le n\). Keeping on the second and
third legs of \(C(r,4r-1)^{\otimes n}\) only the coordinates whose words
have exactly \(m\) middle labels is a restriction to a tensor with
\(M_{\mathrm t}(n,m)=\binom nm2^{n-m}\) sectors sharing the first leg, one
for each such word. Each sector is a tensor product of \(n-m\) factors
isomorphic to \(C(r,r)\) and \(m\) factors isomorphic to
\(C(r,r)^{(23)}\). The choice \(m=\lfloor(n+1)/3\rfloor\) maximizes
\(M_{\mathrm t}(n,m)\) and gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tripling-mode}
 M_{\mathrm t}(n,m)\ge\frac{3^n}{n+1}.
\end{equation}
No divisibility condition on \(n\) is required.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
In each position of a surviving term, a middle label on the third leg
forces a middle label on the second leg. Hence the set of middle positions
on the third leg is contained in that on the second leg; both sets have
\(m\) elements, so they coincide. At the other \(n-m\) positions both
labels are outer, and the second-leg label determines the third-leg label.
Every surviving term therefore carries the same word on both legs, and the
sectors are indexed by the words with \(m\) middle labels: there are
\(\binom nm\) choices of the middle positions and \(2^{n-m}\) choices of
the outer labels.

A sector is the tensor product, over the \(n\) positions, of the blocks
selected by the labels. The left block is \(C(r,r)\) itself, and the right
block becomes \(C(r,r)\) after subtracting \(3r-1\) from \(\beta\) and
\(\gamma\). In the middle block put \(\alpha'=r-1-\alpha\) and
\(\gamma'=\gamma-(2r-1)\); its terms are then
\(x_{r-1-\alpha'}\,y_{r+\alpha'+\gamma'}\,z_{2r-1+\gamma'}\) with
\(0\le\alpha',\gamma'<r\). Reversing the first index and shifting the
second and third indices by \(r\) and \(2r-1\) turns them into
\(\sum_{\alpha',\gamma'}x_{\alpha'}y_{\alpha'+\gamma'}z_{\gamma'}\), which is
\(C(r,r)^{(23)}\). These identifications are coordinate permutations
within each leg. On the shared first leg, the reversal at the middle
positions depends on the word, so, like the reordering of positions, it is
applied only after separation.

Finally,
\(M_{\mathrm t}(n,m+1)/M_{\mathrm t}(n,m)=(n-m)/(2(m+1))\), which is at
least one exactly when \(m\le(n-2)/3\). Hence the counts do not decrease
up to \(m=\lfloor(n+1)/3\rfloor\) and strictly decrease afterwards, so this
\(m\) is a maximizer (tied with \(m-1\) when \(n\equiv2\pmod3\)). The
\(n+1\) counts sum to \((1+2)^n=3^n\), which proves
\eqref{eq:tripling-mode}.
\end{proof}

The interval partition and the identification of the middle block are
those of \citet[Lemma~4.2]{OAI26} with both of its parameters equal to
\(r\). There the terms between blocks are removed by a degeneration that
scales coordinates by powers of \(t\); here the type restriction removes
them.

\subsection{The separator}
\label{subsec:separator}

The separator makes the \(M\) sectors of Lemmas~\ref{lem:multiway}
and~\ref{lem:tripling} independent on all three legs, so that each sector
can be reindexed on its own. The idea is to average over copies with
Fourier phases. On the shared leg each variable receives a tentative
sector label \(g'\), and on the other two legs the variables receive
auxiliary indices \(e\) and \(e'\). The phases are chosen so that
averaging over the \(2M-1\) copies keeps exactly the terms with
\(e-e'+g'-g=0\), where \(g\) is the true sector label. Weights in the
parameter \(t\) then multiply each such term by \(t^{(g'-g)^2}\). At
\(t=0\) only \(g'=g\) survives, which forces \(e=e'\); the free index
\(e=e'\) is the dot product.

\begin{proposition}[Separator]\label{prop:separator}
Let \(T=\sum_{g=0}^{M-1}T_g\) with \(T_g\in X\otimes Y_g\otimes Z_g\),
where \(Y=\bigoplus_gY_g\) and \(Z=\bigoplus_gZ_g\). The substitutions
in the proof give a degeneration
\begin{equation}\label{eq:separator}
 (2M-1)\,T\degen\bigoplus_{g=0}^{M-1}T_g\,B_X(M),
\end{equation}
in which each summand on the right has its own copy of \(X\). Its
polynomial has degree at most \((M-1)^2\), and its exponents lie in
\(\{\delta^2:0\le\delta\le M-1\}\). Permuting the three legs gives the
same statement with any chosen shared leg.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Choose bases and write
\(T_g=\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}T_g[\alpha,\beta,\gamma]\,
x_\alpha y_{g,\beta}z_{g,\gamma}\), where the \(x_\alpha\) are the
coordinates of \(X\) and the \(y_{g,\beta}\) and \(z_{g,\gamma}\) those
of \(Y_g\) and \(Z_g\); the ranges of \(\beta\) and \(\gamma\) may depend
on \(g\). Put \(\zeta=\zeta_{2M-1}\), and index the source copies by
\(\ell=0,\ldots,2M-2\). The target variables are \(X_{\alpha,g'}\),
\(Y_{g,\beta,e}\) and \(Z_{g,\gamma,e'}\), with
\(g',e,e'\in\{0,\ldots,M-1\}\).

\emph{Step 1: Fourier averaging.} In copy \(\ell\) substitute
\begin{align*}
 x_\alpha&\longmapsto\sum_{g'=0}^{M-1}\zeta^{\ell g'}X_{\alpha,g'},\qquad
 y_{g,\beta}\longmapsto\sum_{e=0}^{M-1}\zeta^{\ell(e-g)}Y_{g,\beta,e},\\
 z_{g,\gamma}&\longmapsto(2M-1)^{-1}\sum_{e'=0}^{M-1}\zeta^{-\ell e'}
 Z_{g,\gamma,e'}.
\end{align*}
Each phase uses only indices visible on its own leg, since the second-
and third-leg coordinates carry their sector label \(g\). All copies map
into the same target variables, so the substitutions are linear on each
leg of \((2M-1)T\). Summing over the copies, the term
\(x_\alpha y_{g,\beta}z_{g,\gamma}\) becomes
\[
 \sum_{g',e,e'}\biggl(\frac1{2M-1}\sum_{\ell=0}^{2M-2}
 \zeta^{\ell(e-e'+g'-g)}\biggr)
 X_{\alpha,g'}Y_{g,\beta,e}Z_{g,\gamma,e'}.
\]
The bracket is one if \(e-e'+g'-g\equiv0\pmod{2M-1}\) and zero otherwise.
Since \(|e-e'+g'-g|\le2M-2\), the congruence is the equality
\begin{equation}\label{eq:separator-fourier}
 e-e'+g'-g=0.
\end{equation}

\emph{Step 2: weights.} Multiply \(X_{\alpha,g'}\) by \(t^{g'^2}\),
\(Y_{g,\beta,e}\) by \(t^{2ge-g^2}\), and \(Z_{g,\gamma,e'}\) by
\(t^{-2ge'}\). On a term satisfying~\eqref{eq:separator-fourier} we have
\(e-e'=g-g'\), so the total exponent is
\(g'^2+2g(e-e')-g^2=g'^2+2g(g-g')-g^2=(g'-g)^2\).
The weights complete the square: the cross term \(-2gg'\) mixes the
shared-leg label \(g'\) with the sector label \(g\), and no single leg
sees both, but on~\eqref{eq:separator-fourier} it equals
\(2g(e-e')-2g^2\), which splits between the second and third legs.
Individual weights may be negative, but every surviving exponent is
\((g'-g)^2\), so the transformed tensor is a polynomial in \(t\) of degree
at most \((M-1)^2\) with exponents in \(\{\delta^2:0\le\delta\le M-1\}\).

\emph{Step 3: the constant term.} The terms of exponent zero have
\(g'=g\), and then~\eqref{eq:separator-fourier} gives \(e=e'\). The
constant term is thus
\[
 \sum_{g=0}^{M-1}\ \sum_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}T_g[\alpha,\beta,\gamma]
 \sum_{e=0}^{M-1}X_{\alpha,g}Y_{g,\beta,e}Z_{g,\gamma,e}.
\]
For each \(g\) this is \(T_gB_X(M)\), with first-leg variables
\(X_{\alpha,g}\), second-leg variables \(Y_{g,\beta,e}\) and third-leg
variables \(Z_{g,\gamma,e}\). Different \(g\) use disjoint variables on
all three legs, so the constant term is the direct
sum~\eqref{eq:separator}.
\end{proof}

The output of the separator has two multiplicities: the label \(g\)
counts \(M\) direct summands, for which the source pays \(2M-1\) copies of
\(T\), and the index \(e=e'\) is the dot product \(B_X(M)\) inside each
summand, whose first leg is one-dimensional. This dot product carries the
gain of a relation: in the three orientations of
Section~\ref{subsec:orientations} it becomes the auxiliary tensor
\(\MM_M\), whose side \(M\) is the number of sectors, namely the
multinomial coefficient \(n!/\prod_jn_j!\) for a split and at least
\(3^n/(n+1)\) for a tripling with the middle count of
Lemma~\ref{lem:tripling}. For a split, this gain is set against the
auxiliary \(\MM_{(h+1)^n}\) that the split consumes. The copies cost only
a constant: the three orientations turn \((2M-1)^3\) source copies into
\(M^3\) output summands (Corollary~\ref{cor:oriented-relations}), a ratio
below \(8\) per relation that does not grow with \(n\).

Proposition~\ref{prop:separator} is a variant of
\citet[Proposition~3.1]{OAI26}, which uses \(5M\) source copies; with the
phases above, the quantity in the Fourier condition has absolute value at
most \(2M-2\), so \(2M-1\) copies suffice.
Proposition~\ref{prop:separator-general} in Appendix~\ref{subsec:sparse}
allows a dot product of another dimension and a general phase slope; the
construction uses only Proposition~\ref{prop:separator}.

\subsection{Three orientations}
\label{subsec:orientations}

We apply each relation in the three orientations
\(\pi\in\mathcal A_\sigma\) of one parity class and tensor the results, as
in the cyclic symmetrization of \citet[Section~3.2]{CVZ21}, with the class
alternating between rows. In orientation \(\pi\) we apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:multiway} or~\ref{lem:tripling} to the permuted tensor and
the separator with shared leg \(\pi(1)\). We call the legs of a tensor
product of oriented factors its \emph{global legs}; leg \(l\) of a factor
\(T^\pi\) lies in global leg \(\pi(l)\). The orientations serve two
purposes. First, they put the single-variable leg of a dot product
\(B_X(d)\) on each global leg once, so that, up to the order of the
factors within each global leg, the three oriented copies combine into
\(\MM_d\) by~\eqref{eq:coset-dot-products}; this applies both to the
auxiliary dot product of a split and to the dot product produced by the
separator. Second, the symmetrized tensors satisfy
\(S^\sigma_{r,b}\cong S^{-\sigma}_{b,r}\) by~\eqref{eq:cyclic-identities},
which links adjacent rows (see the end of this section).

After separation, each sector receives its own coordinate permutation,
which identifies it with a fixed \emph{canonical sector tensor}
\(\mathcal V\), the same for all sectors of the relation. For a split,
the positions are sorted by increasing filtration index, and
\(\mathcal V=\prod_jC(r,b+h-2j)^{\otimes n_j}\). For a tripling, the
\(n-m\) outer positions come first and the \(m\) middle positions follow,
each group in its original order, and
\(\mathcal V=C(r,r)^{\otimes(n-m)}(C(r,r)^{(23)})^{\otimes m}\); since the
left and right blocks have the same dimensions, \(\mathcal V\) does not
depend on the outer labels. Independent choices of sectors in the three
orientations therefore all give the same tensor. For a tripling, the
convolution factors of the three oriented copies of \(\mathcal V\)
regroup as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cyclic-tripling-balance}
 \bigotimes_{\pi\in\mathcal A_\sigma}
 \Bigl(C(r,r)^{\otimes(n-m)}\bigl(C(r,r)^{(23)}\bigr)^{\otimes m}\Bigr)^\pi
 \cong(S^\sigma_{r,r})^{\otimes(n-m)}(S^{-\sigma}_{r,r})^{\otimes m}
 \cong(S^\sigma_{r,r})^{\otimes n}.
\end{equation}
The first isomorphism reorders tensor factors within each global leg and
uses \((C(r,r)^{(23)})^\pi=C(r,r)^{\pi(23)}\), where \(\pi(23)\) runs
over \(\mathcal A_{-\sigma}\) as \(\pi\) runs over \(\mathcal A_\sigma\).
The second applies the diagonal isomorphism
\(S^+_{r,r}\cong S^-_{r,r}\) of~\eqref{eq:cyclic-identities} to the last
\(m\) factors. Both are coordinate identifications within each global
leg.

\begin{corollary}[Oriented relations]\label{cor:oriented-relations}
Let \(\sigma\in\{+,-\}\).
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] \emph{Split.} Let \(r\ge1\), \(h\ge1\) and \(b\ge h+1\), let
\((n_0,\ldots,n_h)\) be a type with positive sum \(n\), and put
\(M=n!/\prod_jn_j!\). Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:split-relation}
 (2M-1)^3\,(S^\sigma_{r,b})^{\otimes n}\,\MM_{(h+1)^n}
 \degen M^3\Bigl(\prod_{j=0}^h(S^\sigma_{r,b+h-2j})^{\otimes n_j}\Bigr)
 \MM_M .
\end{equation}
\item[(b)] \emph{Tripling.} Let \(r\ge2\), \(n\ge1\) and \(0\le m\le n\),
and put \(M=M_{\mathrm t}(n,m)\). Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tripling-relation}
 (2M-1)^3\,(S^\sigma_{r,4r-1})^{\otimes n}
 \degen M^3\,(S^\sigma_{r,r})^{\otimes n}\,\MM_M .
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
Both degenerations are composed of the maps of this section, and their
polynomials have degree at most \(3(M-1)^2\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Let \(\mathcal U\) be \((C(r,b)B_X(h+1))^{\otimes n}\) or
\(C(r,4r-1)^{\otimes n}\), and let \(\mathcal V\) be the canonical sector
tensor. In each orientation \(\pi\in\mathcal A_\sigma\), the type
restriction of Lemma~\ref{lem:multiway} or~\ref{lem:tripling}, the
separator of Proposition~\ref{prop:separator} with shared leg \(\pi(1)\),
and the sector-specific coordinate permutations give
\((2M-1)\,\mathcal U^\pi\degen M\,(\mathcal V B_X(M))^\pi\). The coordinate
permutations act after separation and do not involve \(t\). On the first
leg they reindex the copy with tentative label \(g'\) according to the
word of sector \(g'\), so they are defined on all target variables of the
separator, including those of its terms of positive degree
(see~\eqref{eq:reindexing} in Appendix~\ref{sec:composed-maps}), as
Lemma~\ref{lem:composition} requires. Tensoring the three orientations
with one common parameter \(t\) gives, by Lemma~\ref{lem:composition}, a
degeneration
\[
 (2M-1)^3\bigotimes_{\pi\in\mathcal A_\sigma}\mathcal U^\pi
 \degen M^3\bigotimes_{\pi\in\mathcal A_\sigma}
 \bigl(\mathcal V B_X(M)\bigr)^\pi
\]
of degree at most \(3(M-1)^2\). It remains to identify both sides by
coordinate permutations within each global leg; such fixed maps change
neither the degree nor the constant term. For a split,
\(B_X(h+1)^{\otimes n}\cong B_X((h+1)^n)\), so the source is
\((S^\sigma_{r,b})^{\otimes n}\MM_{(h+1)^n}\) and each summand of the
target is \(\prod_j(S^\sigma_{r,b+h-2j})^{\otimes n_j}\MM_M\). For a
tripling, the source is \((S^\sigma_{r,4r-1})^{\otimes n}\), and
\eqref{eq:cyclic-tripling-balance} identifies each summand of the target
with \((S^\sigma_{r,r})^{\otimes n}\MM_M\).
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Alternating signs between rows.}
Row \(r\) of the construction uses the class \(\mathcal A_{\sigma_r}\), where
\(\sigma_r=(-1)^{a-r}\) is the row sign of Section~\ref{sec:flows}, so
adjacent rows use opposite classes. The reason is the boundary between
rows: the walk of row \(r\) ends at column \(r-1\), the factor
\(S^{\sigma_r}_{r,r-1}\), and the walk of row \(r-1\) starts at column
\(r\), the factor \(S^{\sigma_{r-1}}_{r-1,r}\).
By~\eqref{eq:cyclic-identities} the first is isomorphic to
\(S^{-\sigma_r}_{r-1,r}\), which is the second exactly when
\(\sigma_{r-1}=-\sigma_r\). With one sign \(\sigma\) for all rows, the
boundary factors would be \(S^\sigma_{r,r-1}\) and \(S^\sigma_{r-1,r}\);
for \(r\ge3\) the identities~\eqref{eq:cyclic-identities}, the only ones the
ledger of Proposition~\ref{prop:rounded-ledger} uses, do not relate them,
and the ledger would not close. Alternation makes an isomorphism between
\(S^+_{r,b}\) and \(S^-_{r,b}\) for \(r\ne b\) unnecessary, and the
construction uses none. Only the alternation matters: the choice \(\sigma_a=+\) is only a normalization, since
\(S^+_{a,a}\cong S^-_{a,a}\). Applying every relation in all six
orientations, with the products \(Q_{r,b}=S^+_{r,b}S^-_{r,b}\cong Q_{b,r}\),
would need no signs but squares all dimensions;
Section~\ref{subsec:exact-normalization} compares this sixfold
construction with the threefold one used here.
