\section{Exact type restrictions and separation of a shared leg}
\label{sec:maps}

We realize the profile relations by finite tensor maps. Each local map
first produces matched sectors on two legs, with the third leg still
shared. A Fourier separator then makes the sectors independent on all
three legs. More precisely, the intermediate tensor is
\(\sum_\omega A_\omega\), where
\(A_\omega\in X\otimes Y_\omega\otimes Z_\omega\), the spaces
\(Y_\omega\) and \(Z_\omega\) are direct-sum blocks, and \(X\) is common
to all sectors. A separate change of first-leg coordinates in each
sector is valid only after separation.

\subsection{A determinant filtration}

The filtration used here is the integral Clebsch--Gordan filtration;
see \citet[Lemma~2.5, Theorem~2.8, and Corollary~2.9]{Maakestad23}.
We give its bases to identify the convolution slices and then obtain an
exact restriction by matching types in tensor powers.

Let \(V_e=\Q[u,v]_e\) be the homogeneous binary forms of degree \(e\).
For integers \(d\ge h\ge1\), put
\[
 W_{d,h}=\Q[u,v]_d\otimes\Q[s,t]_h,\qquad \Delta=ut-vs.
\]
Multiplication by a form in \(V_{r-1}\), leaving the auxiliary variables
\(s,t\) unchanged, represents \(C(r,d+1)B_X(h+1)\). Its second-input
and output spaces have the filtrations
\[
 F^jW_{d,h}=\Delta^jW_{d-j,h-j}\quad(0\le j\le h),\qquad
 F^{h+1}W_{d,h}=0.
\]
After removal of \(\Delta^j\), diagonal substitution \((s,t)=(u,v)\)
identifies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:filtration-quotient}
 F^jW_{d,h}/F^{j+1}W_{d,h}\cong V_{d+h-2j}.
\end{equation}
Here and below these are fixed-coordinate polynomial spaces; no
representation-theoretic identification is needed.

We use an explicit integer basis for this filtration. Define
\[
 L_{p,q}(i)=
 \begin{cases}
 u^{p-i}v^is^q,&0\le i\le p,\\
 v^ps^{q-(i-p)}t^{i-p},&p<i\le p+q,
 \end{cases}
\]
and take
\begin{equation}\label{eq:determinant-basis}
 B_{j,i}=\Delta^jL_{d-j,h-j}(i),\qquad
 0\le j\le h,\quad 0\le i\le d+h-2j.
\end{equation}
To verify both the basis and \eqref{eq:filtration-quotient}, repeatedly
replace \(ut\) by \(vs+\Delta\). Modulo \(\Delta\), each monomial
reduces to the unique displayed lift having the same total exponent of
\(v,t\). The remainder is divisible by \(\Delta\); repeat in
bidegree \((d-1,h-1)\). The quotient monomials at each stage are
independent under diagonal substitution. This gives unique integer
coordinates and terminates after at most \(h\) such steps. The number
of basis vectors is
\[
 \sum_{j=0}^h(d+h-2j+1)=(d+1)(h+1).
\]
Use the same construction in the output space \(W_{d+r-1,h}\).
The third tensor leg uses the dual of this output basis; applying the
input basis matrix to that leg would give the wrong tensor map.

Multiplication commutes with \(\Delta\), so it never decreases the
filtration index. On quotient \(j\), multiplication by
\(u^{r-1-\alpha}v^\alpha\) sends monomial index \(i\) to
\(i+\alpha\) with coefficient one. With \(d=b-1\), the diagonal
blocks are therefore exactly
\[
 C(r,b+h),\ C(r,b+h-2),\ \ldots,\ C(r,b-h).
\]

\begin{lemma}[Exact multiway types]\label{lem:multiway}\label{lem:determinant}
Let \(r\ge1\), \(h\ge1\), and \(b\ge h+1\). For nonnegative
integers \(n_0,\ldots,n_h\) with positive sum \(n\), a specified
restriction of \((C(r,b)B_X(h+1))^{\otimes n}\) has
\[
 M=\frac{n!}{\prod_{j=0}^h n_j!}
\]
matched sectors on its second and third legs. Each sector is isomorphic
to \(\prod_{j=0}^hC(r,b+h-2j)^{\otimes n_j}\). The first leg is
shared; the sector-specific reordering of its tensor positions is made
only after separation.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use \eqref{eq:determinant-basis} in each tensor position. Retain on both
affected legs only the words containing \(n_j\) occurrences of
filtration index \(j\). Every supported term has input indices
\(j_\nu\) and output indices \(j'_\nu\) with
\(j'_\nu\ge j_\nu\) in each position \(\nu\). Both retained words
have total index \(\sum_jjn_j\), so every inequality is an equality.
Thus all off-diagonal blocks disappear exactly. The remaining words
agree positionwise; their number is the displayed multinomial
coefficient. Their diagonal blocks give the claimed factors.
\end{proof}

For \(h=1\), the two quotient blocks are \(C(r,b+1)\) and
\(C(r,b-1)\), the neighboring-length relation. For larger \(h\),
the auxiliary dimension is \(h+1\), whereas \(h\) repeated binary
steps consume dimension \(2^h\). This changes the finite tensor cost.
It does not strengthen scalar concavity: pairing lengths equally far
from \(b\) already gives
\((h+1)P(r,b)\ge\sum_{j=0}^hP(r,b+h-2j)\).
The exact type restriction itself has no interpolation degree.

\subsection{Tripling with only the middle count fixed}

\begin{lemma}[Exact tripling types]\label{lem:tripling}
Let \(r\ge2\), \(n\ge1\), and \(0\le m\le n\). A coordinate
restriction of \(C(r,4r-1)^{\otimes n}\) has
\[
 M_{\mathrm t}(n,m)=\binom nm2^{n-m}
\]
matched sectors on its second and third legs. Each sector contains
\(n-m\) factors isomorphic to \(C(r,r)\) and \(m\) factors
isomorphic to \(C(r,r)^{(23)}\). The first leg remains shared.
The choice \(m=\lfloor(n+1)/3\rfloor\) maximizes this count and gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tripling-mode}
 M_{\mathrm t}(n,m)\ge\frac{3^n}{n+1}.
\end{equation}
No divisibility condition on \(n\) is required.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A supported term has \(0\le i<r\), \(0\le y<4r-1\), and
\(z=i+y\). Partition the index sets into the following intervals,
with endpoints included:
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
 &\text{left}&\text{middle}&\text{right}\\ \hline
Y &[0,r-1]&[r,3r-2]&[3r-1,4r-2]\\
Z &[0,2r-2]&[2r-1,3r-2]&[3r-1,5r-3].
\end{array}
\]
A left \(Y\)-index forces a left \(Z\)-index, and a right
\(Y\)-index forces a right \(Z\)-index. Every mismatched term has
a middle \(Y\)-label and an outer \(Z\)-label. Retain the words
with exactly \(m\) middle labels on each leg, leaving their outer
labels unrestricted. The number of middle labels cannot increase from
input to output. Equality of their totals therefore removes every
mismatched term. The surviving labels agree in every position, giving
\(\binom nm\) choices of middle positions and \(2^{n-m}\) choices
of outer labels.

Each outer block is a translate of \(C(r,r)\). The middle block has
terms
\[
 x_{r-1-i}\,y_{r+i+j}\,z_{2r-1+j},\qquad 0\le i,j<r.
\]
Reversing the first index and shifting the interval coordinates identifies
it with \(C(r,r)^{(23)}\). This identifies a tensor with permuted
leg dimensions; it does not perform a leg exchange on just one part of
a tensor. The word-specific first-leg reversal is deferred until
separation.

Finally,
\(M_{\mathrm t}(n,m+1)/M_{\mathrm t}(n,m)=(n-m)/(2(m+1))\).
These ratios show that \(\lfloor(n+1)/3\rfloor\) is a maximizing
choice, with a possible tie. The \(n+1\) counts sum to \(3^n\),
which proves \eqref{eq:tripling-mode}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A Fourier separator}

The following form includes an auxiliary dimension and a phase slope.
The main construction uses only \(K_0=M\), \(c=1\).

\begin{proposition}[Shared-leg separator]\label{prop:separator}
Let
\(A=\sum_{h=0}^{M-1}A_h\), with
\(A_h\in X\otimes Y_h\otimes Z_h\), where the \(Y_h\) and \(Z_h\)
are pairwise disjoint sectors. For positive integers \(K_0,c\), put
\(L=K_0+c(M-1)\). There is an explicit degeneration
\begin{equation}\label{eq:separator}
 L A\degen\bigoplus_{h=0}^{M-1}(A_h B_X(K_0)).
\end{equation}
Its polynomial has exponent support contained in
\(\{cd^2:0\le d\le d_*\}\), where
\(d_* =\min(M-1,\lfloor(K_0-1)/c\rfloor)\).
In particular, \(K_0=M,c=1\) uses \(2M-1\) source copies and has
degree at most \((M-1)^2\).
Permuting all three legs gives the same statement with any chosen shared leg.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Choose a primitive \(L\)-th root \(\zeta\). In source copy \(\ell\),
\(0\le\ell<L\), make the substitutions
\begin{align*}
 x_a^{(\ell)}&\longmapsto\sum_{g=0}^{M-1}\zeta^{\ell cg}X_{a,g},\\
 y_{h,b}^{(\ell)}&\longmapsto
       \sum_{u=0}^{K_0-1}\zeta^{\ell(u-ch)}Y_{h,b,u},\\
 z_{h,d}^{(\ell)}&\longmapsto
       L^{-1}\sum_{v=0}^{K_0-1}\zeta^{-\ell v}Z_{h,d,v}.
\end{align*}
Summing the copies retains exactly
\(u-v+c(g-h)\equiv0\pmod L\). Its absolute value is at most \(L-1\),
so the congruence is the equality \(u-v+c(g-h)=0\).
Multiply the new variables by the respective powers of \(t\)
\[
 t^{cg^2},\qquad t^{2hu-ch^2},\qquad t^{-2hv}.
\]
Every surviving term has total exponent
\(cg^2+2h(u-v)-ch^2=c(g-h)^2\).
It is zero exactly when \(g=h\) and \(u=v\). These terms are precisely
the target in \eqref{eq:separator}, whose first variables \(X_{a,h}\)
are now independent for different \(h\).
Moreover \(c|g-h|=|u-v|\le K_0-1\), proving the exponent support.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Threefold balancing}

Products over the three cyclic leg permutations are an established
balancing operation; see \citet[Section~3.3]{CVZ21}. We use the two
parity classes of permutations to make this operation compatible with
the row boundaries of the convolution construction.

Let \(\mathcal A_+\) and \(\mathcal A_-\) be the even and odd
permutations of three legs, as in the definition of \(S^\sigma_{r,b}\).
Apply a relation and its separator in each of the three orientations
\(\pi\in\mathcal A_\sigma\). The shared leg is the image of the
native first leg under \(\pi\); it occupies each global leg exactly
once. Consequently the source auxiliary of a width-\(h\) relation
becomes
\[
 B_X(h+1)B_Y(h+1)B_Z(h+1)\cong\MM_{h+1},
\]
and its separated convolution factors combine into the required
\(S^\sigma_{r,b+h-2j}\). The three separator auxiliaries likewise
combine into \(\MM_M\).

For tripling, put \(C=C(r,r)\) and \(\tau=(23)\). After separation,
every retained word is put into the same order: the \(n-m\) outer
positions, followed by the \(m\) middle positions. Thus independent
sector choices in the three orientations all have the same canonical
sector tensor \(C^{\otimes(n-m)}(C^\tau)^{\otimes m}\). Regrouping
these factors gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cyclic-tripling-balance}
 \bigotimes_{\pi\in\mathcal A_\sigma}
 \left(C^{\otimes(n-m)}(C^\tau)^{\otimes m}\right)^\pi
 \cong(S^\sigma_{r,r})^{\otimes(n-m)}
       (S^{-\sigma}_{r,r})^{\otimes m}
 \cong(S^\sigma_{r,r})^{\otimes n}.
\end{equation}
The first isomorphism reorders tensor factors within each global leg;
the second uses the diagonal sign isomorphism
\(S^+_{r,r}\cong S^-_{r,r}\). All sector-specific reversals have
already been made on independent first-leg blocks. No individual factor
undergoes an illicit exchange of two global legs.

Adjacent rows of the profile construction will use opposite signs.
This makes their boundary factors agree under the actual isomorphism
\(S^\sigma_{r,b}\cong S^{-\sigma}_{b,r}\); using one sign for all
rows would not give that identification. The signed resource accounting
is carried out in the construction below. Appendix~\ref{sec:composed-maps}
specifies the complete relation maps, including their positive-degree
terms. Further separator refinements appear in
Appendix~\ref{subsec:sparse}; they are not needed for the main theorem.
