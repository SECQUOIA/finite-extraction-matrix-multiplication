\section{Introduction}

A bound on the matrix multiplication exponent and a finite description of
an algorithm are different outputs of a proof. An asymptotic tensor
argument can establish that a suitable rank decomposition exists without
specifying its dimension or coefficients. A finite extraction chooses the
tensor maps and their parameters, then accounts for the operations needed
to use them. This paper gives such an extraction for the convolution
relations introduced in the preprint \emph{An Upper Bound of \(9/4\) for
the Matrix Multiplication Exponent}~\citep{OAI26}.

That preprint separates tensor blocks sharing one leg, applies the
separation to polynomial multiplication, and derives inequalities for a
symmetrized convolution profile. Its diagonal growth leads, through a
detecting-character argument, to the claimed bound \(\omega\le9/4\).
Here \(\omega\) is the infimum of arithmetic exponents for square matrix
multiplication. The final algorithmic step chooses a matrix size and an
exact rank decomposition whose existence follows from the bound. The
chosen decomposition is not specified.

We construct the finite maps directly and give quantitative parameter
choices. The main difficulty is to preserve the exact multiplicities of
all tensor factors while controlling the cost of separation, interpolation,
and auxiliary tensors. We use multiway convolution relations, choose their
frequencies through a finite absorbing walk, and round the resulting
rational counts while preserving every resource balance. This avoids
clearing potentially large denominators. The construction gives a family
whose fully counted exponents tend to \(9/4\), with an explicit bound on
its recursive source dimension.

\subsection{Main results}

A rational bilinear seed is a finite list of rational linear forms whose
products and recombinations compute a square matrix product.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For every positive rational \(\eps\), a terminating procedure produces
an integer \(d\ge2\) and an exact rational bilinear algorithm for
\(d\times d\) matrix multiplication using fewer than
\(d^{9/4+\eps}\) bilinear multiplications, each multiplying a linear
form in the left input by a linear form in the right input.
A fixed recursion on this seed multiplies \(n\times n\) matrices over
any field of characteristic zero in
\(O_\eps(n^{9/4+\eps})\) arithmetic operations.
\end{theorem}

In particular, \(\omega\le9/4\). A bound with any positive real allowance
follows by choosing a smaller positive rational allowance. The procedure
generates identities by specified finite maps; it does not search for
solutions to rank equations. Theorem~\ref{thm:effective} and
Corollary~\ref{cor:seed} prove this statement.

The quantitative result first takes the form of a relative scheme:
\(m\) independent products of \(B\times B\) matrices are reduced to
\(r\) products of \(A\times A\) matrices. All input transformations and
output recombinations are specified linear maps. Pooling the subproblems
at each recursive level gives the exponent
\(p=\log(r/m)/\log(B/A)\), with all linear work included when \(p>2\).

\begin{theorem}[Quantitative relative construction]\label{thm:main-relative}
For every integer \(a\ge2^{64}\), a specified finite construction gives
positive integers \(r,m,A,B\), with \(B>A\), and an exact relative
scheme over an explicitly determined cyclotomic field. Put
\(\ell_a=\lceil\log_2a\rceil\). Its recursive exponent and source
dimension satisfy
\[
 \frac94<p=\frac{\log(r/m)}{\log(B/A)}
       <\frac94+\frac{900}{\log a},
 \qquad
 \log A\le181a^5\ell_a^2.
\]
\end{theorem}

Theorem~\ref{thm:quantitative} proves these bounds. In particular, for
a positive rational allowance \(\eps\), one may choose
\[
 \ell=\max\left\{64,\left\lceil\frac{1800}{\eps}\right\rceil\right\},
 \qquad a=2^\ell.
\]
The resulting relative scheme has exponent below \(9/4+\eps\) and
\(\log A\le181\,2^{5\ell}\ell^2\). These choices require only integer
and rational computations. The constants are conservative; the statement
provides a proved finite choice for every allowance.

The bound on \(A\) concerns the recursive source of the relative scheme.
To obtain the rational seed in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, we unroll a fixed
scheme to scalar multiplications, restrict its coefficients from its fixed
number field to \(\Q\), and select a sufficiently large dimension. This
last procedure terminates, but the displayed bound on \(A\) is not a
bound on the seed dimension \(d\), its coefficient sizes, or its
construction time.

\subsection{The construction and its contributions}

The scalar starting point is the least positive symmetric profile satisfying
normalization, discrete concavity, and shifted tripling:
\[
 F(m,n)=s_{\min(m,n)}
       \left(\max(m,n)+\frac{\min(m,n)-1}{2}\right),
 \qquad
 s_a=\prod_{h=1}^{a-1}\left(1+\frac1{3h}\right).
\]
We prove both the formula and its minimality. Its diagonal growth is
\(a^{4/3}\), and the limiting exponent associated with a fixed profile
index is
\[
 \tau_a=\frac{3\log(2a-1)}{\log F(a,a)}.
\]
Every \(\tau_a\) exceeds \(9/4\), and \(\tau_a\to9/4\).
The diagonal certificate uses tripling only at the row index. The full
least-profile theorem allows all shifted tripling inequalities.

To realize the profile relations, we apply the determinant filtration of
binary forms to convolution multiplication. An auxiliary factor of dimension
\(d\) gives \(d\) convolution blocks. In a tensor power, retaining equal
filtration counts on the two affected legs removes every off-diagonal
term exactly. The tripling map requires only the number of middle blocks
to agree; the two outer blocks can be chosen freely. This larger type
selection works for every positive integer tripling count and removes
the need for divisibility by three.

The affine profile determines rational transition probabilities among the
blocks. Their occupation counts solve finite rational linear systems and
give the exact resource identities. A square-root drift estimate bounds
both the total occupation and the logarithmic auxiliary cost by \(O(a)\).
This is the quantitative reason for using multiway relations: their larger
steps keep the expected cost of traversing a row bounded independently
of its length.

We next round the transition counts to integers within their edgewise
floor and ceiling bounds, preserving the resource identities exactly.
For any integer scale \(K\ge1\), the rounded entropy expression has
the form
\[
 K\log F(a,a)-\mathcal L,
 \qquad 0\le\mathcal L\le4J_a,
 \qquad J_a=O(a^3).
\]
The estimate remains valid when occupation counts are arbitrarily small
or zero. Thus the scale can be chosen for its error bound, independently
of the denominators of the exact occupations. Exact denominator clearing
for a binary certificate is analyzed separately as a benchmark; its
exponential scale is not a lower bound for the rounded construction.

Three cyclic leg permutations suffice when successive rows use opposite
orientations. After separation, the common source and output factors form
an explicit catalyst. We retain it, first make the relation exact by
interpolation, and then reuse the exact relation. Pooling the recursive
products removes a per-level rounding loss. The final cost includes every
source copy, output copy, interpolation evaluation, auxiliary factor, and
catalyst supply. The smaller threefold balance alone does not improve the
exponent: at corresponding doubled tensor powers, its charged scalar
counts agree with those of sixfold balance.

The numerical bound \(9/4\) was already announced in \citet{OAI26}.
Compared with that argument, the contributions here are the direct finite
extraction, the multiway convolution restrictions, the quantitative
occupation and rounding analysis, and the resulting explicit source-size
bound. The proof is self-contained at the level of tensor maps and does
not use the preprint's detecting-character theorem or spectral appendix.

\subsection{Relation to previous work}

Strassen's recursive use of a finite bilinear identity established the
link between small matrix algorithms and an arithmetic exponent below
three~\citep{Str69}. Approximate bilinear algorithms, tensor powers, and
interpolation provide the standard passage from approximate identities to
exact algorithms~\citep{Bini80}. Sch\"onhage's asymptotic sum inequality
relates simultaneous matrix products to the exponent~\citep{Sch81}.
An accessible account of finite interpolation and the sum inequality is
given by \citet[Theorem~7 and the preceding discussion]{Sto10}.
Our root-of-unity averaging is an explicit form of interpolation, and our
pooled recursion proves the needed conversion of simultaneous outputs
directly.

The asymptotic spectrum supplies a general language for comparing tensor
powers~\citep{Str88,CVZ}. Relative bilinear complexity is an established
setting for reductions between matrix multiplication tensors~\citep{Str87}.
Catalytic tensor comparisons retain an auxiliary tensor on both sides of
a reduction~\citep{CVZ21}. We use these familiar forms of comparison,
while specifying the maps and retaining the cost of supplying the catalyst.

The determinant filtration itself is also established. Maakestad gives a
universal integral Clebsch--Gordan filtration for binary forms, together
with an explicit basis for its determinant kernel~\citep[Section~2,
Lemma~2.5, Theorem~2.8 and Corollary~2.9]{Maakestad23}.
Our application identifies the induced convolution multiplication on its
successive quotients and the exact restrictions obtained by fixing the
filtration counts. We do not claim a new Clebsch--Gordan filtration.
Likewise, the existence of an integer circulation between integral lower
and upper bounds is a standard consequence of total
unimodularity~\citep[Theorem~2]{HK56}. The algorithmic return-edge
reduction and fractional-cycle rounding also appear in
\citet[Sections~2.2--3.1]{KangPayor15}. We use circulation rounding to
conserve tensor multiplicities; the separate weighted entropy estimate
controls the cost of this rounding for our occupation flow. Related
relative-entropy bounds for optimal fixed-total probability approximations appear
in \citet[Eq.~(42)]{BoechererGeiger16}; our action totals may change
under the globally conserved rounding.
The probabilistic tools for the occupation estimates are the fundamental
matrix of an absorbing chain and a Doob
transform~\citep{GrinsteadSnell97,LevinPeres17}; the required uniform
auxiliary-cost estimate is proved here.

Fixed-frequency restrictions, entropy estimates, and products over leg
permutations are standard in tensor-power methods; Le Gall gives a
detailed example~\citep{LG14}. Threefold cyclic symmetrization is also
explicit in \citet[Section~3.3]{CVZ21}; the alternating row signs below
make it compatible with the convolution resource ledger. Recent
refinements of the laser method use
carefully chosen distributions and asymmetric tensor
powers~\citep{AW21,AD25,Dup26}. For numerical context, the SODA 2025 paper
of \citet{AD25} proves \(\omega<2.371339\), and the 2026 preprint of
\citet{Dup26} reports \(\omega<2.371177\). The \(9/4\) bound announced
in the October 2026 preprint~\citep{OAI26} is substantially stronger.
The present proof derives that bound from finite convolution maps,
independently of its spectral conclusion. Interpolation, type counting,
circulation integrality, symmetrization, and bilinear recursion remain
classical ingredients of the construction.

The closest source for the shared-leg separator and the initial convolution
relations is \citet{OAI26}, Proposition~3.1 and Section~4. We give their
coordinate maps, use exact type restrictions, and reduce the separator's
source count from \(5M\) to \(2M-1\). The subsequent occupation flows,
conserved types, and cost estimates concern this specific family of
relations. No claim of a first general constructive conversion of
asymptotic tensor bounds is intended.

Indeed, existence of a suitable complex rank decomposition already permits
an eventual symbolic search: its defining equations have rational
coefficients, and a complex solution can be replaced by an algebraic one.
The purpose of the present construction is to generate identities through
prescribed operations and to bound their finite resources. This gives
mathematical control of the extraction without solving an unspecified
rank-decomposition problem.

\subsection{Scope and organization}

The results concern exact arithmetic with a fixed positive exponent
allowance. They give neither one fixed recursion with exponent exactly
\(9/4\) nor a numerical stability guarantee. The relative source-size
bound is explicit but enormous, and no useful finite-size implementation
is established. The finite examples illustrate the individual charges in
the proof; they are not measurements of a matrix multiplication program.

Section~\ref{sec:preliminaries} fixes the tensor conventions and arithmetic
model. Section~\ref{sec:profile} proves the least-profile theorem, and
Section~\ref{sec:maps} gives the convolution restrictions, separator, and
threefold balancing. Sections~\ref{sec:flows} and \ref{sec:rounding}
construct the rational occupations and round them with exact resource
balances. Section~\ref{sec:extraction} composes the maps, proves the pooled
recursion, gives the quantitative parameter family, and extracts rational
seeds. Section~\ref{sec:costs} presents finite examples, and
Section~\ref{sec:discussion} states the consequences and limitations.
The appendices give the exact binary certificate and its denominator
bound, full composition formulas, and optional separator refinements.
