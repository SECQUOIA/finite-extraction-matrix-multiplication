\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

The exponent \(\omega\) of matrix multiplication is the least real number
such that, for every \(\eps>0\), two \(n\times n\) complex matrices can
be multiplied with \(O(n^{\omega+\eps})\) arithmetic operations. On
2 October 2026 OpenAI posted a preprint~\citep{OAI26} announcing
\(\omega\le9/4\), well below the bounds near \(2.371\) obtained by the
laser method~\citep{AD25,Dup26}. Its proof works with tensor characters:
functions on tensors that add over direct sums, multiply over tensor
products, do not increase under restriction, and take the value one on
the unit tensor. These are the points of Strassen's asymptotic
spectrum~\citep{Str88}. The preprint derives inequalities for the values
of characters on tensors that encode polynomial multiplication, and
deduces a bound for their values on matrix multiplication tensors. An
existence theorem for characters that detect the rank of matrix
multiplication then turns this into a bound on \(\omega\). The proof
therefore shows that for every \(\eps>0\) some size \(d\) admits a
bilinear algorithm for \(d\times d\) matrices with fewer than
\(d^{9/4+\eps}\) multiplications, but it specifies neither \(d\) nor the
algorithm.

This paper constructs such algorithms. We realize the inequalities that
the preprint's diagonal bound uses by explicit tensor maps, decide how
often to apply each map, and bound the exponent of the resulting finite
construction. Theorem~\ref{thm:main} below gives, for each rational
\(\eps>0\), a terminating procedure that outputs an exact rational
bilinear algorithm, and Theorem~\ref{thm:main-relative} gives a family
of relative schemes (defined below) whose exponents converge to \(9/4\)
at a proved rate.

\subsection{The results}
\label{subsec:intro-results}

Arithmetic operations are additions, subtractions and multiplications of
scalars, including multiplications by fixed constants
(Section~\ref{sec:preliminaries}). A \emph{rational bilinear algorithm}
for \(d_0\times d_0\) matrix multiplication with \(R\) multiplications
consists of \(R\) pairs of linear forms with rational coefficients, one
form in the entries of each input matrix, together with rational linear
combinations of the \(R\) products of these pairs that give the entries
of the product matrix. Equivalently, it is a rank decomposition of the
matrix multiplication tensor with rational coefficients.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For every positive rational \(\eps\), a terminating procedure produces
an integer \(d_0\ge2\) and a rational bilinear algorithm for
\(d_0\times d_0\) matrix multiplication with fewer than
\(d_0^{9/4+\eps}\) multiplications. Recursive use of this algorithm
multiplies \(n\times n\) matrices over every field of characteristic
zero with \(O_\eps(n^{9/4+\eps})\) arithmetic operations.
\end{theorem}

We call such an algorithm a \emph{rational seed} with \emph{seed side}
\(d_0\). In particular \(\omega\le9/4\): for a real \(\eps>0\), apply the
theorem to a rational number in \((0,\eps)\).

The quantitative result is stated for relative schemes. Write \(\MM_d\)
for the tensor of \(d\times d\) matrix multiplication, \(m\MM_d\) for the
direct sum of \(m\) copies of it with independent variables, and
\(T\restr T'\) when \(T'\) is a restriction of \(T\), that is, when
linear maps on the three legs of \(T\) produce \(T'\)
(Section~\ref{sec:preliminaries}). A \emph{relative scheme} is a
restriction \(r_*\MM_A\restr m_*\MM_B\) with positive integers
\(r_*,m_*,A,B\) and \(A<B\). It computes \(m_*\) independent
products of \(B\times B\) matrices from \(r_*\) products of
\(A\times A\) matrices, with all input transformations and output
recombinations given by specified linear maps. We call \(A\) the
\emph{source side} and \(B\) the \emph{target side}. Applying the scheme
recursively, and pooling all subproblems of the same depth into batches
of \(m_*\), multiplies \(n\times n\) matrices with \(O(n^p)\) arithmetic
operations whenever \(p>2\), where
\[
 p=\frac{\log(r_*/m_*)}{\log(B/A)}
\]
is the \emph{pooled exponent} (Definition~\ref{def:relative-scheme} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:batch}). All logarithms are natural. The construction is
indexed by an integer \(a\ge2\), the \emph{target index}: it converts
copies of the tensor \(S^+_{a,a}\) of
Section~\ref{subsec:intro-heuristic} into matrix multiplication tensors,
and larger \(a\) gives exponents closer to \(9/4\).

\begin{theorem}[Quantitative relative construction]\label{thm:main-relative}
For every integer \(a\ge2^{64}\), the construction of
Section~\ref{sec:extraction}, with the parameters of
Theorem~\ref{thm:quantitative}, gives a relative scheme
\(r_*\MM_A\restr m_*\MM_B\) whose coefficients lie in a cyclotomic field
\(\Q(\zeta_\Lambda)\), with \(\Lambda\) computed by the construction.
With \(\ell_a=\lceil\log_2a\rceil\), its pooled exponent and source side
satisfy
\[
 \frac94<p<\frac94+\frac{900}{\log a},
 \qquad
 \log A\le181a^5\ell_a^2.
\]
\end{theorem}

Theorem~\ref{thm:quantitative} proves the two bounds, and
Remark~\ref{rem:field-every-a} identifies the field. The guaranteed bound
is below \(3\) only when \(\log a>1200\)
(Remark~\ref{rem:calibration}), whereas Section~\ref{sec:costs}
evaluates the construction at \(a=12\) with other parameters and obtains
\(2.479<p<2.676\). For a rational \(\eps>0\), the index \(a=2^\ell\) with
\(\ell=\max\{64,\lceil1800/\eps\rceil\}\) gives \(p<9/4+\eps\)
(Corollary~\ref{cor:explicit-allowance}). Theorem~\ref{thm:main} follows
from one scheme of this family: Theorem~\ref{thm:effective} turns it into
an algorithm over \(\Q(\zeta_\Lambda)\) with exponent below
\(9/4+\eps/2\), and Corollary~\ref{cor:seed} unrolls the recursion,
restricts the coefficients to \(\Q\) (Lemma~\ref{lem:descent}), and
stops at the first side \(d_0\) with fewer than \(d_0^{9/4+\eps}\)
multiplications, which exists because of the gap \(\eps/2\).

\subsection{Where the exponent \texorpdfstring{\(9/4\)}{9/4} comes from}
\label{subsec:intro-heuristic}

The following heuristic explains the exponent. No proof depends on it;
Sections~\ref{sec:maps}--\ref{sec:extraction} make each step rigorous
and bound its cost. Say that a tensor \(T\) is \emph{worth} a matrix side \(V\) if, for
large \(n\), explicit maps convert \(T^{\otimes n}\), with the help of a
reusable auxiliary tensor and with losses subexponential in \(n\), into
a matrix multiplication tensor of side \(V^{n-o(n)}\). Worth is
multiplicative under tensor products, \(\MM_d\) is worth \(d\), a tensor
is worth at least as much as every tensor into which it can be
converted, and a bounded factor in the number of copies does not change
worth. If \(T\) has rank \(R\) and is worth \(V\), recursion gives
\(\omega\le(\log R)/\log V\), because \(T^{\otimes n}\) has rank at
most \(R^n\), while \(\MM_d\) has rank at least \(d^\omega\).

The construction works with the \emph{convolution tensor} \(C(r,b)\),
which encodes the multiplication of a polynomial with \(r\) coefficients
by a polynomial with \(b\) coefficients, and with the dot product
\(B_X(d)=x\sum_{\gamma=0}^{d-1}y_\gamma z_\gamma\), whose first leg has a
single variable. The \emph{symmetrized convolution tensor}
\(S^\sigma_{r,b}\) is the tensor product of the three copies
\(C(r,b)^\pi\) whose legs are permuted by the even permutations \(\pi\)
(sign \(\sigma=+\)) or by the odd ones (\(\sigma=-\)); see
Section~\ref{sec:preliminaries}. Let \(V(r,b)\) be the supremum of the
sides that \(S^\sigma_{r,b}\) is worth. It does not depend on
\(\sigma\), because an odd permutation of all three legs exchanges
\(S^+_{r,b}\) and \(S^-_{r,b}\) and maps \(\MM_d\) to an isomorphic
tensor. It is symmetric, \(V(r,b)=V(b,r)\), because
\(S^\sigma_{r,b}\cong S^{-\sigma}_{b,r}\). Since \(C(1,b)=B_X(b)\) and
\(S^\sigma_{1,b}\cong\MM_b\), the boundary values are \(V(1,b)=b\).

Two kinds of tensor relations, \emph{splits} and \emph{triplings},
constrain the other values. Each is built in three steps
(Section~\ref{sec:maps}). First, a restriction of a tensor power
leaves \(M\) \emph{sectors}: blocks with their own variables on the
second and third legs but a common first leg. Second,
the \emph{separator} (Proposition~\ref{prop:separator}) converts \(2M-1\)
copies of this tensor into \(M\) sectors that are independent on all
three legs, and it attaches to each a dot product of dimension \(M\).
Third, the relation is applied to all three factors \(C(r,b)^\pi\) of
\(S^\sigma_{r,b}\), one \emph{orientation} \(\pi\) each. The three
oriented copies of a dot product \(B_X(d)\) have their single-variable
legs in the three different positions, and their product is \(\MM_d\) by
\eqref{eq:coset-dot-products}. So \((2M-1)^3\) source copies yield
\(M^3\) output copies, a ratio below \(8\), each carrying the factor
\(\MM_M\) that the relation gains.

\emph{Split.} Let \(1\le h\le b-1\). In bases adapted to the determinant
filtration of binary forms (Section~\ref{subsec:splits}), the tensor
\(C(r,b)B_X(h+1)\) is block triangular with diagonal blocks
\(C(r,b+h-2j)\), \(0\le j\le h\). Fix nonnegative integers
\(n_0,\ldots,n_h\) with sum \(n\). In the \(n\)-th tensor power, keeping
only the coordinates whose words of block indices have type
\((n_0,\ldots,n_h)\) removes the off-diagonal blocks and leaves
\(M=n!/(n_0!\cdots n_h!)\) sectors. The three orientations of the
auxiliary dot product \(B_X(h+1)\) form the factor \(\MM_{h+1}\) that the
split consumes. Altogether (Corollary~\ref{cor:oriented-relations}),
\((2M-1)^3\) copies of \((S^\sigma_{r,b}\MM_{h+1})^{\otimes n}\) are
converted into \(M^3\) independent copies of
\[
 \prod_{j=0}^h\bigl(S^\sigma_{r,b+h-2j}\bigr)^{\otimes n_j}\otimes\MM_M .
\]
Here \(M=e^{nH(\theta)+O(\log n)}\) for the probability vector
\(\theta_j=n_j/n\), where \(H\) is the Shannon entropy. In terms of
worth, therefore,
\((h+1)V(r,b)\ge e^{H(\theta)}\prod_jV(r,b+h-2j)^{\theta_j}\). The right
side is largest when \(\theta_j\) is proportional to \(V(r,b+h-2j)\),
and its maximum is the sum of these worths. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-split}
 (h+1)V(r,b)\ge\sum_{j=0}^hV(r,b+h-2j).
\end{equation}
For \(h=1\) this is the discrete concavity
\(2V(r,b)\ge V(r,b-1)+V(r,b+1)\). For larger \(h\) it follows from
concavity (Remark~\ref{rem:multiway-scalar}), but wider splits lower the
finite costs.

\emph{Tripling.} The second-leg indices of \(C(r,4r-1)\) fall into three
intervals, of lengths \(r\), \(2r-1\) and \(r\), whose blocks are
\(C(r,r)\), \(C(r,r)^{(23)}\) and \(C(r,r)\) up to coordinate
permutations (Section~\ref{subsec:tripling}). In the \(n\)-th tensor
power, fixing the number of middle blocks leaves \(M\ge3^n/(n+1)\)
sectors, and \((2M-1)^3\) copies of \((S^\sigma_{r,4r-1})^{\otimes n}\)
are converted into \(M^3\) copies of
\((S^\sigma_{r,r})^{\otimes n}\otimes\MM_M\); the middle blocks
contribute \(S^{-\sigma}_{r,r}\cong S^\sigma_{r,r}\). Hence
\(V(r,4r-1)\ge3V(r,r)\).

Normalization \(V(1,b)=b\), symmetry, concavity and tripling are the
axioms of a \emph{profile} (Definition~\ref{def:profile}), except that a
profile satisfies the tripling axiom \(P(r,3b+r-1)\ge3P(r,b)\) at every
column \(b\), whereas the tensor maps supply only the case \(b=r\).
Theorem~\ref{thm:least-profile} shows that every profile \(P\) satisfies
\(P\ge F\) for an explicit \emph{least profile} \(F\), with
\(F(a,a)=s_a(3a-1)/2\), \(s_a=\prod_{h=1}^{a-1}(1+\frac1{3h})\), and
\(\log F(a,a)=\frac43\log a+O(1)\). The diagonal part
\(P(a,a)\ge F(a,a)\) uses only normalization, symmetry, concavity and the
tripling axiom with \(b=r\). The worth \(V\) satisfies all of these, so
\(V(a,a)\ge F(a,a)\). Since \(\R(S^+_{a,a})\le(2a-1)^3\), the heuristic
gives
\[
 \omega\le\tau_a=\frac{3\log(2a-1)}{\log F(a,a)}
 \longrightarrow\frac{3}{4/3}=\frac94 .
\]
We call \(\tau_a\) the \emph{profile exponent}; it exceeds \(9/4\)
because \(F(a,a)<(2a-1)^{4/3}\) for \(a\ge2\). Since \(F\) is itself a
profile, these inequalities cannot show that \(S^+_{a,a}\) is worth more
than \(F(a,a)\), so \(9/4\) is the limit of this approach
(Remark~\ref{rem:profile-limit}).

\subsection{From the heuristic to a finite algorithm}
\label{subsec:intro-finite}

A finite construction must decide how many times to apply each
relation, so that every intermediate convolution factor produced by one
relation is consumed by another. The only net effect on these factors
should be to consume copies of \(S^+_{a,a}\) and to produce copies of
\(\MM_2\cong S^\sigma_{1,2}\); the auxiliary matrix tensors are accounted
for separately. We obtain the frequencies from a random walk in each
row, the \emph{row walk} of Section~\ref{sec:flows}. For
\(r=2,\ldots,a\), the \emph{states} \(x\in\{0,\ldots,3r\}\) of row \(r\)
stand for the factors \(S^{\sigma_r}_{r,r-1+x}\), with column
\(b=r-1+x\) and sign \(\sigma_r=(-1)^{a-r}\). At an interior state
\(x\), a split of width \(h_{r,x}=\min(x,3r-x)\) moves to a
\emph{child} \(y\in\{x-h_{r,x},x-h_{r,x}+2,\ldots,x+h_{r,x}\}\) with
probability proportional to \(F(r,r-1+y)\). Since \(F\) is affine on the
columns \(r-1,\ldots,4r-1\) of row \(r\), which contain all children, it
satisfies \eqref{eq:intro-split} with equality there, and these
probabilities attain the maximum in the split step with \(V\) replaced
by \(F\). At state \(3r\) (column \(4r-1\)) a tripling returns to state
\(1\) (column \(r\)). State \(0\) is column \(r-1\); by the symmetry
\(S^\sigma_{r,r-1}\cong S^{-\sigma}_{r-1,r}\), it is state \(2\) of row
\(r-1\), the \emph{injection state} at which the walk of that row
starts. This identification is why the sign alternates between rows
(Section~\ref{subsec:orientations}). The walk of row \(a\) starts at
state \(1\), the factor \(S^+_{a,a}\), and the walk of row \(2\) ends at
column \(1\), the factor \(S^{\sigma_2}_{2,1}\cong\MM_2\).

The expected numbers of visits, called \emph{occupations}, are
frequencies at which every intermediate factor is produced exactly as
often as it is consumed (Proposition~\ref{prop:occupations}). Since the
probabilities are tight for \(F\), the potential \(\log F\) telescopes
along the walk. Per injected copy of \(S^+_{a,a}\), the expected total
gain is exactly \(\log F(a,a)\), where a split gains the entropy of its
child distribution minus the logarithm of its auxiliary dimension
\(h_{r,x}+1\), a tripling gains \(\log3\), and the final \(\MM_2\)
contributes \(\log2\). Because every split has the largest width that
keeps its children in the row, the expected number of splits in a row
and the sum of the logarithms of their auxiliary dimensions are bounded
independently of the row, and both totals over all rows are at most
\(\Gamma_a=105+180(a-2)\) (Proposition~\ref{prop:occupations}). These
totals control the finite costs: the
auxiliary dimensions multiply into the source side, and the number of
splits sets the size of the catalyst described below.

The occupations are rationals whose denominators can be large; for the
exact binary certificate of Appendix~\ref{sec:exact-certificate}, which
uses splits of width one only, the scale needed to clear them grows at
least exponentially in \(a\). Instead, we multiply the
occupations by a \emph{rounding scale} \(K\) and round the resulting
transition counts to integers so that every balance stays exact
(Section~\ref{sec:rounding}). The telescoping identity then holds up to
a \emph{rounding loss} \(\mathcal L\le4J_a\), where \(J_a=O(a^3)\), the
number of transition coordinates, counts the possible split transitions
\(x\to y\) from interior states in all rows. This bound holds for every \(K\), even when some
occupations are tiny or zero (Proposition~\ref{prop:entropy-loss}).

Section~\ref{sec:extraction} applies every relation its rounded number
of times, inside tensor powers of fixed type at a \emph{type scale}
\(N\), separates the sectors and takes the three orientations. The
factors common to both sides form a \emph{catalyst} \(\Cat_N\), and the
result is one degeneration, the \emph{catalytic degeneration}
(Proposition~\ref{prop:catalytic}): it turns \(L\) copies of
\(\Cat_N(S^+_{a,a})^{\otimes NK}\MM_{A_1}\) into \(M^3\) copies of
\(\Cat_N\MM_{B_1}\). Here \(\MM_{A_1}\) collects the auxiliary factors of
the splits, \(M\) is the product of the sector counts,
\(B_1=2^{NK}M\) collects the \(NK\) final factors \(\MM_2\) and the
separator auxiliaries, and \(L\) counts the separator source copies.
Interpolation at \(k\)-th tensor powers with \(E\) points turns this
degeneration into a restriction (Lemma~\ref{lem:exactification}). Using
the restriction \(j\) times with one copy of the catalyst, supplied once
from a rank decomposition of \(\Cat_N^{\otimes k}\) with \(\kappa_N^k\)
terms, gives a restriction \(r_*\MM_A\restr m_*\MM_B\) with
\(A=A_1^{kj}\) and \(B=B_1^{kj}\) (Proposition~\ref{prop:relative}).
With \(\Delta_N=\log(B_1/A_1)\), it is a relative scheme exactly when
\(\Delta_N>0\), which is not automatic for small \(K\). In that case
Lemma~\ref{lem:batch} turns it into an algorithm, and its pooled
exponent \eqref{eq:pooled-exponent} is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-exponent}
 p=\frac{3NK\log(2a-1)+\log(L/M^3)+(\log E)/k
        +(\log\kappa_N)/j}{\Delta_N}.
\end{equation}
The main term \(3NK\log(2a-1)\) pays for the rank of the \(NK\) source
copies of \(S^+_{a,a}\). Without the overheads of
Table~\ref{tab:overheads}, \(\Delta_N\) would equal \(NK\log F(a,a)\) and
\(p\) would equal \(\tau_a\); all overheads are nonnegative, so
\(p\ge\tau_a\). The family of Theorem~\ref{thm:main-relative} uses
\(K=a^3\ell_a\), \(N=k=1\) and \(j=a\ell_a\), for which every overhead
is at most a constant multiple of \(K\) while the main terms are of
order \(K\log a\); this gives the rate \(900/\log a\)
(Section~\ref{sec:rate}).

\subsection{Relation to the OpenAI preprint}
\label{subsec:intro-oai}

The bound \(\omega\le9/4\) and the scalar framework behind it are due to
\citet{OAI26}. That preprint introduced the profile axioms (its
equations (4.5), (4.6) and (4.9)) and proved the diagonal growth bound
\(P(a,a)\ge a^{4/3}\) (its Lemma~5.1); the diagonal part of
Theorem~\ref{thm:least-profile}, \(P(a,a)\ge F(a,a)\), follows from the
same argument. It also gave the determinant filtration step for width
one (its Lemma~4.1), the partition into three intervals behind our
tripling (its Lemma~4.2), and the shared-leg separator with \(5M\)
source copies (its Proposition~3.1). It applied these constructions to
tensor characters and concluded through the existence of detecting
characters (its Lemma~2.2).

The present paper uses none of the character theory and reproves the
constructions it needs in the following forms. The local relations
become type restrictions instead of degenerations that scale coordinates
by powers of \(t\) (weight degenerations), so that the separator is the
only degeneration. The tripling restriction fixes only the number of
middle blocks, so it works for every tripling count without a
divisibility condition. The splits have arbitrary width \(h\), with
auxiliary dimension \(h+1\). The separator uses \(2M-1\) source copies
instead of \(5M\). On the scalar side, we determine the least profile
off the diagonal as well, show that \(F\) satisfies the axioms, so that
the bound \(P\ge F\) is attained, and prove the upper bound
\(F(m,n)\le(m+n-1)^{4/3}\), which is strict for \(m=n>1\). The finite
analysis is new: the occupations of the row walk and their uniform
bounds, the integer rounding that keeps every balance exact and its
uniform bound on the rounding loss, the bounds on all overheads
(Table~\ref{tab:overheads}), the rate of
Theorem~\ref{thm:main-relative}, and the rational seeds of
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

The termination statement in Theorem~\ref{thm:main} would not by itself
require a construction. As \citet[Remark~5.2]{OAI26} observes, the
equations of an exact rank decomposition have rational coefficients, so
a complex solution implies an algebraic one; once \(\omega\le9/4\) is
known, an exhaustive search over algebraic candidates would therefore
also terminate for each fixed \(\eps\). What the present construction
adds is that the algorithm is generated by prescribed maps whose finite
resources are bounded, as in Theorem~\ref{thm:main-relative}.

\subsection{Related work}
\label{subsec:intro-related}

Strassen's algorithm showed that recursion on a fixed bilinear identity
gives an exponent below three~\citep{Str69}. Approximate bilinear
algorithms and interpolation give the standard passage from
degenerations to exact algorithms~\citep{Bini80}, and Sch\"onhage's
asymptotic sum inequality bounds \(\omega\) through simultaneous
independent matrix products~\citep{Sch81}. \citet[p.~24 and
Theorem~7]{Sto10} gives an accessible account of the passage from
approximate to exact algorithms by coefficient extraction and of the sum
inequality. Lemma~\ref{lem:exactification} is a root-of-unity form of
interpolation, and Lemma~\ref{lem:batch} handles simultaneous products
directly for our relative schemes.

Strassen's laser method~\citep{Str86,Str87} and the construction of
Coppersmith and Winograd~\citep{CW90} extract independent matrix
products from tensor powers, with restrictions to words of a fixed type,
entropy estimates for their number, and products over permuted legs as
standard tools; \citet[Appendix~A of the full version]{LG14} gives a
detailed example. \citet{AW21} refine the
extraction of independent components, \citet{AD25} analyze powers of
the Coppersmith--Winograd tensor asymmetrically and prove
\(\omega<2.371339\), and \citet{Dup26} optimize that analysis
numerically and report \(\omega<2.371177\).

Strassen's asymptotic spectrum compares tensor powers through
characters~\citep{Str88,CVZ}, and relative bilinear
complexity~\citep{Str87} is the setting of our relative schemes. Catalytic comparisons, which
keep an auxiliary tensor on both sides of a reduction, are discussed in
\citet[Section~3.2]{CVZ21}, which also defines the cyclic
symmetrization \(T\otimes T^{(123)}\otimes T^{(132)}\) that underlies
\(S^+_{r,b}\).

The determinant filtration is a case of the universal Clebsch--Gordan
filtration of \citet[Lemma~2.5, Theorem~2.8, Corollary~2.9 and
Corollary~2.9]{Maakestad23}; we identify the multiplication induced on
its quotients and the restrictions obtained by fixing types. Integer
circulations between integral bounds exist by total
unimodularity~\citep[Theorem~2]{HK56}, and rounding through a return
edge appears in \citet[Sections~2.2--3.1]{KangPayor15}.
\citet[Section~IV.D, Eq.~(42)]{BoechererGeiger16} bound the relative
entropy of the best approximation of a distribution by a type with a
fixed total; our rounding may change these totals, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:entropy-loss} proves the bound that we need. The
row walk uses the fundamental matrix of an absorbing chain
\citep[Theorem~11.4]{GrinsteadSnell97} and a Doob transform
\citep[Section~17.6.1]{LevinPeres17}.

\subsection{Scope and organization}
\label{subsec:intro-scope}

All results concern exact arithmetic with a fixed allowance \(\eps\):
the algorithm of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} depends on \(\eps\) but not on
the input size. The bound on \(A\) in Theorem~\ref{thm:main-relative}
concerns the source side of the relative scheme; we give no bound on
the seed side \(d_0\), on the sizes of the coefficients, or on the
preprocessing time. The sizes are astronomically large: even the example
with \(a=12\) in Section~\ref{sec:costs} has a source side \(A\) with
about \(2.3\times10^{11}\) decimal digits. Every member of the family has
exponent strictly above \(9/4\), so the construction gives no algorithm
with \(O(n^{9/4})\) operations. We make no claim about numerical
stability.

Sections~\ref{sec:preliminaries}--\ref{sec:rate} contain the proofs:
general tools
(Section~\ref{sec:preliminaries}), the least profile
(Section~\ref{sec:profile}), the splits, triplings and separator
