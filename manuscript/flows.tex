\section{Rational occupation flows with bounded auxiliary cost}
\label{sec:flows}

We now choose how often to use each multiway relation. The choice must
conserve all convolution factors and keep the product of the auxiliary
dimensions small. Both requirements can be expressed through the
occupation counts of a finite absorbing walk. Expected visits are obtained
from the fundamental matrix of an absorbing chain
\citep[Theorem~11.4]{GrinsteadSnell97}; the change of probabilities below
is a Doob transform by a positive harmonic function
\citep[Section~17.6.1]{LevinPeres17}. The bounds on this particular walk
will be proved directly. The probabilities below
are prescribed by the least profile; no optimization oracle is needed.

Fix a target index \(a\ge2\). In row \(2\le r\le a\), put
\[
 n_r=3r,\qquad x=b-r+1,\qquad
 f_r(x)=\frac{3(r-1)}2+x\quad(0\le x\le n_r).
\]
The profile formula extends to the left boundary:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:affine-row}
 F(r,r-1+x)=s_r f_r(x)\qquad(0\le x\le n_r).
\end{equation}
For \(x\ge1\), this is \eqref{eq:profile-formula}. At \(x=0\),
symmetry and \(s_r/s_{r-1}=(3r-2)/(3r-3)\) give
\(F(r,r-1)=s_{r-1}(3r-2)/2=3(r-1)s_r/2\).

For an interior state \(1\le x<n_r\), define
\begin{align}
 h_{r,x}&=\min(x,n_r-x),&d_{r,x}&=h_{r,x}+1,\notag\\
 \mathcal Y_{r,x}&=\{x-h_{r,x},x-h_{r,x}+2,\ldots,x+h_{r,x}\},
 &q_{r,x,y}&=\frac{f_r(y)}{d_{r,x}f_r(x)}
 \quad(y\in\mathcal Y_{r,x}).\label{eq:multiway-probabilities}
\end{align}
The probabilities are positive rationals and sum to one, since the
average of the children is \(x\) and \(f_r\) is affine. The determinant
relation of Lemma~\ref{lem:multiway} uses auxiliary dimension
\(d_{r,x}\) and these child columns. Its filtration index corresponding
to child \(y\) is \((x+h_{r,x}-y)/2\).
Its hypothesis holds: \(h_{r,x}\le x\) implies
\(b=r-1+x\ge h_{r,x}+1\). Thus every child remains in the row interval
\([r-1,4r-1]\).

State zero absorbs. At state \(n_r\), the walk returns deterministically
to state one, representing the tripling relation from column \(4r-1\)
to column \(r\). Inject one unit at
\[
 i_r=\begin{cases}1,&r=a,\\2,&r<a.\end{cases}
\]
Let \(e_0,\ldots,e_{n_r}\) be the standard coordinate vectors for the
states of this row.

\begin{proposition}[Rational occupation flows]\label{prop:occupations}
The walk is absorbed at zero in finite expected time, from every state.
Its expected interior visit counts \(\lambda_{r,x}\) and expected reset
count \(\mu_r\) are nonnegative rationals, determined by a nonsingular
finite rational linear system. They satisfy the exact row identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:row-flow}
 \sum_{x=1}^{n_r-1}\lambda_{r,x}
 \left(e_x-\sum_{y\in\mathcal Y_{r,x}}q_{r,x,y}e_y\right)
 +\mu_r(e_{n_r}-e_1)=e_{i_r}-e_0.
\end{equation}
The reset counts are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reset-weights}
 \mu_r=
 \begin{cases}
  \dfrac1{r-1},&r=a,\\[4pt]
  \dfrac{2(3r-1)}{(r-1)(3r+1)},&r<a.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
In particular, \(\mu_r\le2\). Each of the two row sums
\(\sum_x\lambda_{r,x}\) and
\(\sum_x\lambda_{r,x}\log d_{r,x}\) is less than \(105\) in the
final row and less than \(180\) in a lower row. Consequently, with
\(C_a=105+180(a-2)\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:occupation-bounds}
 \sum_{r=2}^a\sum_{x=1}^{n_r-1}\lambda_{r,x}\le C_a,
 \qquad
 \sum_{r=2}^a\sum_{x=1}^{n_r-1}
       \lambda_{r,x}\log d_{r,x}\le C_a.
\end{equation}
In particular, every action satisfies
\(\lambda_{r,x}\log d_{r,x}\le180\).
Unreachable states may have zero occupation.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Fix a row and suppress its subscript. First consider the walk that
chooses each child in \(\mathcal Y_x\) with equal probability
\(1/(h(x)+1)\), and stops at either endpoint. Write \(\tau\) for its
first endpoint visit. This walk is a martingale. The nonnegative function
\[
 g(x)=\sqrt{x}+\sqrt{n-x}-\sqrt n,
 \qquad \gamma=1-\frac{2\sqrt2}{3}>0
\]
has the drift bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:square-root-drift}
 g(x)-\mathbb E_0[g(X_{t+1})\mid X_t=x]
 \ge\gamma\sqrt{h(x)}\qquad(1\le x<n).
\end{equation}
Here \(\mathbb E_0\) denotes expectation for the uniform walk. To prove
the bound when \(x\le n/2\), its children are \(0,2,\ldots,2x\).
Concavity of the square root, integrated on each unit interval, gives
\[
 \sum_{k=0}^{x}\sqrt{k}
 \le\frac23x^{3/2}+\frac12\sqrt{x}.
\]
It follows that
\(\mathbb E_0\sqrt{X_{t+1}}\le(2\sqrt2/3)\sqrt{x}\).
Jensen's inequality gives
\(\mathbb E_0\sqrt{n-X_{t+1}}\le\sqrt{n-x}\).
These two inequalities prove \eqref{eq:square-root-drift}; reflection
about \(n/2\) proves the other half.

Stop this estimate at \(\tau\wedge T\), where \(T\) is a positive
integer. Summing the conditional drift and using \(g\ge0\) gives
\[
 \gamma\,\mathbb E_{0,i}
 \sum_{t<\tau\wedge T}\sqrt{h(X_t)}\le g(i).
\]
Monotone convergence therefore proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uniform-excursion-bound}
 \mathbb E_{0,i}\sum_{t<\tau}\sqrt{h(X_t)}
 \le\frac{g(i)}\gamma\le\frac{\sqrt{i}}\gamma.
\end{equation}
Since \(h(x)\ge1\) in the interior, this also proves finite expected
absorption, without assuming it in the stopping argument. The inequality
\(\log(1+h)\le\sqrt h\) for \(h\ge1\) follows because
\(\sqrt h-\log(1+h)\) is positive at one and has nonnegative derivative.
Thus
\eqref{eq:uniform-excursion-bound} bounds both the number of steps and
the sum of \(\log(h+1)\) during an excursion.

Now use the probabilities \(q\), still stopping at the first endpoint.
They are the transform of the uniform probabilities by the positive
harmonic function \(f\). For a path from \(i\) to \(x_t\), the
likelihood ratio telescopes to \(f(x_t)/f(i)\), including when the last
state is an endpoint. For every interior entry \(i\ge1\),
\[
 \frac{f(x_t)}{f(i)}\le\frac{f(n)}{f(i)}
 \le\frac{f(n)}{f(1)}=3.
\]
Applying this bound at each time to nonnegative charges gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:harmonic-excursion-bound}
 C_i\le\frac{3\sqrt i}\gamma,
\end{equation}
where \(C_i\) denotes either the expected number of interior visits or
the expected sum of \(\log(h+1)\) in one \(q\)-excursion.
In particular, these excursions also end in finite expected time.

The uniform walk exits at \(n\) with probability \(i/n\): apply the
martingale identity at \(\tau\wedge T\), then use bounded convergence.
The endpoint likelihood ratio therefore gives the \(q\)-exit probability
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exit-probability}
 p_i=\frac{i f(n)}{n f(i)},\qquad
 p_1=\frac1r,\qquad
 p_2=\frac{2(3r-1)}{r(3r+1)}<\frac2r.
\end{equation}
After an exit at \(n\), the reset starts a new excursion at one.
For either of the charges in \eqref{eq:harmonic-excursion-bound}, the
total expectation from entry \(i\) is consequently
\[
 C_i+\frac{p_i C_1}{1-p_1}.
\]
For entry one this is at most
\[
 \frac6\gamma=54+36\sqrt2<105.
\]
For entry two it is at most
\[
 \frac{3(\sqrt2+2)}\gamma=90+63\sqrt2<180.
\]
The expected number of resets is \(p_i/(1-p_1)\), which gives
\eqref{eq:reset-weights}. The same formulas give finite expectations
from every other interior entry; from state \(n\), there is one initial
reset followed by entry at one. Thus absorption has finite expected time
from every state of the full walk.

Let \(P_{uv}\) be its one-step transition probability from \(u\) to
\(v\), for \(u,v\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\). This transient matrix includes
the reset state \(n\). Finite expected
absorption implies that \(\sum_{t\ge0}P^t\) has finite entries and is
the inverse of \(I-P\). The occupation vector is therefore the unique
solution of
\[
 (I-P^{\mathsf T})
 (\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_{n-1},\mu)^{\mathsf T}
 =(\mathbf1_{\{v=i\}})_{v=1}^{n}.
\]
The matrix and right-hand side are rational, so this solution is rational
and can be obtained by exact Gaussian elimination. To recover the
coordinate at zero as well, sum the successive differences
\(e_{X_t}-e_{X_{t+1}}\) up to absorption and take expectations. Their
sum is \(e_i-e_0\), proving \eqref{eq:row-flow}. The row estimates give
\eqref{eq:occupation-bounds}, and nonnegativity gives the individual
action bound.
\end{proof}

The bound \eqref{eq:occupation-bounds} controls two different costs:
the total relation count before integer scaling, and the logarithm of
the source auxiliary dimension. Both grow at most linearly with the
number of rows. There are nevertheless up to \(3r-1\) interior actions
in row \(r\), each with up to \(\lfloor3r/2\rfloor+1\) children.
Thus the whole construction has \(O(a^2)\) possible actions and
\(O(a^3)\) child counts. Their rounding and finite-type costs must also
be controlled. The next section does this while preserving the row
identities exactly, rather than clearing the occupation denominators.
