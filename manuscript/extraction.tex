\section{The counted finite construction}
\label{sec:extraction}

Fix a profile index \(a\ge2\) and a positive integer rounding scale
\(K\). Use the integer counts of Proposition~\ref{prop:rounded-ledger}.
Let \(\mathcal I_c\) index the actions with \(w_i>0\), and let
\(\mathcal I_t\) index the resets with \(z_r>0\). Put
\(\mathcal J=\mathcal I_c\sqcup\mathcal I_t\) and \(I=|\mathcal J|\).
An action \(i=(r,x)\) has auxiliary dimension \(d_i=d_{r,x}\),
child counts \(n_{i,y}\), and empirical distribution
\(p_{i,y}=n_{i,y}/w_i\). Counts for children are assigned to the
filtration index \((x+h_{r,x}-y)/2\).

The remaining parameters have distinct roles. A type scale \(N\)
reduces the relative loss in counting sector words. A tensor power
\(k\) amortizes interpolation, and the number \(j\) of exact reuses
amortizes the catalyst. Unlike exact normalization of the binary
certificate, the choice of \(K\) is unrestricted.

At scale \(N\), define the sector counts
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sector-counts}
 M_i=\frac{(Nw_i)!}{\prod_y(Nn_{i,y})!}\quad(i\in\mathcal I_c),
 \qquad
 M_r=\binom{Nz_r}{m_r}2^{Nz_r-m_r},\quad
 m_r=\left\lfloor\frac{Nz_r+1}{3}\right\rfloor
 \quad(r\in\mathcal I_t).
\end{equation}
Zero child counts contribute \(0!=1\). Put
\begin{align}
 M&=\prod_{i\in\mathcal J}M_i,&
 S&=\prod_{i\in\mathcal J}(2M_i-1)^3,&
 H&=M^3,\label{eq:copy-counts}\\
 D&=3\sum_{i\in\mathcal J}(M_i-1)^2,&
 u&=\prod_{i\in\mathcal I_c}d_i^{Nw_i},&
 v&=2^{NK}M.\label{eq:dimensions}
\end{align}
Here \(S\) is an integer multiplicity; the tensors
\(S^\sigma_{r,b}\) always carry indices.
Let \(\Cat_N\) be the catalyst in \eqref{eq:rounded-catalyst}.

\begin{proposition}[Counted catalytic degeneration]\label{prop:catalytic}
The specified maps give a polynomial degeneration of degree at most \(D\):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:catalytic-degeneration}
 S\Cat_N(S^+_{a,a})^{\otimes NK}\MM_u
 \degen H\Cat_N\MM_v.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For each action use the fixed-type restriction of
Lemma~\ref{lem:multiway}, and for each reset use the partial-middle
restriction of Lemma~\ref{lem:tripling}. In row \(r\), take the three
permutations in the coset with sign \(\sigma_r=(-1)^{a-r}\).
For each such permutation apply the separator of
Proposition~\ref{prop:separator}, with auxiliary dimension \(M_i\).
It consumes \(2M_i-1\) source copies and produces \(M_i\)
independent copies of the sector tensor, each with a dot product of
dimension \(M_i\).

Write the type restriction as \(\mathsf T_i\), the separator map as
\(\mathsf S_i(t)\), and the sector-specific coordinate maps after
separation as \(\mathsf R_i\). The complete map for a permuted relation is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:complete-relation-map}
 \Psi_{i,\pi}(t)=\mathsf R_i^\pi\circ\mathsf S_i(t)^\pi\circ
 \left(\bigoplus_{\ell=0}^{2M_i-2}\mathsf T_i^\pi\right).
\end{equation}
For a reset, group its three permuted factors before using the diagonal
identity in \eqref{eq:cyclic-identities}. This identifies every separated
sector with the same power of \(S^{\sigma_r}_{r,r}\), regardless of
its outer labels. Appendix~\ref{sec:composed-maps} specifies these maps
on the whole target spaces, including positive-degree terms.

Tensoring all relation maps gives the copy counts \(S\) and \(H\).
The signed ledger \eqref{eq:rounded-ledger} identifies the convolution
factors on the two sides with
\(\Cat_N(S^+_{a,a})^{\otimes NK}\) and
\(\Cat_N\MM_{2^{NK}}\), respectively. Each action contributes
three dot products of dimension \(d_i^{Nw_i}\), one in each leg.
Their product is \(\MM_u\). The separator outputs similarly contribute
\(\MM_M\), giving \(\MM_v\).
Use one common parameter for all separators. Their degrees add by
Lemma~\ref{lem:composition}, giving \(D\). All preceding type maps and
subsequent coordinate maps are independent of that parameter.
\end{proof}

The estimate \(1\le S/H<2^{3I}\) bounds a cost. Neither direct-sum
multiplicity has been canceled.

\subsection{Exactification and repeated use of one catalyst}

Define the explicit rank bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kappa}
 \kappa_N=\prod_{\sigma,r,b}(r+b-1)^{3Nc_{\sigma,r,b}}
 \ge\R(\Cat_N).
\end{equation}
It tensors the evaluation decompositions \eqref{eq:convolution-rank}.
The boundary factor \(\MM_2\) has zero catalyst multiplicity, so this
product uses only the remaining convolution factors.

\begin{proposition}[An exact relative multiplication scheme]
\label{prop:relative}
For positive integers \(k,j\), put \(E=kD+1\),
\(A=u^{kj}\), \(B=v^{kj}\), and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:relative-counts}
 r_* =\kappa_N^k E^j S^{kj}(2a-1)^{3NKkj},
 \qquad m_* =H^{kj}.
\end{equation}
There is a specified exact restriction \(r_*\MM_A\restr m_*\MM_B\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:exactification} to the \(k\)-th power of
\eqref{eq:catalytic-degeneration}. Replace each source copy of
\(S^+_{a,a}\) by its rank-\((2a-1)^3\) evaluation decomposition.
Writing \(\alpha=ES^k(2a-1)^{3NKk}\) and \(\beta=H^k\), we obtain
\[
 \alpha\Cat_N^{\otimes k}\MM_{u^k}
 \restr\beta\Cat_N^{\otimes k}\MM_{v^k}.
\]
Apply this exact restriction \(j\) times while retaining the same catalyst:
\[
 \alpha^j\Cat_N^{\otimes k}\MM_{u^{kj}}
 \restr\beta^j\Cat_N^{\otimes k}\MM_{v^{kj}}.
\]
For the induction, tensor the preceding restriction with
\(\alpha\MM_{u^k}\), then apply the one-step restriction to the
retained catalyst in each output summand. The matrix factors combine by
\eqref{eq:matrix-products}. The catalyst exponent stays \(k\).
Supply its source using \(\kappa_N^k\) rank-one tensors and restrict
the output catalyst to a coefficient equal to one. Such a coefficient
is available by choosing a monomial in every convolution factor.
This proves \eqref{eq:relative-counts}.
\end{proof}

Exactification precedes reuse. Iterating the degeneration itself would
require a separate bound on the accumulated approximation degree.

\subsection{Pooled recursion with padding}

\begin{lemma}[Relative schemes give recursive algorithms]\label{lem:batch}
Suppose an explicit restriction \(r\MM_A\restr m\MM_B\) is given,
with positive integers \(B>A\). Set
\[
 q=r/m,\qquad p=\frac{\log q}{\log(B/A)}.
\]
Then \(p\ge2\). The restriction yields a fixed arithmetic algorithm
with cost \(O(n^p)\) if \(p>2\), and \(O(n^2\log n)\) if \(p=2\).
Every scalar product is a product of a linear form in the left input
entries and a linear form in the right input entries.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The first-leg flattenings have ranks \(rA^2\) and \(mB^2\).
Rank cannot increase under restriction, so \(q\ge(B/A)^2>1\).

Pool all products at a given recursion depth. If there are \(v_\ell\)
products of side \(n_\ell\), group them in batches of \(m\), filling
the last batch with zero products. Pad each input to side
\(B\lceil n_\ell/B\rceil\), divide it into a \(B\times B\) array of blocks of side
\(\lceil n_\ell/B\rceil\), and apply the restriction. This gives
\[
 v_{\ell+1}=r\lceil v_\ell/m\rceil\le qv_\ell+r,
 \qquad n_{\ell+1}=A\lceil n_\ell/B\rceil.
\]
The scalar identity remains valid on matrix blocks: its products place a
left-input factor before a right-input factor, and its coefficients are
central scalars. Starting with \(v_0=1\), the count obeys
\(v_\ell\le q^\ell(1+r/(q-1))\).
There is a constant \(C\), depending only on the scheme, for which
all transformations at this level take at most \(Cv_\ell n_\ell^2\)
operations. To justify pooling across different parents, perform the
input transformations in increasing depth, store their linear output
maps, and perform the output combinations in reverse depth after computing
the leaves. Products at the same depth can then be grouped together
without changing any algebraic dependency.

Put \(\rho=A/B\) and \(c=A/(1-\rho)\). Since
\(n_{\ell+1}\le\rho n_\ell+A\),
\(n_\ell\le c+\rho^\ell(n-c)\).
Use classical multiplication at the fixed leaf threshold
\(n_* =\lceil c\rceil+1\). There are at most
\(\log_{1/\rho}n+O(1)\) levels. The total work is bounded by a
constant times the sum of
\(q^\ell(1+\rho^{2\ell}n^2)\) over these levels, including leaves.
As \(q\rho^2\ge1\), this gives the two asserted bounds.
At every stage the input transformations are separately linear in the
original left and right entries. This remains true at the classical
leaves and proves the last assertion.
\end{proof}

For the relative scheme above, write \(\Delta_N=\log(v/u)\).
Whenever \(\Delta_N>0\), its pooled exponent is exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pooled-exponent}
 p=\frac{3NK\log(2a-1)+\log(S/H)+\log E/k+\log\kappa_N/j}
 {\Delta_N}.
\end{equation}
For comparison with saved finite cost certificates, also define the
slightly larger bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-exponent}
 \Phi=\frac{3NK\log(2a-1)+\log(S/H)+\log E/k
              +\log\kappa_N/j+\log2/(kj)}{\Delta_N}.
\end{equation}
To see the origin of the extra term, let \(\operatorname{cost}(n)\) be the cost of
one batch of \(m_*\) products when each parent's children are grouped
separately. With \(b_0=\lceil r_*/m_*\rceil\), this schedule obeys
\[
 \operatorname{cost}(n)\le b_0\operatorname{cost}\bigl(A\lceil n/B\rceil\bigr)+O(n^2).
\]
The dimension estimate in Lemma~\ref{lem:batch} gives exponent
\(\log b_0/\log(B/A)\), bounded by \(\Phi\) since
\(b_0\le2r_*/m_*\). Pooling removes this last ceiling charge. Both expressions retain the actual
copy, interpolation, and catalyst costs. The source \(\MM_A\) is
the recursive operation; no fast decomposition of it has been assumed.

\subsection{Type losses and finite cost bounds}

\begin{lemma}[A quantitative type bound]\label{lem:types}
Let \(n_1+\cdots+n_s=n\), with all \(n_i>0\), and put \(p_i=n_i/n\).
Then
\[
 \frac{\exp(nH(p))}{(n+1)^{s-1}}
 \le\frac{n!}{\prod_i n_i!}\le\exp(nH(p)),
 \qquad H(p)=-\sum_i p_i\log p_i.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The multinomial probability of the type \((n_i)\) is its coefficient
times \(\exp(-nH(p))\), and is at most one. It is also a mode:
the factor \(n_i^h/h!\) is maximized at \(h=n_i\) or \(h=n_i-1\),
as follows by comparing consecutive terms. The joint probability, up to
a common factor, is the product of these factors. There are at most
\((n+1)^{s-1}\) types, so a mode has probability at least their reciprocal.
\end{proof}

For an action \(i\), let \(s_i=|\{y:n_{i,y}>0\}|\) and
\(H_i=w_iH(p_i)\). For a reset put \(H_r=z_r\log3\).
Define
\begin{align}
 \mathcal T_N&=\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_c}(s_i-1)\log(Nw_i+1)
             +\sum_{r\in\mathcal I_t}\log(Nz_r+1),\notag\\
 B_N&=N\max_{i\in\mathcal J}H_i.\label{eq:type-penalty}
\end{align}
Lemma~\ref{lem:types} and the modal estimate for partial-middle
tripling give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:type-error}
 NG-\mathcal T_N\le\Delta_N\le NG,
 \qquad G=K\log F(a,a)-\mathcal L,
 \qquad 0\le\mathcal L\le4J_a.
\end{equation}
Only the number of used children enters the type penalty. The source
auxiliary dimension remains \(d_i\), including when some children
have count zero.

The other charges have the elementary bounds
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-cost-bounds}
 \begin{split}
 0&\le\log(S/H)<3I\log2,\\
 \log E&\le\log(3kI+1)+2B_N,\\
 \log\kappa_N&\le3N(U+T-K)\log(5a).
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
Indeed \(M_i\le\exp(NH_i)\), and
\(E=1+3k\sum_i(M_i-1)^2\le(3kI+1)\exp(2B_N)\).
Every rank base \(r+b-1\) is less than \(5a\); the catalyst count
is \(U+T-K\). These bounds do not require expanding the sector words
or writing out any tensor coefficients.

The uncharged profile expression
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tau}
 \tau_a=\frac{3\log(2a-1)}{\log F(a,a)}
 \longrightarrow\frac94
\end{equation}
remains useful for comparison. Since \(\Delta_N\le NK\log F(a,a)\)
and all numerator charges in \eqref{eq:pooled-exponent} are nonnegative,
every scheme here with \(B>A\) has \(p\ge\tau_a>9/4\).
The finite construction therefore approaches the endpoint from above.

\subsection{An effective algorithm over a specified field}

\begin{theorem}[Effective finite extraction]\label{thm:effective}
There is a terminating procedure that, given a positive rational
\(\eps\), specifies a finite arithmetic algorithm for multiplying
arbitrary complex \(n\times n\) matrices in
\(O_\eps(n^{9/4+\eps})\) operations. All coefficients lie in an
explicitly specified cyclotomic field. The procedure uses only finite
integer and rational computations, coordinate permutations, tensor
products, and the maps proved above.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose \(a,K,N,k,j\) by Corollary~\ref{cor:explicit-allowance} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:quantitative}. Compute the rational occupations by their
finite linear systems, round their circulations using the integral
max-flow procedure in Lemma~\ref{lem:circulation-rounding}, and form the
integer counts and catalyst. Proposition~\ref{prop:relative} gives the
exact relative scheme. Theorem~\ref{thm:quantitative} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:batch} give an exponent strictly below \(9/4+\eps\).
The possible quadratic-logarithmic term is also within this allowance.

For a deterministic specification, order states, edges, tensor positions,
and sector words lexicographically, and use the first available augmenting
path at each max-flow step. Use the displayed determinant bases, interval
sectors, Fourier substitutions, evaluation decompositions, and root
averages. Separate sectors before applying their individual coordinate
maps. Compose the maps in the order in the proofs of
Propositions~\ref{prop:catalytic} and \ref{prop:relative}.
Every root of unity has an explicitly known finite order. One may take
\[
 L_* =\lcm\bigl(E,\,2a-1,\,
       \{2M_i-1:i\in\mathcal J\},\,
       \{r+b-1:c_{\sigma,r,b}>0\}\bigr).
\]
All coefficients lie in \(\Q(\zeta_{L_*})\), represented by the power
basis modulo the cyclotomic polynomial. Catalyst reuse and recursion
introduce no further roots of unity. Thus the procedure specifies finite
coefficient arrays and their composition; expanding them is not needed
for the proof of correctness.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Rational coefficients and an explicit square seed}

The field issue does not require an existence theorem for algebraic
solutions of rank equations.

\begin{lemma}[Restriction of coefficients to the rationals]
\label{lem:descent}
Let \(T\) have rational coefficients, and suppose an explicit rank-\(R\)
decomposition of \(T\) is given over a number field \(\mathbb K\) of degree
\(d\). Then an explicit rational decomposition has length at most
\(d^2R\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose a rational basis \(e_1=1,e_2,\ldots,e_d\) of \(\mathbb K\), and let
\(\phi:\mathbb K\to\Q\) extract the coefficient of \(e_1\).
For each summand write its first two linear forms as
\(\sum_s e_s\alpha_s\) and \(\sum_t e_t\beta_t\), where the
\(\alpha_s,\beta_t\) have rational coefficients, and call its third
form \(\gamma\). Apply \(\phi\) coefficientwise to the tensor identity.
This summand becomes
\(\sum_{s,t}\alpha_s\beta_t\phi(e_se_t\gamma)\), a sum of at most
\(d^2\) rational rank-one tensors. The target is unchanged because its
coefficients are rational and \(\phi(1)=1\).
All field operations are effective; for the field above, use the power
basis modulo the cyclotomic polynomial.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Finite rational seeds]\label{cor:seed}
For every positive rational \(\eps\), a terminating procedure produces
an integer \(d_0\ge2\) and a rational rank decomposition of
\(\MM_{d_0}\) of length \(R_{\mathrm{seed}}<d_0^{9/4+\eps}\).
Consequently a fixed rational bilinear recursion attains an exponent
strictly below \(9/4+\eps\).
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:effective} with \(\eps/2\).
For each integer dimension \(n\), unroll its pooled recursion to
classical leaves, and set the unused initial input pairs to zero.
Lemma~\ref{lem:batch} gives a bilinear decomposition of \(\MM_n\).
Its number of leaf products is at most its arithmetic cost, hence is
\(O_\eps(n^{9/4+\eps/2})\). Its coefficient field is fixed independently
of \(n\). Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:descent}; this multiplies the length
by at most a fixed constant.

Enumerate \(n=2,3,\ldots\), generate the finite recursion and its rational
decomposition, and stop when its length \(R_n\) satisfies
\(R_n^{\beta_\eps}<n^{\alpha_\eps}\), where the positive integers
\(\alpha_\eps,\beta_\eps\) satisfy \(\alpha_\eps/\beta_\eps=9/4+\eps\).
The strict gap in exponents proves termination, regardless of the fixed
constants. This is a construction of identities by proved operations,
not a search for solutions to unspecified rank equations.
Ordinary block recursion on the resulting seed has exponent
\(\log_n R_n<9/4+\eps\); its fixed linear maps cost quadratically per node.
\end{proof}

Every rational seed identity also holds over every field of characteristic
zero. The preprocessing time of this procedure is finite for each fixed
\(\eps\). No quantitative bound on preprocessing time or coefficient bit size is
asserted.
