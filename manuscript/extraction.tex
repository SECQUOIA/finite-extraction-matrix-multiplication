\section{The finite construction}
\label{sec:extraction}

This section turns the integer counts of Section~\ref{sec:rounding} into a
relative scheme and computes its exponent. Proposition~\ref{prop:catalytic}
combines all splits and triplings into one catalytic degeneration, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:relative} makes it exact and reuses the catalyst.
Section~\ref{subsec:cost-bounds} bounds every term of the resulting
exponent~\eqref{eq:pooled-exponent} by quantities computed from the counts
and proves \(p\ge\tau_a>9/4\).

\subsection{Parameters and sector counts}
\label{subsec:parameters}

Fix the target index \(a\ge2\) and a rounding scale \(K\ge1\), and let
\(n_{r,x,y}\), \(w_{r,x}\) and \(z_r\) be the integer counts of
Section~\ref{sec:rounding}. Only relations with a positive count are used.
Let \(\mathcal I_c\) be the set of active splits, that is, of pairs
\(i=(r,x)\) with \(w_i=w_{r,x}>0\), and let \(\mathcal I_t\) be the set of
active triplings, each indexed by its row \(r\), so that \(z_r>0\). Put
\(\mathcal J=\mathcal I_c\sqcup\mathcal I_t\) and \(I=|\mathcal J|\); we call
an element of \(\mathcal J\) a relation. A split \(i=(r,x)\) has width
\(h_{r,x}\), auxiliary dimension \(d_i=d_{r,x}\), child counts
\(n_{i,y}=n_{r,x,y}\), and empirical distribution of children
\(\hat q_i=\hat q_{r,x}\), with \(\hat q_{i,y}=n_{i,y}/w_i\). Its child
\(y\) is column \(r-1+y\), which is the block of Lemma~\ref{lem:multiway}
with filtration index \((x+h_{r,x}-y)/2\).

Three further positive integers amortize finite overheads
(Table~\ref{tab:overheads}): the type scale \(N\) multiplies all counts,
the interpolation power \(k\) is the tensor power used in exactification,
and the reuse count \(j\) is the number of uses of one copy of the
catalyst. All results of Sections~\ref{sec:rate} and~\ref{sec:costs} take
\(N=1\); a larger \(N\) matters only when \(K\) is held fixed, since it
makes the type penalty and the copy overhead small compared with
\(\Delta_N\).

At type scale \(N\), a split \(i=(r,x)\) is applied to
\((C(r,r-1+x)B_X(d_i))^{\otimes Nw_i}\) with the type
\((Nn_{i,y})_y\), and a tripling \(r\) is applied to
\(C(r,4r-1)^{\otimes Nz_r}\) with middle count \(m_r\). The numbers of
sectors retained by Lemmas~\ref{lem:multiway} and~\ref{lem:tripling} are
the sector counts
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sector-counts}
 M_i=\frac{(Nw_i)!}{\prod_y(Nn_{i,y})!}\quad(i\in\mathcal I_c),
 \qquad
 M_r=\binom{Nz_r}{m_r}2^{Nz_r-m_r},\quad
 m_r=\left\lfloor\frac{Nz_r+1}{3}\right\rfloor
 \quad(r\in\mathcal I_t).
\end{equation}
Zero child counts contribute \(0!=1\). The middle count \(m_r\) is the
maximizing choice of Lemma~\ref{lem:tripling}, so
\(M_r\ge3^{Nz_r}/(Nz_r+1)\) by \eqref{eq:tripling-mode}.

Proposition~\ref{prop:catalytic} below applies the separator to
\(L_i=2M_i-1\) copies of each relation \(i\) in each of three orientations.
It turns \(L\) source copies into \(M^3\) output copies, with degree at
most \(D\), the sum of the degree bounds \((M_i-1)^2\) of all separators.
The one-step source side \(A_1\) collects the auxiliary dot products
\(B_X(d_i)\) of the splits; the one-step target side \(B_1\) collects
\(NK\) boundary factors \(\MM_2\) and the separator auxiliaries:
\begin{align}
 M&=\prod_{i\in\mathcal J}M_i,&
 L&=\prod_{i\in\mathcal J}L_i^3,\quad L_i=2M_i-1,
 \label{eq:copy-counts}\\
 D&=3\sum_{i\in\mathcal J}(M_i-1)^2,&
 A_1&=\prod_{i\in\mathcal I_c}d_i^{Nw_i},\quad
 B_1=2^{NK}M.
 \label{eq:dimensions}
\end{align}
Finally, \(\Cat_N=\Cat^{\otimes N}\), where \(\Cat\) is the catalyst of
\eqref{eq:rounded-catalyst}.

\subsection{The catalytic degeneration}
\label{subsec:catalytic}

\begin{proposition}[Catalytic degeneration]\label{prop:catalytic}
The maps described in the proof give a degeneration of degree at most
\(D\):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:catalytic-degeneration}
 L\,\Cat_N(S^+_{a,a})^{\otimes NK}\MM_{A_1}
 \degen M^3\,\Cat_N\MM_{B_1}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The idea is to apply Corollary~\ref{cor:oriented-relations} to every
relation, to tensor the results with one degeneration parameter, and to
read off the factors on the two sides from the ledger.

\emph{Step 1: one relation.} Let \(i=(r,x)\in\mathcal I_c\) be a split. Apply
Corollary~\ref{cor:oriented-relations}(a) with \(\sigma=\sigma_r\),
\(b=r-1+x\), \(h=h_{r,x}\), \(n=Nw_i\), and the type that has \(Nn_{i,y}\)
positions of filtration index \((x+h_{r,x}-y)/2\) for every child \(y\).
The hypotheses hold: the state \(x\) is interior, so \(h\ge1\), and
\(b\ge x+1\ge h+1\) because \(r\ge2\). The multinomial coefficient of the
corollary is \(M_i\), its auxiliary side is \((h+1)^n=d_i^{Nw_i}\), and the
block of filtration index \((x+h-y)/2\) has column \(b+h-(x+h-y)=r-1+y\).
Hence
\[
 L_i^3\,(S^{\sigma_r}_{r,r-1+x})^{\otimes Nw_i}\MM_{d_i^{Nw_i}}
 \degen M_i^3\Bigl(\prod_y(S^{\sigma_r}_{r,r-1+y})^{\otimes Nn_{i,y}}\Bigr)
 \MM_{M_i}.
\]
For a tripling \(r\in\mathcal I_t\) (so \(r\ge2\)), part~(b) with \(n=Nz_r\ge1\) and
\(m=m_r\) gives
\(L_r^3\,(S^{\sigma_r}_{r,4r-1})^{\otimes Nz_r}\degen
M_r^3\,(S^{\sigma_r}_{r,r})^{\otimes Nz_r}\MM_{M_r}\). Each of these
degenerations has degree at most \(3(M_i-1)^2\), and its maps before and
after the separators are fixed linear maps of the full variable spaces that
do not depend on \(t\); Appendix~\ref{sec:composed-maps} writes them out,
including the terms of positive degree.

\emph{Step 2: all relations with one parameter.} Tensor the degenerations
of Step~1 over all \(i\in\mathcal J\), with one common parameter \(t\) for
all separators. Tensor products distribute over direct sums, so the left
side has \(\prod_iL_i^3=L\) copies and the constant term has
\(\prod_iM_i^3=M^3\) copies. By Lemma~\ref{lem:composition}, the result
is a polynomial in \(t\) of degree at most \(\sum_i3(M_i-1)^2=D\), and its
constant term is the tensor product of the constant terms of Step~1.

\emph{Step 3: the convolution factors.} In one source copy, every split
\(i=(r,x)\) contributes \(Nw_i\) factors with symbol
\([\sigma_r;r,r-1+x]\), and every tripling contributes \(Nz_r\) factors
\([\sigma_r;r,4r-1]\); in one output copy, the splits contribute
\(Nn_{i,y}\) factors \([\sigma_r;r,r-1+y]\) and the triplings \(Nz_r\)
factors \([\sigma_r;r,r]\). These are \(N\) times the source and output
multiplicities of the ledger~\eqref{eq:rounded-ledger}. By
Proposition~\ref{prop:rounded-ledger}, the two multiplicities agree for
every symbol except that \([+;a,a]\) has \(K\) more source than output
copies and \([\MM_2]\) has \(K\) output copies and no source copy. Since
the catalyst multiplicities~\eqref{eq:rounded-catalyst} are the minima of
the two, the source factors form \(\Cat_N(S^+_{a,a})^{\otimes NK}\) and the
output factors form \(\Cat_N\MM_2^{\otimes NK}\cong\Cat_N\MM_{2^{NK}}\).
These identifications of symbols are the coordinate isomorphisms
of~\eqref{eq:cyclic-identities}, applied within each global leg (on the
source side before the type restrictions, on the output side after the
separators); they do not depend on \(t\).

\emph{Step 4: the auxiliary factors.} On the left, the auxiliary factors
give \(\bigotimes_{i\in\mathcal I_c}\MM_{d_i^{Nw_i}}\cong\MM_{A_1}\). On
the right, the separator auxiliaries give
\(\bigotimes_{i\in\mathcal J}\MM_{M_i}\cong\MM_M\), and together with
\(\MM_{2^{NK}}\) from Step~3 they give \(\MM_{2^{NK}M}=\MM_{B_1}\), by
\eqref{eq:matrix-products}. This proves \eqref{eq:catalytic-degeneration}.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[The ledger for \(a=2\)]\label{ex:ledger-a2}
For \(a=2\) there is one row, \(r=2\), with sign \(\sigma_2=+\) and
injection state \(\xin_2=1\). Its occupations are \(\lambda_{2,1}=2\),
\(\lambda_{2,2}=14/5\), \(\lambda_{2,4}=11/5\) and \(\mu_2=1\), and states
3 and 5 are never visited (Example~\ref{ex:row-two}). For \(K=15\) all
scaled values \(K\lambda_{2,x}q_{2,x,y}\) and \(K\mu_2\) are integers, so the
rounding leaves them unchanged. The split at state 1 (column 2, width 1,
\(d=2\)) has children \(0,2\) with counts \(9,21\); the split at state 2
(column 3, width 2, \(d=3\)) has children \(0,2,4\) with counts
\(6,14,22\); the split at state 4 (column 5, width 2, \(d=3\)) has children
\(2,4,6\) with counts \(7,11,15\); and \(z_2=15\). Thus
\(\bar w=30+42+33=105\) and \(\bar z=15\). Child \(y\) is column \(1+y\),
and the tripling consumes column 7 and produces column 2. The ledger
involves the symbols \([+;2,b]\) for \(b=1,2,3,5,7\), where
\([+;2,1]=[-;1,2]=[\MM_2]\):
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrr}
\toprule
symbol & source multiplicity & output multiplicity
 & \(c_{+,2,b}\)\\
\midrule
\([\MM_2]=[+;2,1]\) & \(0\) & \(9+6=15\) & \(0\)\\
\([+;2,2]\) & \(30\) & \(15\) & \(15\)\\
\([+;2,3]\) & \(42\) & \(21+14+7=42\) & \(42\)\\
\([+;2,5]\) & \(33\) & \(22+11=33\) & \(33\)\\
\([+;2,7]\) & \(15\) & \(15\) & \(15\)\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The sources exceed the outputs by \(K=15\) copies of \([+;2,2]=[+;a,a]\),
and the outputs exceed the sources by \(15\) copies of \([\MM_2]\). The
catalyst is
\[
 \Cat=(S^+_{2,2})^{\otimes15}(S^+_{2,3})^{\otimes42}
      (S^+_{2,5})^{\otimes33}(S^+_{2,7})^{\otimes15},
\]
with \(105=\bar w+\bar z-K\) factors. In
Proposition~\ref{prop:catalytic}, \(A_1=2^{30N}3^{75N}\), and \(M\) is the
product of the three multinomial sector counts of the splits and the
sector count of the tripling~\eqref{eq:sector-counts}.
\end{example}

\subsection{Exactification and repeated use of one catalyst}
\label{subsec:relative}

The degeneration \eqref{eq:catalytic-degeneration} is approximate, and it
needs the catalyst as an input. We make it exact by interpolation, use the
resulting restriction \(j\) times while passing one copy of the catalyst
from one use to the next, and supply that copy once from a rank
decomposition, so that the catalyst is paid for once and not \(j\) times.
Exactification comes first because restrictions compose
freely, whereas iterating the degeneration would require a bound on the
accumulated degree.

The catalyst is supplied by tensoring the evaluation decompositions
\eqref{eq:convolution-rank} of its convolution factors. Each factor
\(S^\sigma_{r,b}\) has rank at most \((r+b-1)^3\) by
\eqref{eq:cyclic-identities}, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kappa}
 \kappa_N=\prod_{\sigma,r,b}(r+b-1)^{3Nc_{\sigma,r,b}}
 \ge\R(\Cat_N),
\end{equation}
where the product runs over the symbols, as in \eqref{eq:rounded-catalyst}.
For positive integers \(k\) and \(j\), put \(E=kD+1\) (the number of
interpolation points), \(A=A_1^{kj}\), \(B=B_1^{kj}\), and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:relative-counts}
 r_*=\kappa_N^kE^jL^{kj}(2a-1)^{3NKkj},
 \qquad m_*=M^{3kj}.
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Relative scheme]\label{prop:relative}
For all positive integers \(k\) and \(j\), the maps described in the proof
give a restriction \(r_*\MM_A\restr m_*\MM_B\). When \(B>A\), it is a
relative scheme (Definition~\ref{def:relative-scheme}).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1: exactification.} The substitutions of
Proposition~\ref{prop:catalytic} transform the left side of
\eqref{eq:catalytic-degeneration} into a polynomial in \(t\) of degree at
most \(D\) whose constant term is the right side. Apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:exactification} with parameter \(k\). Since tensor products
distribute over direct sums and \(\MM_d\MM_e\cong\MM_{de}\) by
\eqref{eq:matrix-products}, the result reads
\[
 EL^k\,\Cat_N^{\otimes k}(S^+_{a,a})^{\otimes NKk}\MM_{A_1^k}
 \restr M^{3k}\,\Cat_N^{\otimes k}\MM_{B_1^k}.
\]

\emph{Step 2: decomposing the source factors.} A rank decomposition of a
tensor \(T\) with \(R\) terms is the same as a restriction
\(R\,\MM_1\restr T\), where \(\MM_1\) is the \(1\times1\times1\) tensor:
the \(s\)-th copy of \(\MM_1\) is sent to the \(s\)-th term. By
\eqref{eq:cyclic-identities}, \(\R(S^+_{a,a})\le(2a-1)^3\), and hence
\((2a-1)^{3NKk}\MM_1\restr(S^+_{a,a})^{\otimes NKk}\). Tensoring this with
\(EL^k\,\Cat_N^{\otimes k}\MM_{A_1^k}\) and composing with Step~1 gives the
one-step restriction
\begin{equation}\label{eq:one-step}
 \alpha\,\Cat_N^{\otimes k}\MM_{A_1^k}
 \restr\beta\,\Cat_N^{\otimes k}\MM_{B_1^k},
 \qquad \alpha=EL^k(2a-1)^{3NKk},\quad \beta=M^{3k}.
\end{equation}

\emph{Step 3: reuse.} We show by induction that, for every \(j'\ge1\),
\[
 \alpha^{j'}\Cat_N^{\otimes k}\MM_{A_1^{kj'}}
 \restr\beta^{j'}\Cat_N^{\otimes k}\MM_{B_1^{kj'}}.
\]
The case \(j'=1\) is \eqref{eq:one-step}. For the induction step, tensor
the restriction for \(j'\) with the identity on
\(\alpha\MM_{A_1^k}\):
\[
 \alpha^{j'+1}\Cat_N^{\otimes k}\MM_{A_1^{k(j'+1)}}
 \restr\beta^{j'}\bigl(\alpha\,\Cat_N^{\otimes k}\MM_{A_1^k}\bigr)
 \MM_{B_1^{kj'}}.
\]
Each of the \(\beta^{j'}\) direct summands on the right is the left side
of \eqref{eq:one-step} tensored with \(\MM_{B_1^{kj'}}\); in it,
\(\Cat_N^{\otimes k}\) occurs once as a tensor factor. Apply
\eqref{eq:one-step} inside each summand, with the identity on
\(\MM_{B_1^{kj'}}\). The summand becomes
\(\beta\,\Cat_N^{\otimes k}\MM_{B_1^k}\MM_{B_1^{kj'}}\cong
\beta\,\Cat_N^{\otimes k}\MM_{B_1^{k(j'+1)}}\), which proves the claim for
\(j'+1\). Since the one-step restriction acts summand by summand, every
summand keeps the single tensor factor \(\Cat_N^{\otimes k}\); the catalyst
is never raised to a higher tensor power.

\emph{Step 4: supplying and removing the catalyst.} By \eqref{eq:kappa},
\(\R(\Cat_N^{\otimes k})\le\kappa_N^k\), so
\(\kappa_N^k\MM_1\restr\Cat_N^{\otimes k}\), and hence
\(\kappa_N^k\alpha^j\MM_A\restr\alpha^j\Cat_N^{\otimes k}\MM_A\). On the
right side, choose one monomial in every convolution factor of
\(\Cat_N^{\otimes k}\). All coefficients of a convolution tensor are \(0\)
or \(1\), so the product monomial has coefficient one, and setting all
other variables to zero restricts \(\Cat_N^{\otimes k}\) to \(\MM_1\).
Hence \(\beta^j\Cat_N^{\otimes k}\MM_B\restr\beta^j\MM_B\). Composing
these two restrictions with Step~3 for \(j'=j\) gives
\(r_*\MM_A\restr m_*\MM_B\) with \(r_*=\kappa_N^k\alpha^j\) and
\(m_*=\beta^j\), which are the values in \eqref{eq:relative-counts}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The exponent of the relative scheme}
\label{subsec:exponent}

Lemma~\ref{lem:batch} turns a relative scheme into a recursive algorithm.
For the restriction of Proposition~\ref{prop:relative}, put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:delta-N}
 \Delta_N=\log\frac{B_1}{A_1}
 =NK\log2+\log M-N\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_c}w_i\log d_i,
\end{equation}
the logarithm of the ratio of the target side to the source side in one
use of the catalytic degeneration. Since \(B/A=(B_1/A_1)^{kj}\), the
restriction is a relative scheme exactly when \(\Delta_N>0\). In that
case, by \eqref{eq:relative-counts},
\[
 \log\frac{r_*}{m_*}
 =kj\left(3NK\log(2a-1)+\log\frac{L}{M^3}+\frac{\log E}{k}
          +\frac{\log\kappa_N}{j}\right),
 \qquad \log\frac BA=kj\,\Delta_N,
\]
so the pooled exponent \(p=\log(r_*/m_*)/\log(B/A)\) of the scheme is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pooled-exponent}
 p=\frac1{\Delta_N}\left(3NK\log(2a-1)+\log\frac{L}{M^3}
   +\frac{\log E}{k}+\frac{\log\kappa_N}{j}\right).
\end{equation}
The numerator consists of the main term \(3NK\log(2a-1)\), which pays for
the rank of the \(NK\) source copies of \(S^+_{a,a}\), and of the copy
overhead \(\log(L/M^3)\), the interpolation overhead \((\log E)/k\) and
the catalyst overhead \((\log\kappa_N)/j\). The source \(\MM_A\) is the
matrix product to which the recursion is applied again, so no decomposition
of it enters the formula; its side \(A=A_1^{kj}\) comes from the auxiliary
factor \(\MM_{A_1}\) of Proposition~\ref{prop:catalytic}.

The condition \(\Delta_N>0\) is not automatic. For \(a=2\) and \(N=1\),
every rounding allowed by Lemma~\ref{lem:circulation-rounding} gives
\(B_1<A_1\), that is, \(\Delta_1<0\), for every \(K\le7\), as an
exhaustive computation in exact integer arithmetic shows (script
\path|extraction_a2_small_scale.py|; see the supporting files in
Section~\ref{sec:discussion}). For the counts of
Example~\ref{ex:ledger-a2} (\(K=15\)), a floating-point evaluation gives
\(\Delta_1\approx7.28\). A proof of \(B>A\) therefore needs the lower
bound on \(\Delta_N\) in \eqref{eq:type-error} below;
Theorem~\ref{thm:quantitative} supplies such a proof for its parameters.

\subsection{Type losses and finite cost bounds}
\label{subsec:cost-bounds}

The terms of \eqref{eq:pooled-exponent} involve the sector counts \(M_i\),
which are large multinomial coefficients. This subsection bounds every
term by quantities computed directly from the integer counts, starting
with a comparison of multinomial coefficients and entropies.

\begin{lemma}[Counting the words of one type]\label{lem:types}
Let \(\mathcal Y\) be a finite nonempty set, let \(n_y\), for
\(y\in\mathcal Y\), be positive integers with sum \(n\), and put
\(\theta=(n_y/n)_{y\in\mathcal Y}\). Then
\[
 \frac{\exp(nH(\theta))}{(n+1)^{|\mathcal Y|-1}}
 \le\frac{n!}{\prod_{y\in\mathcal Y}n_y!}\le\exp(nH(\theta)).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Draw \(n\) independent samples from the distribution \(\theta\) on
\(\mathcal Y\). For nonnegative integers \(g_y\) with sum \(n\), the
probability that each \(y\) is drawn exactly \(g_y\) times is
\[
 \mathbb P(g)=n!\prod_{y}\frac{\theta_y^{g_y}}{g_y!}
     =\frac{n!}{n^n}\prod_y\frac{n_y^{g_y}}{g_y!}.
\]
Since \(\prod_y\theta_y^{n_y}=\exp(-nH(\theta))\), the probability of
\(g=(n_y)_y\) equals the multinomial coefficient times
\(\exp(-nH(\theta))\), and \(\mathbb P\le1\) gives the upper bound. The
factor \(n_y^{g_y}/g_y!\) changes by the ratio \(n_y/(g_y+1)\) when
\(g_y\) increases by one, so it is largest at \(g_y=n_y\), and
\(g=(n_y)_y\) maximizes \(\mathbb P\). At most \((n+1)^{|\mathcal Y|-1}\) vectors \(g\)
occur (all entries but one lie in \(\{0,\ldots,n\}\)), and their
probabilities add up to one; so the largest is at least
\((n+1)^{-(|\mathcal Y|-1)}\), which is the lower bound.
\end{proof}

For a split \(i\), let \(\mathcal Y_i^+=\{y:n_{i,y}>0\}\) be the set of
its children with positive count; its auxiliary dimension is \(d_i\) even
when some children have count zero, because Lemma~\ref{lem:multiway} uses
the whole factor \(B_X(d_i)\). The entropy charge of a relation is
\(H_i=w_iH(\hat q_i)\) for a split and \(H_r=z_r\log3\) for a tripling.
Define the type penalty \(\mathcal T_N\) and the largest scaled entropy
charge \(\bar H_N\) by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:type-penalty}
 \begin{split}
 \mathcal T_N&=\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_c}(|\mathcal Y_i^+|-1)\log(Nw_i+1)
             +\sum_{r\in\mathcal I_t}\log(Nz_r+1),\\
 \bar H_N&=N\max_{i\in\mathcal J}H_i.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
The type penalty bounds the distance of \(\Delta_N\) from its ideal value
\(NG\), where \(G\) is the rounded potential and \(\mathcal L\) the
rounding loss of \eqref{eq:rounded-gain}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:type-error}
 NG-\mathcal T_N\le\Delta_N\le NG,
 \qquad G=K\log F(a,a)-\mathcal L,
 \qquad 0\le\mathcal L\le4J_a.
\end{equation}
Here \(J_a\) is the number of transition coordinates, and the second and
third relations are \eqref{eq:rounded-entropy}.

To prove the first relation, recall from \eqref{eq:rounded-gain} that
\(G=K\log2+\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_c}w_i(H(\hat q_i)-\log d_i)+\bar z\log3\).
Since \(\bar z\log3=\sum_{r\in\mathcal I_t}H_r\), comparison with
\eqref{eq:delta-N} gives
\[
 \Delta_N-NG=\sum_{i\in\mathcal J}(\log M_i-NH_i).
\]
For a split, apply Lemma~\ref{lem:types} to the positive counts
\(Nn_{i,y}\), \(y\in\mathcal Y_i^+\). Their sum is \(Nw_i\) and their
distribution is \(\hat q_i\); zero counts change neither \(M_i\) nor
\(H(\hat q_i)\). This gives
\(-(|\mathcal Y_i^+|-1)\log(Nw_i+1)\le\log M_i-NH_i\le0\). For a tripling,
\(M_r\le3^{Nz_r}\), because the numbers \(\binom nm2^{n-m}\),
\(0\le m\le n\), add up to \(3^n\); together with
\(M_r\ge3^{Nz_r}/(Nz_r+1)\) this gives
\(-\log(Nz_r+1)\le\log M_r-NH_r\le0\). Summing over \(\mathcal J\) proves
\eqref{eq:type-error}. We call the nonnegative difference
\(NG-\Delta_N=\sum_{i\in\mathcal J}(NH_i-\log M_i)\) the \emph{type loss};
the type penalty bounds it.

In Example~\ref{ex:ledger-a2} with \(N=1\), every empirical distribution
equals the transition probabilities, so \(\mathcal L=0\) and
\(G=15\log(10/3)\approx18.06\); the type penalty is
\(\mathcal T_1=\log31+2\log43+2\log34+\log16\approx20.78\), and
\(\Delta_1\approx7.28\) lies between \(G-\mathcal T_1\approx-2.72\) and
\(G\).

The numerator terms of \eqref{eq:pooled-exponent} satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-cost-bounds}
 \begin{split}
 0&\le\log(L/M^3)<3I\log2,\\
 \log E&\le\log(3kI+1)+2\bar H_N,\\
 \log\kappa_N&\le3N(\bar w+\bar z-K)\log(5a).
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
For the first bound, \(L_i/M_i=2-1/M_i\) lies in \([1,2)\) for every
relation, and \(L/M^3=\prod_i(L_i/M_i)^3\). For the second,
Lemma~\ref{lem:types} and \(M_r\le3^{Nz_r}\) give
\(M_i\le\exp(NH_i)\le\exp(\bar H_N)\) for every relation, and therefore
\(E=1+3k\sum_i(M_i-1)^2\le1+3kI\exp(2\bar H_N)
\le(3kI+1)\exp(2\bar H_N)\). For the third, the catalyst has
\(\sum_{\sigma,r,b}c_{\sigma,r,b}=\bar w+\bar z-K\) factors by
\eqref{eq:catalyst-size}. Every symbol with positive catalyst multiplicity
is consumed by some relation (Proposition~\ref{prop:rounded-ledger}), so
it has a representative \([\sigma_r;r,b]\) with
\(2\le r\le a\) and \(r\le b\le4r-1\); hence the base of its rank bound
\((r+b-1)^3\) satisfies \(r+b-1\le5a-2<5a\). These bounds use only the
integer counts.

Without any overhead, and with \(\Delta_N\) equal to \(NK\log F(a,a)\),
the formula \eqref{eq:pooled-exponent} would give the profile exponent
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tau}
 \tau_a=\frac{3\log(2a-1)}{\log F(a,a)}
 \longrightarrow\frac94.
\end{equation}
The overheads can only increase the exponent: since
\(\Delta_N\le NG\le NK\log F(a,a)\) by \eqref{eq:type-error} and all three
overheads in the numerator of \eqref{eq:pooled-exponent} are nonnegative,
every scheme of this section with \(B>A\) satisfies \(p\ge\tau_a\).
The strict bound \(F(a,a)<(2a-1)^{4/3}\) of
Theorem~\ref{thm:least-profile} gives \(\tau_a>9/4\).
