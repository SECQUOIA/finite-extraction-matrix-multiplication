\section{The exact binary certificate and its denominator scale}
\label{sec:exact-certificate}

This appendix explains why the construction rounds its counts instead of
clearing denominators. A \emph{certificate} is a set of positive rational
weights on finitely many profile relations whose weighted sum telescopes;
scaled to integers, the weights count how often each relation is used in
a tensor construction. The certificate below uses only binary splits
(splits of width one) and triplings, and it weights the two children of
each split by their tangent probabilities. It reproves the diagonal bound
\(P(a,a)\ge F(a,a)\) of Theorem~\ref{thm:least-profile}
(Proposition~\ref{prop:dual}), but the least scale \(K_a\) that clears
its denominators grows at least exponentially in \(a\)
(Theorem~\ref{thm:normalization}), and the source of the corresponding
tensor relation at least doubly exponentially. The rounding of
Section~\ref{sec:rounding} avoids these exponential denominators: it
keeps every row identity exact at every scale \(K\ge1\), at the cost of a
rounding loss of at most \(4J_a=O(a^3)\)
(Proposition~\ref{prop:entropy-loss}). The main construction does not
use this appendix.

Quantities of the certificate that correspond to quantities of
Sections~\ref{sec:flows} and~\ref{sec:rounding} carry the superscript
\(\mathrm{ex}\); the tangent probabilities \(q^\pm_{r,x}\) and the counts
\(w^\pm_{r,x}\) take the place of \(q_{r,x,y}\) and \(n_{r,x,y}\), and the
tripling weights \(\mu_r\) are those of~\eqref{eq:reset-weights}.

\subsection{Binary splits with tangent probabilities}

Fix a target index \(a\ge2\). As in Section~\ref{sec:flows}, state \(x\)
of row \(r\) (\(2\le r\le a\), \(0\le x\le3r\)) corresponds to column
\(b=r-1+x\). By~\eqref{eq:affine-row}, \(F(r,r-1+x)=s_rf_r(x)\), where
\(f_r(x)=\frac{3(r-1)}2+x\) has slope one in \(x\). Hence
\(2F(r,b)=F(r,b-1)+F(r,b+1)\) for \(r\le b\le4r-2\): on these columns
the concavity inequality~\eqref{eq:concavity} holds for \(F\) with
equality.

A binary split at an interior state \(1\le x\le3r-1\) is the relation of
Lemma~\ref{lem:multiway} with width one: it consumes \(C(r,b)B_X(2)\),
where \(b=r-1+x\), and produces the children \(C(r,b\pm1)\) at the
states \(x\pm1\). Its \emph{tangent probabilities} are the shares of the
two children in the concavity equality for \(F\):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:probabilities}
 q^\pm_{r,x}=\frac{F(r,b\pm1)}{2F(r,b)}=\frac{f_r(x\pm1)}{2f_r(x)}
 =\frac{3r-3+2x\pm2}{2(3r-3+2x)}.
\end{equation}
They are positive rationals, since \(f_r(x-1)\ge f_r(0)=3(r-1)/2>0\),
and \(q^+_{r,x}+q^-_{r,x}=1\) because \(f_r(x+1)+f_r(x-1)=2f_r(x)\).
They are the probabilities that~\eqref{eq:multiway-probabilities} gives
when the width \(h_{r,x}\) is replaced by one, that is, with auxiliary
dimension two and children \(x\pm1\).

The certificate applies to an arbitrary profile \(P\). Put
\(\eta_{r,b}=\log(P(r,b)/F(r,b))\). These logarithmic ratios are
symmetric, \(\eta_{r,b}=\eta_{b,r}\), because \(P\) and \(F\) are, and
normalization~\eqref{eq:normalization} together with \(F(1,b)=b\) gives
\(\eta_{1,b}=0\); in particular \(\eta_{1,2}=0\). For \(1\le x\le3r-1\)
and \(b=r-1+x\), the concavity inequality takes the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:log-concavity}
 \eta_{r,b}-q^-_{r,x}\eta_{r,b-1}-q^+_{r,x}\eta_{r,b+1}\ge0.
\end{equation}
Indeed \(b\ge2\), so~\eqref{eq:concavity} applies, and dividing it by
\(2F(r,b)\) gives
\(e^{\eta_{r,b}}\ge q^-_{r,x}e^{\eta_{r,b-1}}+q^+_{r,x}e^{\eta_{r,b+1}}\);
the weighted arithmetic--geometric mean inequality bounds the right side
below by \(\exp(q^-_{r,x}\eta_{r,b-1}+q^+_{r,x}\eta_{r,b+1})\), and taking
logarithms proves~\eqref{eq:log-concavity}. The instance of
the tripling inequality~\eqref{eq:tripling} whose right side is
\(3P(r,r)\) reads \(P(r,4r-1)\ge3P(r,r)\). Since \(f_r(3r)=3f_r(1)\), we
have \(F(r,4r-1)=3F(r,r)\), and therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:log-tripling}
 \eta_{r,4r-1}-\eta_{r,r}\ge0.
\end{equation}

The certificate multiplies each inequality~\eqref{eq:log-concavity} by a
weight \(\lambda^{\mathrm{ex}}_{r,x}\) and each
inequality~\eqref{eq:log-tripling} by a weight \(\mu_r\). In the final
row \(r=a\), put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:final-weights}
 \lambda^{\mathrm{ex}}_{a,x}=\frac{4f_a(x)(3a-x)}{3(a-1)(3a-1)}
 \quad(1\le x\le3a-1),\qquad
 \mu_a=\frac1{a-1}.
\end{equation}
In each lower row \(2\le r\le a-1\), put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lower-weights}
 \lambda^{\mathrm{ex}}_{r,1}=\frac{2(3r-1)}{3(r-1)},\quad
 \lambda^{\mathrm{ex}}_{r,x}=\frac{8f_r(x)(3r-x)}{3(r-1)(3r+1)}
 \ (2\le x\le3r-1),\quad
 \mu_r=\frac{2(3r-1)}{(r-1)(3r+1)}.
\end{equation}
These weights are the occupations of the row walk of
Section~\ref{sec:flows} with every width replaced by one: the proof of
Proposition~\ref{prop:dual} shows that they satisfy the row
identity~\eqref{eq:row-flow} of this walk, which determines them because
the walk reaches state \(0\) from every state (as in the proof of
Proposition~\ref{prop:occupations}). We call the numbers
\(\lambda^{\mathrm{ex}}_{r,x}q^+_{r,x}\),
\(\lambda^{\mathrm{ex}}_{r,x}q^-_{r,x}\) and \(\mu_r/3\) the \emph{type
weights}; Section~\ref{subsec:types} explains the division by three.

\begin{proposition}[Rational telescoping]\label{prop:dual}
Multiply~\eqref{eq:log-concavity} at each state \(1\le x\le3r-1\) of each
row \(2\le r\le a\) by \(\lambda^{\mathrm{ex}}_{r,x}\), and
multiply~\eqref{eq:log-tripling} for each row by \(\mu_r\). As a linear
form in the variables \(\eta_{r,b}=\eta_{b,r}\), the sum of the resulting
left sides is exactly \(\eta_{a,a}-\eta_{1,2}\). In particular
\(\eta_{a,a}-\eta_{1,2}\ge0\) for every profile \(P\). All weights
\(\lambda^{\mathrm{ex}}_{r,x},\mu_r\) and all type weights are positive
rationals.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The idea is that within each row the weights cancel the coefficient of
every column except column \(r-1\) and the column of the injection state;
the terms left over by adjacent rows then cancel by symmetry.

Fix a row \(r\). Write \(f=f_r\) and \(\eta_x=\eta_{r,r-1+x}\) for
\(0\le x\le3r\); put \(g(x)=\lambda^{\mathrm{ex}}_{r,x}/f(x)\) for
\(1\le x\le3r-1\), and \(g(x)=0\) for all other integers \(x\). The
weighted sum for this row is
\[
 R_r=\sum_{x=1}^{3r-1}\lambda^{\mathrm{ex}}_{r,x}
 \bigl(\eta_x-q^-_{r,x}\eta_{x-1}-q^+_{r,x}\eta_{x+1}\bigr)
 +\mu_r(\eta_{3r}-\eta_1).
\]
By~\eqref{eq:probabilities},
\(\lambda^{\mathrm{ex}}_{r,x}q^\pm_{r,x}=g(x)f(x\pm1)/2\). The variable
\(\eta_x\) occurs in the split at \(x\), as its source, in the splits at
\(x-1\) and \(x+1\), as a child (for those of these states that are
interior), and, if \(x=1\) or \(x=3r\), in the tripling. Since \(g\)
vanishes outside \(1\le x\le3r-1\), the coefficient of \(\eta_x\) in
\(R_r\) is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:interior-coefficient}
 \beta_x=f(x)\Bigl(g(x)-\frac{g(x-1)+g(x+1)}2\Bigr)
 -\mu_r[x=1]+\mu_r[x=3r]
 \qquad(0\le x\le3r),
\end{equation}
where \([\cdot]\) is \(1\) if the condition holds and \(0\) otherwise.
In particular
\(\beta_x=0\) whenever \(x\ne1,3r\) and \(g\) is affine on
\(\{x-1,x,x+1\}\). We use the values \(f(0)=3(r-1)/2\),
\(f(1)=(3r-1)/2\), \(f(2)=(3r+1)/2\) and \(f(3r)=3(3r-1)/2\).

\emph{The final row \(r=a\).} Here \(g(x)=4(3a-x)/(3(a-1)(3a-1))\) for
\(1\le x\le3a\), including \(x=3a\), where both sides vanish; so \(g\) is
affine on \(\{1,\ldots,3a\}\), and \(\beta_x=0\) for
\(2\le x\le3a-1\). The affine formula takes the value
\(4a/((a-1)(3a-1))\) at \(x=0\), so \(g(1)-g(2)/2=2a/((a-1)(3a-1))\);
moreover \(g(1)=4/(3(a-1))\) and \(g(3a-1)f(3a)/2=1/(a-1)\). Therefore
\(\beta_0=-g(1)f(0)/2=-1\),
\(\beta_1=f(1)(g(1)-g(2)/2)-\mu_a=a/(a-1)-1/(a-1)=1\) and
\(\beta_{3a}=\mu_a-g(3a-1)f(3a)/2=0\), so that
\(R_a=\eta_1-\eta_0=\eta_{a,a}-\eta_{a,a-1}\).

\emph{A lower row \(r<a\).} Here \(g(1)=4/(3(r-1))\), while
\(g(x)=8(3r-x)/(3(r-1)(3r+1))\) for \(2\le x\le3r\) is affine, so
\(\beta_x=0\) for \(3\le x\le3r-1\). The affine formula takes the value
\(g(1)+4/(3r+1)\) at \(x=1\), so~\eqref{eq:interior-coefficient} gives
\(\beta_2=\frac{f(2)}2\cdot\frac4{3r+1}=1\). Next,
\(g(1)-g(2)/2=4/((r-1)(3r+1))\) and
\(g(3r-1)f(3r)/2=2(3r-1)/((r-1)(3r+1))=\mu_r\). Therefore
\(\beta_0=-g(1)f(0)/2=-1\),
\(\beta_1=2(3r-1)/((r-1)(3r+1))-\mu_r=0\) and
\(\beta_{3r}=\mu_r-\mu_r=0\), so that
\(R_r=\eta_2-\eta_0=\eta_{r,r+1}-\eta_{r,r-1}\).

In both cases \(R_r=\eta_{\xin_r}-\eta_0\). With \(\eta_x\) replaced by
the basis vector \(\mathbf e_x\), this is the row
identity~\eqref{eq:row-flow} for the walk of width one.

\emph{Summing the rows.} By symmetry, \(\eta_{r,r-1}=\eta_{r-1,r}\), so
the term \(-\eta_{r,r-1}\) left by row \(r\) cancels the term
\(+\eta_{r-1,r}\) left by row \(r-1\), and the sum of \(R_r\) over
\(2\le r\le a\) is \(\eta_{a,a}-\eta_{2,1}=\eta_{a,a}-\eta_{1,2}\). Only
coefficient identities and the symmetry \(\eta_{r,b}=\eta_{b,r}\) were
used, so this is an identity of linear forms; since each weighted
inequality is nonnegative, \(\eta_{a,a}-\eta_{1,2}\ge0\). Finally,
\(\lambda^{\mathrm{ex}}_{r,x}>0\) because \(f_r(x)>0\) and \(3r-x>0\), the weights \(\mu_r\) are positive,
and \(q^\pm_{r,x}>0\) by~\eqref{eq:probabilities}; hence all weights and
type weights are positive rationals.
\end{proof}

Since \(\eta_{1,2}=0\), Proposition~\ref{prop:dual} gives
\(P(a,a)\ge F(a,a)\) for every profile \(P\), the diagonal bound of
Theorem~\ref{thm:least-profile}. Since the weights depend only on \(F\),
they can be realized by tensor maps with fixed types once the type
weights are scaled to integers.

\subsection{Integer types and their entropy identity}
\label{subsec:types}

A tensor realization of the certificate uses every relation at its
tangent distribution. For a split at state \(x\) of row \(r\), this is
the type that sends the fractions \(q^+_{r,x}\) and \(q^-_{r,x}\) of the
factors to the children at states \(x+1\) and \(x-1\). For a tripling,
it is the uniform distribution on the three label classes (left, middle
and right) of Lemma~\ref{lem:tripling}: on a tensor power
\(C(r,4r-1)^{\otimes n}\) with \(3\mid n\), take the sectors of
Lemma~\ref{lem:tripling} with \(n/3\) middle labels and keep only those
whose outer labels are \(n/3\) left and \(n/3\) right labels; this is
again a coordinate restriction, with \(n!/((n/3)!)^3\) sectors. Because
the certificate prescribes this uniform type for every tripling, the
count of every tripling must be divisible by three; this is why
\(\mu_r/3\), rather than \(\mu_r\), is a type weight.

Let \(K_a\) be the least positive integer for which all the numbers
\(K_a\lambda^{\mathrm{ex}}_{r,x}q^\pm_{r,x}\) and \(K_a\mu_r/3\) are
integers, that is, the least common multiple of the denominators of all
type weights in lowest terms. The counts and their totals
\begin{equation}\label{eq:type-totals}
 w^{\mathrm{ex}}_{r,x}=K_a\lambda^{\mathrm{ex}}_{r,x},\quad
 w^\pm_{r,x}=K_a\lambda^{\mathrm{ex}}_{r,x}q^\pm_{r,x},\quad
 z^{\mathrm{ex}}_r=K_a\mu_r,\quad
 \bar w^{\mathrm{ex}}=\textstyle\sum_{r,x}w^{\mathrm{ex}}_{r,x},\quad
 \bar z^{\mathrm{ex}}=\sum_rz^{\mathrm{ex}}_r
\end{equation}
are integers, with \(w^{\mathrm{ex}}_{r,x}=w^+_{r,x}+w^-_{r,x}\) and
\(3\mid z^{\mathrm{ex}}_r\).
Since every weight is positive, the certificate uses all
\(\sum_{r=2}^a(3r-1)=(3a^2+a-4)/2\) splits and all \(a-1\) triplings,
\(3(a+2)(a-1)/2\) relations in total.

We record tensor factors by formal symbols in a free abelian group, as in
Section~\ref{subsec:ledger}, but with one symbol \([r,b]=[b,r]\) for each
unordered pair of positive integers, standing for the sixfold product
\(Q_{r,b}\) of~\eqref{eq:balanced}; the identification is the
isomorphism \(Q_{r,b}\cong Q_{b,r}\) of~\eqref{eq:balanced-properties},
and a coefficient \(n\) on \([r,b]\) stands for \(Q_{r,b}^{\otimes n}\).
Since Proposition~\ref{prop:dual} is an identity of linear forms in
variables subject only to \(\eta_{r,b}=\eta_{b,r}\), multiplying it by
\(K_a\) and replacing each \(\eta_{r,b}\) by \([r,b]\) gives the integer
identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:formal-ledger}
 \begin{split}
 &\sum_{r=2}^a\sum_{x=1}^{3r-1}
 \bigl(w^{\mathrm{ex}}_{r,x}[r,r-1+x]-w^+_{r,x}[r,r+x]
 -w^-_{r,x}[r,r-2+x]\bigr)\\
 &\qquad+\sum_{r=2}^az^{\mathrm{ex}}_r\bigl([r,4r-1]-[r,r]\bigr)
 =K_a[a,a]-K_a[1,2].
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
As in the signed ledger~\eqref{eq:rounded-ledger}, the split at state
\(x\) of row \(r\) consumes \(w^{\mathrm{ex}}_{r,x}\) factors of its
column and produces \(w^\pm_{r,x}\) factors of its children at \(x\pm1\),
and the tripling of row \(r\) consumes \(z^{\mathrm{ex}}_r\) factors of
column \(4r-1\) and produces as many of column \(r\).

For example, let \(a=2\): there is one row, with states \(0,\ldots,6\)
and columns \(1,\ldots,7\). The weights are
\(\lambda^{\mathrm{ex}}_{2,x}=10/3,\,56/15,\,18/5,\,44/15,\,26/15\) for
\(x=1,\ldots,5\) and \(\mu_2=1\), so \(K_2=15\); the counts
\((w^+_{2,x},w^-_{2,x})\) are \((35,15)\), \((36,20)\), \((33,21)\),
\((26,18)\) and \((15,11)\), \(z^{\mathrm{ex}}_2=15\),
\(\bar w^{\mathrm{ex}}=230\) and \(\bar z^{\mathrm{ex}}=15\).
In~\eqref{eq:formal-ledger}, column \(2\) is consumed \(50\) times (by
the split at state \(1\)) and produced \(20+15=35\) times (by the split
at state \(2\) and the tripling), each of the columns \(3,\ldots,7\) is
consumed and produced equally often, and column \(1\) is produced \(15\)
times; since \([2,1]=[1,2]\), the left side reduces to
\(15[2,2]-15[1,2]\).

As in~\eqref{eq:rounded-catalyst}, the factors common to the consumed
and the produced side form a catalyst: for \(r\le b\), let
\(c^{\mathrm{ex}}_{r,b}\) be the smaller of the total coefficients of
\([r,b]\) in the positive and in the negative terms on the left
of~\eqref{eq:formal-ledger}, and put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:catalyst}
 \Cat^{\mathrm{ex}}=\bigotimes_{r\le b}Q_{r,b}^{\otimes c^{\mathrm{ex}}_{r,b}},
 \qquad
 \Cat^{\mathrm{ex}}_N=(\Cat^{\mathrm{ex}})^{\otimes N}.
\end{equation}
Both kinds of terms have total coefficient
\(\bar w^{\mathrm{ex}}+\bar z^{\mathrm{ex}}\), because every relation
produces as many factors as it consumes, and by~\eqref{eq:formal-ledger}
they agree symbol by symbol except at \([a,a]\), which is consumed
\(K_a\) more times than it is produced, and at \([1,2]\), which is
produced \(K_a\) times and never consumed. Hence
\(\sum_{r\le b}c^{\mathrm{ex}}_{r,b}=\bar w^{\mathrm{ex}}
+\bar z^{\mathrm{ex}}-K_a\).

The certificate describes a tensor relation, built with the maps of
Section~\ref{sec:maps} at type scale \(N\) as in
Section~\ref{sec:extraction}, but with every relation used in all six
leg orientations \(\pi\in S_3\), so that no row signs are needed; each
split applies Lemma~\ref{lem:multiway} with width one and type
\((Nw^+_{r,x},Nw^-_{r,x})\), and each tripling the three-class
restriction above. By~\eqref{eq:coset-dot-products}, the six orientations
of the auxiliary \(B_X(2)^{\otimes Nw^{\mathrm{ex}}_{r,x}}\) of a split
combine into \(\MM_{4^{Nw^{\mathrm{ex}}_{r,x}}}\), so the source auxiliary
is \(\MM_{2^{2N\bar w^{\mathrm{ex}}}}\). By~\eqref{eq:formal-ledger}, the
relation consumes the convolution factors
\(\Cat^{\mathrm{ex}}_N\,Q_{a,a}^{\otimes NK_a}\) and produces
\(\Cat^{\mathrm{ex}}_N\,Q_{1,2}^{\otimes NK_a}\cong
\Cat^{\mathrm{ex}}_N\,\MM_{4^{NK_a}}\), retaining the catalyst as in
Section~\ref{sec:extraction}.

Because every relation is used at its tangent distribution, the
certificate loses nothing in the potential \(K_a\log F(a,a)\). In
multiplicative form, this is the identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:entropy-identity}
 2^{K_a-\bar w^{\mathrm{ex}}}\,3^{\bar z^{\mathrm{ex}}}
 \prod_{r,x}\frac{(w^{\mathrm{ex}}_{r,x})^{w^{\mathrm{ex}}_{r,x}}}
 {(w^+_{r,x})^{w^+_{r,x}}(w^-_{r,x})^{w^-_{r,x}}}
 =F(a,a)^{K_a},
\end{equation}
where the product runs over all splits. To prove it, fix a split at
state \(x\) of row \(r\) and put \(b=r-1+x\). By~\eqref{eq:probabilities}
and \(w^\pm_{r,x}=w^{\mathrm{ex}}_{r,x}q^\pm_{r,x}\), we have
\(F(r,b\pm1)=2F(r,b)\,w^\pm_{r,x}/w^{\mathrm{ex}}_{r,x}\), and hence
\[
 (2F(r,b))^{w^{\mathrm{ex}}_{r,x}}=
 \frac{(w^{\mathrm{ex}}_{r,x})^{w^{\mathrm{ex}}_{r,x}}}
 {(w^+_{r,x})^{w^+_{r,x}}(w^-_{r,x})^{w^-_{r,x}}}
 \,F(r,b+1)^{w^+_{r,x}}F(r,b-1)^{w^-_{r,x}}.
\]
Each tripling gives
\(F(r,4r-1)^{z^{\mathrm{ex}}_r}=3^{z^{\mathrm{ex}}_r}F(r,r)^{z^{\mathrm{ex}}_r}\).
Multiply these identities over all relations. The map
\([r,b]\mapsto\log F(r,b)\) is well defined because \(F\) is symmetric;
applied to~\eqref{eq:formal-ledger}, it shows that the profile values
cancel between the two sides except for \(F(a,a)^{K_a}\) on the consumed
side and \(F(1,2)^{K_a}=2^{K_a}\) on the produced side, which
gives~\eqref{eq:entropy-identity}. In logarithmic form,
\eqref{eq:entropy-identity} is the analogue, for the walk of width one,
of~\eqref{eq:rounded-entropy} with \(\mathcal L=0\): every empirical
distribution \((w^+_{r,x},w^-_{r,x})/w^{\mathrm{ex}}_{r,x}\) equals its
tangent distribution \((q^+_{r,x},q^-_{r,x})\).

\subsection{The minimal denominator scale}

\begin{theorem}[Minimal denominator scale]\label{thm:normalization}
The scale \(K_a\) of Section~\ref{subsec:types} has the closed form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:minimal-scale}
 K_a=\lcm\left(
 \frac{3(a-1)(3a-1)}{\gcd(3a-1,4)},\quad
 \left\{\frac{3(r-1)(3r+1)}{\gcd(r-1,2)^2}:2\le r\le a-1\right\}
 \right).
\end{equation}
In particular,
\begin{gather}
 3\lcm(1,\ldots,a-1)\mid 2K_a,
 \qquad K_a\mid3\lcm(1,\ldots,3a-1),\label{eq:scale-divisibility}\\
 K_a\ge\frac32\lcm(1,\ldots,a-1)\ge\frac{3\cdot2^{a-2}}a.
 \label{eq:scale-growth}
\end{gather}
\end{theorem}

For example, \(K_2=15\), \(K_3=84\), \(K_4=3465\), \(K_6=1113840\) and
\(K_{10}=5956750800\).

\begin{proof}
The idea is to compute, row by row, the least common multiple of the
reduced denominators of the type weights, by putting them over one
common denominator and finding the greatest common divisor of that
denominator and all numerators.

\emph{Step 1: from numerators to a row denominator.} If the type
weights of row \(r\) are the numbers \(\nu/\delta\) with \(\nu\) in a
finite set \(\mathcal N\) of positive integers, their reduced
denominators \(\delta/\gcd(\delta,\nu)\) have the least common multiple
\(\rho_r=\delta/\gcd(\delta,\mathcal N)\), where
\(\gcd(\delta,\mathcal N)\) is the greatest common divisor of \(\delta\)
and all elements of \(\mathcal N\). Indeed, for every prime
\(\mathfrak p\), with \(v\) the exponent of \(\mathfrak p\), both numbers
have exponent \(v(\delta)-\min(v(\delta),\min_{\nu\in\mathcal N}v(\nu))\).
Hence \(K_a=\lcm_{2\le r\le a}\rho_r\), and it remains to compute
\(\gcd(\delta,\mathcal N)\) in each row.

\emph{Step 2: the final row.} The type weights of row \(a\) have the
common denominator \(\delta=3(a-1)(3a-1)\). The numerator of
\(\mu_a/3=1/(3(a-1))\) is \(3a-1\). By~\eqref{eq:final-weights}
and~\eqref{eq:probabilities},
\(\lambda^{\mathrm{ex}}_{a,x}q^\pm_{a,x}=2f_a(x\pm1)(3a-x)/\delta
=(3a-3+2x\pm2)(3a-x)/\delta\) for \(1\le x\le3a-1\). Since the numerator \(3a-1\) divides \(\delta\), the number
\(\gcd(\delta,\mathcal N)\) equals the greatest common divisor of
\(3a-1\) and the other numerators, which may be reduced modulo \(3a-1\).
Modulo \(3a-1\) we have \(3a-3+2x\pm2\equiv2x-2\pm2\) and
\(3a-x\equiv-(x-1)\), so the products are congruent to \(-2x(x-1)\) for
the plus sign and to \(-2(x-1)(x-2)\) for the minus sign. Each is \(-2\)
times a product of two consecutive integers and is therefore divisible by
four, and the plus sign at \(x=2\) gives \(-4\). Hence
\(\gcd(\delta,\mathcal N)=\gcd(3a-1,4)\) and
\(\rho_a=3(a-1)(3a-1)/\gcd(3a-1,4)\), the first entry
of~\eqref{eq:minimal-scale}.

\emph{Step 3: a lower row.} Let \(2\le r\le a-1\) and use the common
denominator \(\delta=3(r-1)(3r+1)\). By~\eqref{eq:lower-weights}
and~\eqref{eq:probabilities}, the state \(x=1\) contributes
\(\lambda^{\mathrm{ex}}_{r,1}q^+_{r,1}=(3r+1)/(3(r-1))\) and
\(\lambda^{\mathrm{ex}}_{r,1}q^-_{r,1}=1\), with numerators \((3r+1)^2\)
and \(3(r-1)(3r+1)\). The tripling contributes \(\mu_r/3\), with
numerator \(2(3r-1)\), and the states \(2\le x\le3r-1\) contribute the
numerators \(2(3r-3+2x\pm2)(3r-x)\). An odd prime dividing both
\((3r+1)^2\) and \(2(3r-1)\) would divide \(3r+1\) and \(3r-1\), hence
their difference \(2\), which is impossible; so
\(\gcd(\delta,\mathcal N)\) is a power of two. If \(r\) is even,
\((3r+1)^2\) is odd and \(\gcd(\delta,\mathcal N)=1\). If \(r\) is odd,
then \(r-1\), \(3r+1\), \(3r-1\) and \(3r-3+2x\pm2\) are even, so
\(\delta\) and every numerator are divisible by four. Moreover
\((3r+1)^2\) has exactly two factors of two when \(r\equiv3\pmod4\), and
\(2(3r-1)\) has exactly two when \(r\equiv1\pmod4\); so
\(\gcd(\delta,\mathcal N)=4\). In both cases
\(\gcd(\delta,\mathcal N)=\gcd(r-1,2)^2\), and
\(\rho_r=3(r-1)(3r+1)/\gcd(r-1,2)^2\). These are the remaining entries
of~\eqref{eq:minimal-scale}.

\emph{Step 4: divisibility.} Since \(3a-1=3(a-1)+2\) and
\(3r+1=3(r-1)+4\), we have \(\gcd(a-1,3a-1)=\gcd(a-1,2)\) and
\(\gcd(r-1,3r+1)=\gcd(r-1,4)\). By Steps 2 and 3,
\[
 \rho_a=3\lcm(a-1,3a-1)\,\frac{\gcd(a-1,2)}{\gcd(3a-1,4)},\qquad
 \rho_r=3\lcm(r-1,3r+1)\,\frac{\gcd(r-1,4)}{\gcd(r-1,2)^2}
 \quad(r<a).
\]
Each ratio of greatest common divisors is \(1\) or \(1/2\): it is \(1\)
when \(a\) (respectively \(r\)) is even, and otherwise it is
\(2/\gcd(3a-1,4)\) (respectively \(\gcd(r-1,4)/4\)), where the greatest
common divisor is \(2\) or \(4\). The integers \(a-1\), \(3a-1\), \(r-1\)
and \(3r+1\) lie between one and \(3a-1\), so every \(\rho_r\) divides
\(3\lcm(1,\ldots,3a-1)\), and so does \(K_a\). In every row, \(2\rho_r\)
is a multiple of the corresponding \(3\lcm(\cdot,\cdot)\), hence of
\(3(r-1)\). As \(r\) runs from \(2\) to \(a\), the number \(r-1\) runs
through \(1,\ldots,a-1\), so \(3\lcm(1,\ldots,a-1)\) divides \(2K_a\).
This proves~\eqref{eq:scale-divisibility}.

\emph{Step 5: growth.} The first divisibility gives
\(2K_a\ge3\lcm(1,\ldots,a-1)\). Every binomial coefficient
\(\binom n\alpha\) divides \(\lcm(1,\ldots,n)\): by Legendre's formula,
the exponent of a prime \(\mathfrak p\) in \(\binom n\alpha\) is the sum
over \(e\ge1\) of
\(\lfloor n/\mathfrak p^e\rfloor-\lfloor\alpha/\mathfrak p^e\rfloor
-\lfloor(n-\alpha)/\mathfrak p^e\rfloor\in\{0,1\}\), whose terms vanish
for \(\mathfrak p^e>n\); so it is at most the exponent of \(\mathfrak p\)
in \(\lcm(1,\ldots,n)\), the largest \(e\) with \(\mathfrak p^e\le n\).
The \(n+1\) binomial
coefficients \(\binom n\alpha\) sum to \(2^n\), so the largest of them is
at least \(2^n/(n+1)\). With \(n=a-1\) this gives
\(\lcm(1,\ldots,a-1)\ge2^{a-1}/a\), and~\eqref{eq:scale-growth} follows.
\end{proof}

The scale \(K_a\) also bounds the size of the tensor relation of
Section~\ref{subsec:types} from below. By~\eqref{eq:formal-ledger}, the
symbol \([a,a]\) has net coefficient \(K_a\), and it is consumed only by
the split at state \(1\) of row \(a\), because a consumed symbol
\([r,r-1+x]\) or \([r,4r-1]\) equals \([a,a]\) only when \(r=a\) and
\(x=1\). Hence \(\bar w^{\mathrm{ex}}\ge w^{\mathrm{ex}}_{a,1}\ge K_a\),
and the source auxiliary \(\MM_{2^{2N\bar w^{\mathrm{ex}}}}\) has side at
least \(2^{2NK_a}\), which by~\eqref{eq:scale-growth} grows at least
doubly exponentially in \(a\).

\subsection{Comparison with the main construction}
\label{subsec:exact-normalization}

The splits of larger width reduce the auxiliary cost. If the rounded
counts \(w_{r,x}\) are replaced by the scaled occupations
\(K\lambda_{r,x}\), then \(\log A_1\) becomes
\(NK\sum_{r,x}\lambda_{r,x}\log d_{r,x}\), and
Proposition~\ref{prop:occupations} bounds this sum by
\(\Gamma_a=105+180(a-2)\). With splits of width one, every auxiliary
dimension is \(2\), and the weights~\eqref{eq:final-weights}
and~\eqref{eq:lower-weights} add up to \(5a+O(1)\) in the final row and
to \(10r+O(1)\) in each lower row \(r\), so the corresponding sum
\(\log2\sum_{r,x}\lambda^{\mathrm{ex}}_{r,x}\) is \((5\log2+o(1))a^2\).
The auxiliary cost of order \(a\) and the polynomial rounding scale give
the bound \(\log A=O(a^5(\log a)^2)\) of Theorem~\ref{thm:quantitative}.

The \emph{sixfold construction} uses every relation in all six leg
orientations, that is, \(Q_{r,b}=S^+_{r,b}S^-_{r,b}\) in place of
\(S^\sigma_{r,b}\), as the certificate above does; since
\(Q_{r,b}\cong Q_{b,r}\), it needs no row signs. For the same integer
counts it has the same ledger and catalyst multiplicities as the signed
ledger of Section~\ref{subsec:ledger}: a symbol of row \(r\) involves a
pair \(\{r,b\}\) with \(r-1\le b\le4r-1\), so two rows \(r<r'\) share a
symbol only if \(r'=r+1\), and then only \([r,r+1]=[r+1,r]\), which the
signed ledger identifies as well,
\([\sigma_r;r,r+1]=[\sigma_{r+1};r+1,r]\). Every factor is squared:
\(A_1\), because the six orientations of \(B_X(d_i)^{\otimes Nw_i}\) give
\(\MM_{d_i^{2Nw_i}}\) by~\eqref{eq:coset-dot-products}, and likewise
\(B_1\), \(L\), \(M^3\) and \(\kappa_N\); the source factor
\(Q_{a,a}^{\otimes NK}\) has rank at most \((2a-1)^{6NK}\); and the degree
becomes \(2D\), since each relation has six separators. Hence the sixfold
construction with interpolation power \(k\) has \(E=2kD+1\) and, for every
reuse count \(j\), gives through Proposition~\ref{prop:relative} the same
\(r_*\), \(m_*\), \(A\) and \(B\) as the threefold construction of
Section~\ref{sec:extraction} with power \(2k\). With the other parameters
fixed, \(k\) enters~\eqref{eq:pooled-exponent}
and~\eqref{eq:finite-exponent} only through \((\log(kD+1))/k\) and
\((\log2)/(kj)\), which do not increase with \(k\) because
\(\log(1+u)/u\) decreases for \(u>0\). So the threefold construction
gives smaller dimensions at a given interpolation power, but with power
\(k\) its pooled exponent is never smaller than that of the sixfold
construction with power
\(\lceil k/2\rceil\).

Finally, the source side \(A\) is a property of the specified relative
scheme, not a lower bound for schemes derived from it with the same
exponent: tensoring \(r_*\MM_A\restr m_*\MM_B\) with \(\MM_2\) gives
\(r_*\MM_{2A}\restr m_*\MM_{2B}\) by~\eqref{eq:matrix-products}, with the
same exponent and source side \(2A\), and restricting the factor \(\MM_2\)
to a single coefficient recovers a scheme with source side \(A\).
