\section{Finite examples}
\label{sec:costs}

Theorem~\ref{thm:quantitative} applies only to target indices
\(a\ge2^{64}\), and its bound is weak for moderate \(a\)
(Remark~\ref{rem:calibration}). For a small fixed index we can instead
compute the rounded counts and bound the exponent directly. This section
does so for \(a=4\), \(8\) and \(12\); no theorem of the paper depends on
it. The examples are computer-assisted. The bounds \(\overline\Phi\) and
digit counts in Table~\ref{tab:finite-multiway}, and the other bounds
quoted from the report, are certified by exact rational interval
arithmetic (Section~\ref{subsec:log-enclosures}) and recorded in the
report \path|results/multiway-finite-costs.json|. The column \(\Phi\)
and the other derived values (\(\tau_a\), the terms of
\(\overline\Phi\), differences and percentages) are floating-point
evaluations. The supplementary script \path|finite_table_checks.py|
reproduces every number of this section from the report.

\subsection{An upper expression for the exponent}
\label{subsec:sector-bounds}

Let \(\Delta_N>0\), so that Proposition~\ref{prop:relative} gives a
relative scheme with pooled exponent \(p\) as in
\eqref{eq:pooled-exponent}. The examples bound the slightly larger
\emph{fixed-batch bound}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-exponent}
 \Phi=\frac1{\Delta_N}\left(3NK\log(2a-1)+\log\frac{L}{M^3}
   +\frac{\log E}{k}+\frac{\log\kappa_N}{j}+\frac{\log2}{kj}\right)
 =p+\frac{\log2}{kj\Delta_N},
\end{equation}
which also bounds the exponent of the recursion without pooling. Indeed,
if the \(r_*\) products created by one batch of \(m_*\) products of
side \(n\) are batched only among themselves, they have side
\(A\lceil n/B\rceil\) and fill \(\beta_0=\lceil r_*/m_*\rceil\) batches, the
last one completed by zero products, so the cost of one batch
satisfies
\(\operatorname{cost}(n)\le \beta_0\operatorname{cost}(A\lceil n/B\rceil)+O(n^2)\),
where \(O(n^2)\) accounts for the linear maps of the scheme. The estimate
in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:batch}, with \(\beta_0\) in place of
\(r_*/m_*\), gives the exponent \((\log \beta_0)/\log(B/A)\). Since
\(r_*/m_*\ge(B/A)^2>1\) by Step~1 of that proof, \(\beta_0\le2r_*/m_*\), and
this exponent is at most \(\Phi\).

The sector counts \eqref{eq:sector-counts} enter \(\Phi\) through
\(\Delta_N\), the number \(E\) of interpolation points and the copy
overhead \(\log(L/M^3)\). Rather than computing the large integers
\(M_i\), we bound \(\log M_i\). Lemma~\ref{lem:types} gives such bounds
with an error of at most \(\log(Nw_i+1)\) for each used child beyond the
first; the following factorial bounds are usually sharper, and we use the
intersection of both.

\begin{lemma}[Elementary factorial bounds]\label{lem:factorial}
Put \(c_0=(1+\log2)/2\). For every integer \(\nu\ge1\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:elementary-factorial}
 (\nu+\tfrac12)\log\nu-\nu+c_0
 \le\log(\nu!)\le(\nu+\tfrac12)\log\nu-\nu+1.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since \(\log\) is concave, its chord over \([m,m+1]\) lies below its
graph, so
\(\frac12(\log m+\log(m+1))\le\int_m^{m+1}\log\xi\,d\xi\). Summing over
\(1\le m<\nu\) gives the trapezoid inequality
\[
 \log(\nu!)-\tfrac12\log\nu
 \le\int_1^{\nu}\log\xi\,d\xi=\nu\log\nu-\nu+1,
\]
which is the upper bound. By Jensen's inequality,
\(\int_{m-1/2}^{m+1/2}\log\xi\,d\xi\le\log m\); summing over
\(1\le m\le\nu\) gives
\[
 \log(\nu!)\ge\int_{1/2}^{\nu+1/2}\log\xi\,d\xi
 =(\nu+\tfrac12)\log(\nu+\tfrac12)-\nu+\tfrac12\log2 .
\]
Finally, \((\nu+\frac12)\log(\nu+\frac12)-(\nu+\frac12)\log\nu
=(\nu+\frac12)\log(1+\frac1{2\nu})\ge\frac12\) by
\(\log(1+\delta)\ge\delta/(1+\delta)\), and \(\frac12+\frac12\log2=c_0\).
\end{proof}

Now let \(\nu_y\), \(y\in\mathcal Y\), be positive integers with sum
\(\nu\). Applying the lower bound of \eqref{eq:elementary-factorial} to
\(\nu!\) and the upper bound to each \(\nu_y!\), and then the other way
round, we obtain, since the linear terms cancel,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-multinomial}
 c_0-|\mathcal Y|
 \le\log\frac{\nu!}{\prod_{y}\nu_y!}
   -\nu H\bigl((\nu_y/\nu)_{y}\bigr)
   -\frac12\log\frac{\nu}{\prod_{y}\nu_y}
 \le1-|\mathcal Y|c_0 ,
\end{equation}
where \(\nu H((\nu_y/\nu)_y)=\nu\log\nu-\sum_y\nu_y\log\nu_y\).

We apply \eqref{eq:finite-multinomial} to each relation separately. For a
split \(i\in\mathcal I_c\), take \(\mathcal Y=\mathcal Y_i^+=\{y:n_{i,y}>0\}\)
and \(\nu_y=Nn_{i,y}\); children with \(n_{i,y}=0\) contribute \(0!=1\).
Then \(\nu=Nw_i\) and \(\nu H((\nu_y/\nu)_y)=NH_i\), where
\(H_i=w_iH(\hat q_i)\) is the entropy charge. We intersect the resulting
interval for \(\log M_i\) with the interval
\([NH_i-(|\mathcal Y_i^+|-1)\log(Nw_i+1),\,NH_i]\) of
Lemma~\ref{lem:types} and with \([0,\infty)\); if
\(|\mathcal Y_i^+|=1\), then \(M_i=1\) and we use \(\log M_i=0\). For
the tripling of row \(r\in\mathcal I_t\), with
\(M_r=\binom{Nz_r}{m_r}2^{Nz_r-m_r}\), we bound the logarithm of the
binomial coefficient in the same way, using the two counts \(m_r\) and
\(Nz_r-m_r\) (if \(m_r=0\), the coefficient is \(1\)), add
\((Nz_r-m_r)\log2\), and intersect with
\([Nz_r\log3-\log(Nz_r+1),\,Nz_r\log3]\). The lower end of this interval
is \eqref{eq:tripling-mode}; the upper end holds because the numbers
\(M_{\mathrm t}(Nz_r,m)\), \(0\le m\le Nz_r\), add up to \(3^{Nz_r}\).

For each relation \(i\in\mathcal J\), write
\([\underline\eta_i,\overline\eta_i]\) for the interval obtained in this way,
so that \(\underline\eta_i\le\log M_i\le\overline\eta_i\). Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lower-contraction}
 \underline\Delta_N=NK\log2-N\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_c}w_i\log d_i
 +\sum_{i\in\mathcal J}\underline\eta_i
\end{equation}
and define the \emph{sufficient upper expression}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sufficient-expression}
 \overline\Phi=\frac{3NK\log(2a-1)+3I\log2
 +\frac1k\bigl(\log(3kI+1)+2\max_{i\in\mathcal J}\overline\eta_i\bigr)
 +\frac1j\log\kappa_N+\frac{\log2}{kj}}{\underline\Delta_N},
\end{equation}
where \(\log\kappa_N=3N\sum_{\sigma,r,b}c_{\sigma,r,b}\log(r+b-1)\) is
computed from the catalyst multiplicities, as in \eqref{eq:kappa}.

\begin{lemma}[A sufficient upper expression]
\label{lem:sufficient-expression}
We have \(\underline\Delta_N\le\Delta_N\). If \(\underline\Delta_N>0\),
then \(B>A\) and \(p<\Phi\le\overline\Phi\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:delta-N} and \(\log M_i\ge\underline\eta_i\),
\(\underline\Delta_N\le\Delta_N\). If \(\underline\Delta_N>0\), then
\(B/A=(B_1/A_1)^{kj}>1\), and \(p<\Phi\) by \eqref{eq:finite-exponent}.
We compare the numerators of \(\Phi\) and \(\overline\Phi\) term by term.
The copy overhead is below \(3I\log2\) by \eqref{eq:finite-cost-bounds}.
Since every \(M_i\ge1\), we have
\(E=1+3k\sum_{i\in\mathcal J}(M_i-1)^2\le(3kI+1)\max_iM_i^2\), so
\(\log E\le\log(3kI+1)+2\max_i\overline\eta_i\). The other terms agree.
Hence the numerator of \(\overline\Phi\) is at least that of \(\Phi\).
Both numerators are positive, so replacing \(\Delta_N\) by the smaller
positive number \(\underline\Delta_N\) can only increase the quotient.
\end{proof}

In all three examples \(\underline\Delta_N>0\), so by
Lemma~\ref{lem:sufficient-expression} each entry \(\overline\Phi\) bounds
\(\Phi\) and hence \(p\), while \(p\ge\tau_a\)
(Section~\ref{sec:extraction}). For
example, the relative scheme with \(a=12\) has
\(2.479<p<2.675820359\). The column \(\Phi\) is computed from the same
counts, with \(\log M_i\) from the log-gamma function and with \(E\) and
\(L/M^3\) from the actual sector counts. The relaxations in
\(\overline\Phi\) raise the bound by about \(1.5\%\) at \(a=4\) and
\(0.1\%\) at \(a=12\), and pooling lowers \(\Phi\) by
\((\log2)/(kj\Delta_N)<10^{-7}\).

The parameters of Theorem~\ref{thm:quantitative}, \(K=a^3\ell_a\),
\(k=1\) and \(j=a\ell_a\), suffice for large \(a\). For \(a\le12\),
however, the overheads per unit of \(K\) are large compared with
\(\log F(a,a)<3.8\), and the table amortizes them further: \(k=32\)
divides the interpolation overhead by \(32\), the factor \(128\) in \(j\)
divides the catalyst overhead by \(128\), and the extra factor \(\ell_a\)
in \(K\) reduces the shares of the copy overhead, the rounding loss and
the type penalty, which grow more slowly than \(K\). With the same \(K\)
and \(N\), the report gives the bounds \(\overline\Phi\le16.826\),
\(10.175\) and \(8.097\) for \(a=4,8,12\) when \((k,j)=(1,a\ell_a)\), and
\(12.501\), \(7.629\) and \(6.009\) when \((k,j)=(32,a\ell_a)\). With
\(k\) and \(j\) as in Theorem~\ref{thm:quantitative} (but the larger \(K\)
of the table), the catalyst overhead is therefore the largest cost at
these indices. Each increase enlarges the source side
\(A=A_1^{kj}\), whose logarithm is linear in \(k\) and \(j\) and roughly
linear in \(K\).

The main term would equal the profile exponent \(\tau_a\) if
\(\underline\Delta_N\) were \(NK\log F(a,a)\). By
Proposition~\ref{prop:entropy-loss}, the rounded potential satisfies
\(NG=NK\log2-N\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_c}w_i\log d_i+N\sum_{i\in\mathcal J}H_i
=NK\log F(a,a)-N\mathcal L\), and therefore
\[
 \underline\Delta_N=NK\log F(a,a)-N\mathcal L
 -\sum_{i\in\mathcal J}(NH_i-\underline\eta_i).
\]
The rounding loss \(N\mathcal L\) is below one in all three examples. The
last sum bounds the type loss \(\sum_i(NH_i-\log M_i)\) of
Section~\ref{subsec:cost-bounds} and is at most the type penalty
\(\mathcal T_N\); it removes about \(22\%\), \(7.6\%\) and \(3.9\%\) of
\(NK\log F(a,a)\) for \(a=4,8,12\). So the type loss is the largest
finite cost, and interpolation is the largest overhead in the numerator.
At \(a=12\), all finite costs together raise the bound by about
\(0.197\) above \(\tau_{12}\), which is less than the remaining gap
\(\tau_{12}-9/4\approx0.229\).

\subsection{Rational enclosures of logarithms}
\label{subsec:log-enclosures}

The report builds \(\overline\Phi\), the \(\underline\eta_i\) and
\(\overline\eta_i\), and \(\log A\) from logarithms of positive integers by
rational operations, maxima and minima. Write such an integer as
\(\nu=2^e\theta\) with an integer \(e\ge0\) and \(1\le\theta<2\), and put
\(\varrho=(\theta-1)/(\theta+1)\). For \(1\le\theta\le2\) we have
\(0\le\varrho\le1/3\), and the series
\(\log\theta=2\operatorname{artanh}\varrho=2(\varrho+\varrho^3/3+\cdots)\)
has nonnegative terms, those after \(2\varrho^{79}/79\) adding up to at
most \(2\varrho^{81}/(81(1-\varrho^2))\). Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:log-enclosure}
 2\Bigl(\varrho+\frac{\varrho^3}3+\cdots+\frac{\varrho^{79}}{79}\Bigr)
 \le\log\theta\le
 2\Bigl(\varrho+\frac{\varrho^3}3+\cdots+\frac{\varrho^{79}}{79}\Bigr)
 +\frac{2\varrho^{81}}{81(1-\varrho^2)},
\end{equation}
and the two sides differ by less than \(10^{-40}\). The report evaluates
the lower and upper bounds at multiples \(\theta^-\le\theta\le\theta^+\)
of \(2^{-80}\) in \([1,2]\) with \(\theta^+-\theta^-\le2^{-80}\), which
encloses \(\log\theta\) because \(\log\) is increasing; it encloses
\(\log2\) in the same way and puts \(\log\nu=e\log2+\log\theta\).
Logarithms of rationals are differences of such enclosures. All further
operations are interval arithmetic in exact rationals, and all roundings
(of each logarithm to a multiple of \(2^{-160}\), and of printed values)
are outward. Finally, \(A\) has
\(\lfloor\log_{10}A\rfloor+1\) decimal digits, and the report encloses
\(\log_{10}A=(\log A)/(\log10)\) in an interval whose endpoints have the
same integer part.
