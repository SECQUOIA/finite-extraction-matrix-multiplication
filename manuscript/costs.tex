\section{Finite size and limitations of the prescribed construction}
\label{sec:costs}

The preceding proof is effective, but its parameters grow rapidly.
This section gives an exact denominator formula for every profile index
and a finite example. Appendix~\ref{subsec:obstruction} proves a lower
bound for one prescribed small-index schedule. These size bounds concern
the specified construction, not general matrix multiplication algorithms.

\subsection{The minimal denominator scale}

\begin{theorem}[Exact normalization]\label{thm:normalization}
The least integer scale in Section~\ref{subsec:types} is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:minimal-scale}
 K_a=\lcm\left(
 \frac{3(a-1)(3a-1)}{\gcd(3a-1,4)},\quad
 \left\{\frac{3t(3t+4)}{\gcd(t,2)^2}:1\le t\le a-2\right\}
 \right).
\end{equation}
In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scale-divisibility}
 3\lcm(1,\ldots,a-1)\mid 2K_a,
 \qquad K_a\mid3\lcm(1,\ldots,3a-1),
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scale-growth}
 K_a\ge\frac32\lcm(1,\ldots,a-1)\ge\frac{3\,2^{a-2}}a.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
In the final row put \(t=a-1\), \(D_r=3t+2\), and \(b=a+h\).
All split weights have common denominator \(3tD_r\).
Their integer numerators are \(D_r\), from \(\mu/3\), and
\((D_r+2h\pm2)(D_r-h)\) for \(0\le h\le D_r-1\).
Modulo \(D_r\), the latter products are \(-2h(h\pm1)\), all divisible
by four. The case \(h=1\) with the plus sign is congruent to \(-4\).
Thus the joint gcd of the denominator and all numerators is exactly
\(\gcd(D_r,4)\). This gives the first entry in \eqref{eq:minimal-scale}.

In a lower row put \(t=r-1\) and \(V=3t+4\).
Use common denominator \(3tV\). At \(b=r\), the two split numerators
are \(V^2\) and \(3tV\); the tripling split numerator is \(2(3t+2)\).
For \(b=r+h>r\), the split numerators are
\(2(3t+2+2h\pm2)(3t+2-h)\).
An odd prime dividing both \(V^2\) and \(2(3t+2)\) would divide the
difference between \(V\) and \(3t+2\), namely two, which is impossible.
If \(t\) is odd, \(V^2\) is odd, so the joint gcd is one.
If \(t\) is even, all numerators and the denominator are divisible
by four. When \(t\equiv2\pmod4\), \(V^2\) has exactly two factors
of two; when \(t\equiv0\pmod4\), \(2(3t+2)\) has exactly two.
Thus the joint gcd is exactly four. This proves the remaining entries.

Each row denominator is either \(3\lcm(t,3t+c)\) or half that number,
with \(c=2\) for the final row and \(c=4\) for lower rows.
The integers involved lie between one and \(3a-1\); twice each row
denominator is divisible by \(3t\). Taking least common multiples
proves \eqref{eq:scale-divisibility}.

For completeness, every binomial coefficient \(\binom nh\) divides
\(\lcm(1,\ldots,n)\). In its factorial valuation at a prime \(p\),
each power \(p^j\le n\) contributes zero or one, so its valuation
is at most \(\lfloor\log_p n\rfloor\).
The largest binomial coefficient in row \(n\) is at least
\(2^n/(n+1)\). Apply this with \(n=a-1\) and use
\eqref{eq:scale-divisibility} to obtain \eqref{eq:scale-growth}.
\end{proof}

The scale is minimal for these exact rational split weights. Approximate
types, different weights, or different tensor maps need not obey this
bound. Since \(U\ge K_a\), already the unpowered source dimension
\(u_0=2^{2NU}\) grows at least doubly exponentially in \(a\) under
this bound. The inequality \(U\ge K_a\) also follows directly from
\eqref{eq:formal-ledger}: the net consumption of \([a,a]\) is \(K_a\),
and all its source occurrences belong to concavity relations.

For \(a=2\), the concavity types, indexed by \(b=2,\ldots,6\), are
\[
 \begin{array}{c|rrrrr}
 b&2&3&4&5&6\\ \hline
 w_b&50&56&54&44&26\\
 u_b&35&36&33&26&15\\
 d_b&15&20&21&18&11
 \end{array}
\]
The tripling count is \(z=15\). In this subsection write
\(Q_b=Q_{2,b}\). The catalyst is
\[
 \Cat_N=Q_2^{35N}Q_3^{56N}Q_4^{54N}Q_5^{44N}Q_6^{26N}Q_7^{15N},
\]
where powers denote tensor powers. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:small-kappa}
 \kappa_N=3^{210N}4^{336N}5^{324N}6^{264N}7^{156N}8^{90N},
 \qquad u_0=2^{460N},\quad v_0=2^{30N}M^2.
\end{equation}
The entropy identity becomes
\[
 \frac{3^{15}}{2^{215}}\prod_{b=2}^6
 \frac{w_b^{w_b}}{u_b^{u_b}d_b^{d_b}}=(10/3)^{15}.
\]

The archived calculation for \((N,k,j)=(300,125,3750)\) bounds
\eqref{eq:finite-exponent}, using \(\log(S/H)<36\log2\), in the
interval \((2.78918,2.78919)\).
Its target dimension \(B=v_0^{kj}\) has \(21\,665\,218\,118\)
decimal digits; the recursive input dimension \(A=u_0^{kj}\) has
\(19\,472\,877\,845\) digits. These are finite construction parameters,
not measured running times or storage requirements of an implemented
algorithm. The example illustrates exact finite cost accounting; it is
not competitive with existing theoretical bounds or practical methods.

The saved certificates use rational logarithm enclosures. For clarity,
the entire enclosure method is short. Write a positive integer as \(2^e y\),
\(1\le y\le2\), bracket \(y\) by adjacent rationals with denominator
\(2^{80}\), and set \(z=(y-1)/(y+1)\). Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:log-enclosure}
 2\sum_{h=0}^{39}\frac{z^{2h+1}}{2h+1}
 \le\log y\le
 2\sum_{h=0}^{39}\frac{z^{2h+1}}{2h+1}
 +\frac{2z^{81}}{81(1-z^2)}.
\end{equation}
The bound follows by summing the positive tail of the power series and
using \(2h+1\ge81\) in that tail. Apply the same bound to \(\log2\),
then combine rational intervals. For a positive rational, enclose the
logarithms of its numerator and denominator separately and subtract the
intervals. Decimal digit counts follow by enclosing
\(\log_{10}A\) or \(\log_{10}B\) strictly between consecutive integers.
These saved computations corroborate the displayed example; no numerical
experiment is required by Theorem~\ref{thm:effective}.
