\section{Consequences, scope, and further questions}
\label{sec:discussion}

The construction gives an effective answer to a particular extraction
problem. The convolution relations can be combined with probabilities and
integer multiplicities that are known in advance. Their composition
produces an exact relative multiplication identity, and a concrete recursion
converts that identity to ordinary matrix multiplication. Neither a
detecting tensor character nor an unknown optimal rank decomposition is
needed. Theorem~\ref{thm:effective} and Corollary~\ref{cor:seed} make
this statement precise for each fixed positive exponent allowance.

The least-profile theorem has a different use. It identifies the exact
limit of arguments using only normalization, symmetry, concavity, and
shifted tripling. A stronger diagonal exponent would require additional
constraints, rather than more instances of the same scalar inequalities.
The diagonal bound already follows from tripling at the row index, which
is the subfamily used in the tensor construction. Only the off-diagonal
minimality proof needs all the shifted tripling inequalities.
The rational certificate is useful beyond its numerical bound because it
specifies precisely which tensor factors must be supplied, produced, and
retained. This makes hidden cancellation assumptions testable at the level
of finite maps.

The separator refinements also have an independent scope. They apply to
any family of matched sectors sharing one leg. The smaller copy count and
the two-power projection improve particular finite reductions, without
requiring any convolution structure. Their use in another tensor argument
would still require separate accounting for the auxiliary dot products and
for conversion of simultaneous outputs to an algorithm.

There are three limitations to the exponent conclusion. First, every
finite profile index in this construction has \(\tau_a>9/4\); the proof
does not supply one fixed recursion with bound \(O(n^{9/4})\).
Second, the algorithm and coefficient set are fixed after the allowance
is chosen. A uniform version with a changing allowance requires additional
conventions for generating and loading its rational constants, as well as
an analysis of setup time. We do not give a quantitative convergence rate
for such a version.
Third, its exact arithmetic identities do not establish numerical stability
or useful finite-size performance. The denominator growth and the
small-index cost obstruction explain the large parameter sizes of the
prescribed construction. They do not give a stability analysis.

Several changes could alter this conclusion about cost. One could replace
the exact tangent probabilities by types whose error is controlled with
smaller denominators; use a different family of convolution relations;
find a cheaper way to supply the common tensor factors; or exploit
structure among the simultaneous recursive products. None of the lower
bounds in Section~\ref{sec:costs} or Appendix~\ref{subsec:obstruction}
excludes these alternatives. Establishing
such a change would require a new finite identity or a new cost analysis,
not an interpretation of an asymptotic tensor inequality as a finite
cancellation rule.

\subsection*{Supporting calculations and source files}

The proof is contained in the formulas and arguments in this paper.
The accompanying supplement, \path|finite-extraction-supplement-v1.zip|
(version 1, 7 October 2026), includes exact-arithmetic scripts for the rational
weights and resource counts, component-map checks, and saved rational
interval certificates. The general type data are generated by
\path|experiments/general_profile_extraction.py|; the small-index
certificates are recorded in
\path|results/small-profile-extraction.json| and their method in
\path|experiments/small_profile_extraction.py|.
The separator calculations are in
\path|experiments/finite_constructions.py|, and the scalar-profile
calculations in \path|experiments/profile_bounds.py|.
These files are supplementary checks, not assumptions of the main theorem.
The complete composed coefficient arrays are specified by
Theorem~\ref{thm:effective}; they are not stored as expanded arrays.

The numerical example and interval bounds in Section~\ref{sec:costs}
and Appendix~\ref{subsec:obstruction}
are taken from the archived calculations. They were not obtained from
timing a realization of the large construction. No numerical benchmark
is used to infer an arithmetic exponent.
