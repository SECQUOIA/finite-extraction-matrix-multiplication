\section{Integer types with exact resource balances}
\label{sec:rounding}

The occupations in Proposition~\ref{prop:occupations} are rational, but
their denominators need not be small. We do not clear those denominators.
Instead, we round the transition counts while preserving every vertex
balance. The resulting entropy loss has a bound that is uniform even
when some occupations are arbitrarily small or zero.

The following integrality fact is classical: it follows from the total
unimodularity of a directed incidence matrix and the integer-bound theorem
of \citet[Theorem~2]{HK56}. The return-edge reduction and rounding by
fractional-cycle cancellation are described by
\citet[Sections~2.2--3.1]{KangPayor15}. We give a residual-flow construction
to specify the rounded counts used in the tensor maps. The estimate
needed for our construction is the uniform weighted entropy loss for the
chosen occupation flow, proved below.

\begin{lemma}[Rounding a circulation]\label{lem:circulation-rounding}
Let \(\phi_e\) be a nonnegative rational circulation on a finite directed
multigraph. There is an effective construction of an integer circulation
\(n_e\) satisfying
\[
 \lfloor\phi_e\rfloor\le n_e\le\lceil\phi_e\rceil
 \quad\text{for every edge }e.
\]
Thus \(|n_e-\phi_e|<1\), and every edge with an integer target is
unchanged. Self-loops and parallel or opposing edges are allowed.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put \(\ell_e=\lfloor\phi_e\rfloor\), and at each vertex set
\[
 b_v=\sum_{e\text{ entering }v}\ell_e
       -\sum_{e\text{ leaving }v}\ell_e.
\]
The fractional parts \(f_e=\phi_e-\ell_e\) have outgoing minus incoming
balance \(b_v\). Discard loops for this step, since they have zero
incidence. Give each nonintegral non-loop edge capacity one, in its
original direction. Add an edge from a new source to each vertex with
\(b_v>0\), of capacity \(b_v\), and an edge from each vertex with
\(b_v<0\) to a new sink, of capacity \(-b_v\). The fractional parts, together with the added
edges at full capacity, give a feasible flow of value
\(B=\sum_{b_v>0}b_v\).

An augmenting-path algorithm starting from zero finds an integral flow
of this value. Indeed all residual capacities are integers, so each
augmentation increases the value by a positive integer. If it stopped
at a value below \(B\), the vertices reachable from the source would
define a cut of capacity below \(B\), contradicting the feasible
fractional flow. Add the resulting zero-or-one value on each original
edge to its floor. Keep integer-target edges and loops at their floors.
The source and sink edges are saturated, so the increments have balance
\(b_v\), and the new original counts have balance zero. Distinct edges
and their residual reverse arcs are kept separately; therefore parallel
and opposing edges cause no identification. This proves the claim and
gives an algorithm using exact rational and integer arithmetic.
\end{proof}

Fix \(a\ge2\) and any integer \(K\ge1\). Use the row notation of
Section~\ref{sec:flows}. In row \(r\), assign target count
\(K\lambda_{r,x}q_{r,x,y}\) to the transition \(x\to y\), and target
count \(K\mu_r\) to the reset \(n_r\to1\). Equation~\eqref{eq:row-flow}
says that their outgoing minus incoming balance is
\(K(e_{i_r}-e_0)\). Add a return edge \(0\to i_r\) of value \(K\).
The augmented counts are a circulation, and this return edge remains
exactly \(K\) under Lemma~\ref{lem:circulation-rounding}.
Remove it after rounding. Write the remaining integer counts as
\(n_{r,x,y}\) and \(z_r\), and put
\[
 w_{r,x}=\sum_y n_{r,x,y},\qquad
 U=\sum_{r,x}w_{r,x},\qquad T=\sum_r z_r.
\]
They obey the exact row identities
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rounded-row-flow}
 \sum_{x=1}^{n_r-1}
 \left(w_{r,x}e_x-\sum_y n_{r,x,y}e_y\right)
 +z_r(e_{n_r}-e_1)=K(e_{i_r}-e_0),
\end{equation}
and the strict error bounds
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rounded-edge-errors}
 |n_{r,x,y}-K\lambda_{r,x}q_{r,x,y}|<1,
 \qquad |z_r-K\mu_r|<1,
 \qquad |w_{r,x}-K\lambda_{r,x}|<d_{r,x}.
\end{equation}
An edge with zero target stays zero. In particular, an action with
\(\lambda_{r,x}=0\) has \(w_{r,x}=0\). Zero-weight actions are omitted
when constructing tensor maps. No divisibility condition is imposed on
\(z_r\).

\subsection{The signed resource ledger}

Put \(b_r(x)=r-1+x\) and \(\sigma_r=(-1)^{a-r}\). We record convolution
factors using formal symbols \([\sigma;r,b]\), with the identifications
\[
 [\sigma;r,b]=[-\sigma;b,r],\qquad
 [+;r,r]=[-;r,r],\qquad
 [\sigma;1,b]=[\MM_b].
\]
These are the legwise isomorphisms for the threefold tensors
\(S^\sigma_{r,b}\). The symbols belong to a free abelian group after
these identifications; they record multiplicities of tensor factors.

\begin{proposition}[Exact rounded ledger]\label{prop:rounded-ledger}
The rounded counts satisfy
\begin{align}
 &\sum_{r=2}^a\sum_{x=1}^{n_r-1}
 \left(w_{r,x}[\sigma_r;r,b_r(x)]
       -\sum_y n_{r,x,y}[\sigma_r;r,b_r(y)]\right)\notag\\
 &\qquad +\sum_{r=2}^a z_r
   \left([\sigma_r;r,4r-1]-[\sigma_r;r,r]\right)
 =K[+;a,a]-K[\MM_2].\label{eq:rounded-ledger}
\end{align}
Let \(i_{\sigma,r,b}\) and \(o_{\sigma,r,b}\) be the total source and
output multiplicities after choosing representatives for the identified
symbols, and set
\(c_{\sigma,r,b}=\min(i_{\sigma,r,b},o_{\sigma,r,b})\). Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rounded-catalyst}
 \Cat=\bigotimes_{\sigma,r,b}
       (S^\sigma_{r,b})^{\otimes c_{\sigma,r,b}},
 \qquad \Cat_N=\Cat^{\otimes N},
 \qquad \sum_{\sigma,r,b}c_{\sigma,r,b}=U+T-K.
\end{equation}
Boundary factors in this product are interpreted as \(\MM_b\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Replace the vectors in \eqref{eq:rounded-row-flow} by the corresponding
symbols. The final row, whose injection is at \(i_a=1\), leaves
\(K([+;a,a]-[+;a,a-1])\). A lower row has injection at \(i_r=2\)
and leaves
\(K([\sigma_r;r,r+1]-[\sigma_r;r,r-1])\).
Since \(\sigma_{r+1}=-\sigma_r\), its positive boundary equals the
negative boundary of row \(r+1\). The sum therefore leaves
\(K[+;a,a]-K[\sigma_2;2,1]\), which is
\eqref{eq:rounded-ledger}. Before removing common multiplicities, both
the source and output totals are \(U+T\). Their difference has just
the two distinct residual symbols in \eqref{eq:rounded-ledger}, each
of multiplicity \(K\). The sum of the coordinatewise minima is thus
\(U+T-K\).
\end{proof}

This is bookkeeping for actual source and output products. The common
factors in \eqref{eq:rounded-catalyst} remain on both sides of the
eventual tensor relation. They are not canceled from a restriction.
The auxiliary factors of the determinant relations are charged separately.

\subsection{Entropy loss without a minimum occupation bound}

For a probability vector \(p\), let \(H(p)=-\sum_y p_y\log p_y\),
where \(0\log0=0\). For positive \(q\), write
\(D(p\Vert q)=\sum_{p_y>0}p_y\log(p_y/q_y)\).
This quantity is nonnegative: applying \(\log t\le t-1\) to
\(q_y/p_y\) and summing gives \(-D(p\Vert q)\le0\).
For an active action put \(p_{r,x,y}=n_{r,x,y}/w_{r,x}\). Inactive
actions contribute zero to all the sums below.

For fixed-total probability approximation,
\citet[Section~IV.D, Eq.~(42)]{BoechererGeiger16} bound the relative
entropy of the minimizing \(M\)-type approximation by
\(1/(Mq_{\min})\). Here an \(M\)-type has probabilities that are
integer multiples of \(1/M\). Thus \(q_{\min}\ge1/(3d)\) gives weighted
loss at most \(3d\) for that optimum. The empirical probabilities
produced by our flow rounding need not minimize relative entropy, and
the action total \(w\) may differ from its ideal total \(K\lambda\).
We prove the required estimate directly, including actions whose rounded
counts are all zero.

\begin{proposition}[Uniform entropy loss]\label{prop:entropy-loss}
Define
\begin{align}
 G&=K\log2+\sum_{r,x:w_{r,x}>0}
 w_{r,x}\bigl(H(p_{r,x})-\log d_{r,x}\bigr)+T\log3,\notag\\
 \mathcal L&=\sum_{r,x:w_{r,x}>0}
       w_{r,x}D(p_{r,x}\Vert q_{r,x}).\notag
\end{align}
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rounded-entropy}
 G=K\log F(a,a)-\mathcal L,
 \qquad 0\le\mathcal L\le4J_a,
\end{equation}
where the number of candidate transition coordinates is
\begin{align}
 J_a&=\sum_{r=2}^a\sum_{x=1}^{3r-1}d_{r,x}
     =\sum_{r=2}^a\left(\left\lfloor\frac{9r^2}{4}\right\rfloor+3r-1\right)
     \notag\\
 &=\frac{12a^3+42a^2+12a-65+(-1)^a}{16}=O(a^3).
 \label{eq:coordinate-count}
\end{align}
The bound holds for every positive integer \(K\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
First we give a uniform lower bound for the harmonic probabilities.
In one row write \(n=3r\), \(c=3(r-1)/2\), \(f(x)=c+x\),
\(h=\min(x,n-x)\), and \(d=h+1\). The smallest child has
\(y=x-h\). For \(x\le n/2\), the ratio \(f(x-h)/f(x)=c/(c+x)\)
decreases with \(x\). For \(x\ge n/2\), the ratio
\((c+2x-n)/(c+x)\) increases with \(x\). Comparing both with the
continuous midpoint, whether or not it is an integer, gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:child-probability-bound}
 q_{r,x,y}\ge\frac{c}{d(c+n/2)}
 =\frac{r-1}{(2r-1)d}\ge\frac1{3d}.
\end{equation}

For an active action abbreviate \(V=K\lambda_{r,x}\),
\(w=w_{r,x}\), and \(p=p_{r,x}\). By
\eqref{eq:rounded-edge-errors}, \(|w-V|<d\), and hence, for each
positive \(n_y\),
\[
 \frac{p_y}{q_y}
 <\frac{V}{w}+\frac1{wq_y}
 \le1+\frac{4d}{w}.
\]
It follows that
\[
 wD(p\Vert q)\le w\log(1+4d/w)\le4d.
\]
This argument divides only by the positive integer \(w\), never by
the occupation \(\lambda_{r,x}\). If \(w=0\), the action's loss is
zero. If \(\lambda_{r,x}=0\), its counts are all zero by construction.
Summing over the candidate actions proves \(\mathcal L\le4J_a\).
The expression for \(J_a\) follows by summing
\(\sum_{x=1}^{3r-1}(1+\min(x,3r-x))
 =\lfloor9r^2/4\rfloor+3r-1\).

For the identity in \eqref{eq:rounded-entropy}, use
\(q_{r,x,y}=F(r,b_r(y))/(d_{r,x}F(r,b_r(x)))\). Each active action
satisfies
\begin{align*}
 &w_{r,x}\log F(r,b_r(x))
       -\sum_y n_{r,x,y}\log F(r,b_r(y))\\
 &\qquad=w_{r,x}\bigl(H(p_{r,x})-\log d_{r,x}\bigr)
       +w_{r,x}D(p_{r,x}\Vert q_{r,x}).
\end{align*}
The same equality reads \(0=0\) for an inactive action. Each reset
contributes \(z_r\log3\), because
\(F(r,4r-1)=3F(r,r)\). Apply the symmetric potential \(\log F\)
to \eqref{eq:rounded-ledger}. Its total is
\(K\log F(a,a)-K\log2\), proving the identity. The reset counts
therefore cause no additional entropy error.
\end{proof}

The same rounding supplies the bounds needed to charge auxiliary
dimensions and interpolation. Put
\(W=\sum_{r,x}w_{r,x}\log d_{r,x}\) and
\(C_a=105+180(a-2)\). Proposition~\ref{prop:occupations} bounds
both \(\sum_{r,x}\lambda_{r,x}\log d_{r,x}\) and
\(\sum_{r,x}\lambda_{r,x}\) by \(C_a\). These two bounds and
\eqref{eq:rounded-edge-errors} give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rounded-count-bounds}
 \begin{split}
 W&\le KC_a+J_a\log(2a),\qquad U\le KC_a+J_a,\\
 T&\le(2K+1)(a-1),\\
 w_{r,x}\log d_{r,x}&\le180K+2a\log(2a).
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
Indeed \(d_{r,x}\le\lfloor3a/2\rfloor+1\le2a\), so summing
\(w_{r,x}<K\lambda_{r,x}+d_{r,x}\) proves the first two bounds.
The exact reset occupations obey \(\mu_r\le2\), whence
\(z_r<2K+1\). Finally, the nonnegative ideal charge of an individual
action is at most its row's charge, which is at most \(180\).
Multiplying its rounding bound by \(\log d_{r,x}\) proves the last
inequality. This individual bound will control the largest sector count;
the total bound for \(W\) alone would be weaker by a factor of order
\(a\).
