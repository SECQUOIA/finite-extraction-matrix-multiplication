\begin{abstract}
The convolution-profile argument for the matrix multiplication exponent
\(9/4\) passes from finite tensor relations to an existence statement for
fast algorithms. We give a direct finite extraction from those relations.
An explicit positive rational combination of neighboring-length and
three-sector relations telescopes to a diagonal profile bound.
Clearing denominators gives integer tensor types.
We compose their exact restrictions with a Fourier separator, exact
interpolation, repeated use of a fixed catalyst, and a batched recursion.
All direct-sum multiplicities and finite overheads are retained.
For a profile index \(a\), the resulting exponents approach
\(3\log(2a-1)/\log F(a,a)\), where
\(F(a,a)=\frac{3a-1}{2}\prod_{h=1}^{a-1}(1+1/(3h))\).
These bounds tend to \(9/4\). An integer-arithmetic selection procedure
therefore produces, for each positive rational \(\eps\), a finite
algorithm with arithmetic complexity \(O_\eps(n^{9/4+\eps})\).
It also specifies a rational bilinear seed with exponent below
\(9/4+\eps\). We prove that \(F\) is the least profile satisfying the
underlying scalar axioms and determine the exact denominator scale of the
rational certificate. That scale grows at least exponentially in \(a\).
The construction resolves the finite extraction problem for these
relations, but supplies neither an exact exponent-\(9/4\) recursion nor
a practical algorithm.
\end{abstract}
