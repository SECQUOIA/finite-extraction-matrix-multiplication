\begin{abstract}
We give a finite construction of matrix multiplication algorithms from
convolution relations whose limiting exponent is \(9/4\).
A determinant filtration gives exact restrictions with several output
lengths. Rational occupation flows prescribe their frequencies with total
logarithmic auxiliary cost linear in the profile index before integer
scaling. Integral circulation rounding
preserves every tensor balance and loses at most a cubic additive term in
the logarithmic profile potential. Threefold balancing, a Fourier sector
separator, exact interpolation, and repeated use of one catalyst then give
an exact relative multiplication scheme. We retain all direct-sum
multiplicities and finite costs. An explicit parameter family has recursive
exponent less than \(9/4+900/\log a\) for \(a\ge2^{64}\), with
\(\log A\le181a^5\lceil\log_2a\rceil^2\) for its recursive source
side \(A\). Thus a prescribed integer parameter choice gives, for every
positive rational \(\varepsilon\), a finite arithmetic algorithm of
exponent below \(9/4+\varepsilon\). Coefficient descent also gives a
terminating construction of a rational bilinear seed with that exponent
allowance. We identify the least scalar profile and compare the rounded
construction with an exact binary certificate whose denominators grow
at least exponentially. The results concern finite mathematical
construction; they give neither a fixed exponent-\(9/4\) seed nor a
practical algorithm.
\end{abstract}
